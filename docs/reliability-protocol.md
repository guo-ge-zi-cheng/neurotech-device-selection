# How we find and judge reliable information

Working protocol for the additional six-device research, 20 October 2026. Apply it to each claim before adding it to a report. Reliability means that a source supports a particular statement within a stated scope; it is not a property conferred by a familiar organisation's name.

## Start with an answerable question

Choose the exact device/version, original development period and market. Break each report topic into its actual questions: for example, the investor, round amount and date are separate from a device budget or valuation. Search the missing answer through the routes in the [source guide](research-source-guide.md).

Start with records made by the actor responsible for the event: regulator decisions, patent texts and assignment/licensing records, investor or grant-funder records, filings, original study reports and payer policies. Use news or search snippets to locate those records. A company announcement may establish what the company announced; its commercial claims need different evidence.

## Five checks before accepting a finding

1. **Traceability:** identify the author/issuer, title, publication or event date, stable URL and a page/section containing the evidence. Keep the actual date checked and access route.
2. **Claim support:** read the relevant passage, table or claim. Search-result snippets and inaccessible documents remain leads, not accepted findings. Record whether review covered full text, selected sections or only an abstract.
3. **Scope match:** check product/version, indication, population/denominator, development stage, geography, setting and year. Company funding, disease burden, a predecessor patent and a comparator price answer different questions from device expenditure, eligible patients, exact patent coverage and actual purchase price.
4. **Method and interest:** distinguish an administrative record, experimental result, observational study, economic model, forecast and recollection. Note important design limits and commercial interests. A primary source can still be biased or insufficient for the claim being made.
5. **Cross-check where needed:** seek a second independent record for consequential or ambiguous claims, especially product-to-patent attribution, original financing and payment/approval applicability. Syndicated copies of one release are one account. If corroboration or scope remains incomplete, narrow the claim and keep the gap visible.

## Use explicit evidence labels

- **Documented fact:** a specific record establishes the event, term or reported figure within its scope. State when this is a manufacturer's or funder's own account.
- **Study result:** an original study reports an outcome; include design, population and key limits. An abstract supports only the details actually available there.
- **Context:** relevant disease, care-pathway, comparator or healthcare-system evidence. Explain how it helps without presenting it as a result for this device.
- **Inference:** our conclusion from stated premises, with uncertainty. Do not present it as a discovered historical fact.
- **Estimate:** an explicit model with sourced inputs, assumptions and range. Required development capital is separate from money actually raised.
- **Unresolved:** no adequate supporting record was reviewed. Record the search route and what would resolve the question. Failure to locate a record is not proof of absence.

## Apply stricter scope checks to the difficult topics

For IP, read the relevant independent claims and establish the assignee/licence and technical connection; a shared inventor or chronology is only a candidate link. Do not claim freedom to operate or current enforceability from an initial search.

For regulation, distinguish registration, clearance, approval and market availability; an old decision does not settle every current indication or market. For payment, distinguish coding, coverage, payment amount and procurement. A general procedure policy or an economic model does not establish coverage of the selected device.

For first-hand access, a named role must connect to a specific question. Treat a possible interview as an access lead until it happens. No interviews or outreach are authorised by this research task.

## Keep the report layers separate

Preserve the existing research and dates as the original layer. Append a dated additional-research layer, organised by the same five topics. For each finding show the source route, evidence label, supported finding, why it helps the section and the remaining limit. Explicitly flag any correction to the earlier layer rather than silently rewriting history.

The additional layer must distinguish accepted evidence from unsuccessful searches. No requirement to fill every topic with a confident answer overrides the checks above. Completeness is assessed by questions answered and gaps recorded, not by source counts.
