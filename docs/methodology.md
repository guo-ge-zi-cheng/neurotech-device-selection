# How the comparison was made

The question is how easily a team can find information that answers the five sections in Project spec. Scientific interest and the presence of a nearby expert do not answer that question by themselves.

For each section we record four things: the source found, how directly it concerns this device, what it lets the team explain, and what is still missing. “Clear sources” means a useful direct starting point is accessible. “Some gaps” means the source answers only part of the question or concerns the company more broadly. “Hard to find” means an important answer remains unverified in this search. These are descriptions of access to information, not scores for the device or proof that the report is complete.

The five sections follow the specification: quantitative unmet need and costs; first-version IP, inventors/employers and independent claims; early development finance; market-specific regulation, evidence, standards and manufacture; and payment, health economics and the required stakeholder map. If original funding cannot be found, the specification permits an estimate with its method stated.

A named contact must have a documented connection to the actual product, exact equipment or its invention/company history. The report then states which questions that person could help answer and which questions are outside that connection. A broad subject specialist is not included solely because they are nearby. No interview or visit is arranged.

The London comparison uses documented connections, not a survey of all local users. No figures establish which device has the largest London user community. An unsuccessful search means no user was verified here, not that no user exists.

Original sources were checked on 19 October 2026, with targeted additions/rechecks on 20 October. The catalog records actual coverage, including selective review, abstracts and indexed text when full fetch failed. Original market authorization, current clinical use and routine NHS funding remain separate questions.
