# How the comparison was made

The question is how easily a team can find information that answers the five sections in Project spec. Scientific interest and the presence of a nearby expert do not answer that question by themselves.

For each section we record four things: the source found, how it relates to this device and question, what it lets the team explain, and what is still missing. “Clear sources” means useful records provide a supported starting point for the section. “Some gaps” means the sources answer only part of the question. “Hard to find” means an important answer remains unverified in this search. These are descriptions of access to information, not scores for the device or proof that the report is complete. The six-device table retains the evidence already reviewed; adding alternative search routes does not automatically change a device's label.

Follow the [research source guide](research-source-guide.md), informed by the [two-example source audit](example-source-audit.md). Direct device records, disease and care-pathway research, investor/funder records, predecessor inventions, procedure payment, procurement, comparators and real interviews may answer different parts of one section. Search for the missing answer rather than accumulating sources. A source is direct relative to the claim: an investor filing can directly establish funding but not device R&D spend.

For new evidence record the supported claim, fact/background/inference/estimate status, device relationship, date/version/market, access route, actual review coverage and limit. A missing code is not evidence of no payment; a model is not a payer decision; a comparator is not proof of the product's performance or price. Keep an estimate of required development capital separate from actual fundraising. Record whether access is open, via a library subscription or dependent on a future conversation.

The five sections follow the specification: quantitative unmet need and costs; first-version IP, inventors/employers and independent claims; early development finance; market-specific regulation, evidence, standards and manufacture; and payment, health economics and the required stakeholder map. If original funding cannot be found, the specification permits an estimate with its method stated.

A named contact must have a documented connection to the product, its invention/company history, or a specific relevant clinical or purchasing question. The report states which questions that person could help answer and which are outside that connection. Clearly distinguish contextual care-pathway expertise from exact-device use. A broad subject specialist is not included solely because they are nearby. No interview or visit is arranged.

The London comparison uses documented connections, not a survey of all local users. No figures establish which device has the largest London user community. An unsuccessful search means no user was verified here, not that no user exists.

Original sources were checked on 19 October 2026, with targeted additions/rechecks on 20 October. The catalog records actual coverage, including selective review, abstracts and indexed text when full fetch failed. Original market authorization, current clinical use and routine NHS funding remain separate questions.
