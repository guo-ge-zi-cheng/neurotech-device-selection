# Screening methodology

The question is: which of these nine devices can this London-based team research well enough to write a complete engineering entrepreneurship case? Scientific interest, source accessibility and first-hand access matter for different reasons; one should not substitute for the others.

## Basis for the dimensions

The supplied assignment has five equally weighted content topics: clinical need, IP, early funding, regulation and reimbursement. Product scope is a prerequisite: a precise version and intended use determine which need, patent, funding event, evidence and payer policy are relevant. Public-source access is a practical constraint on completing the report. London access can add implementation or origin evidence, but an interview is not guaranteed and should not be a single point of failure.

## Readiness labels

- **S — Strong starting evidence:** substantive relevant primary material or a specific policy was located.
- **P — Partial:** concrete sources/leads exist, but important scope, attribution or interpretation work remains.
- **G — Material gap:** a selection-critical answer remains unresolved in the bounded search.

Grades measure research readiness, not clinical benefit, publication volume, or final report completeness. A small randomized study can be highly accessible and still have uncertain external validity. All regulation/manufacturing entries remain partial because a historical FDA record does not establish every current market, version and production detail. None of the nine has a completed independent-patent-claim map here.

The matrix is a qualitative synthesis of the 19 October source snapshot, structured on 20 October. It does not use a weighted total. An overall score would obscure a team's legitimate preference for neural interfaces, first-hand local access, or a particular business question.

## Evidence hierarchy and states

Start with original regulator records, clinical studies, patent/assignment records, SEC filings, NHS/NICE policy and institutional equipment/service pages. Use company records for what the company reports, with their commercial provenance visible. Record whether material was read in full, reviewed selectively, available as an abstract, or seen through search indexing when direct access was restricted.

Separate fact, manufacturer report, historical record, interpretation and unresolved gap. A failed fetch is not evidence of absence. A source being present does not mean it supports every claim about the device. The source catalog retains access dates and review coverage; the repository edition date does not refresh those checks.

## First-hand access categories

1. Exact equipment/site or exact-product clinician documented.
2. Founder/origin expert documented, with the business perspective distinguished from clinical use.
3. Related clinical/research domain expert only.
4. Overseas product team or no verified local product-specific route.

No category establishes willingness to meet, a booked visit, permission to observe patients, routine funding or current recruitment. TRIDENT is retained as a historical site lead and is marked completed in the checked record.

## Minimum follow-up after choosing a device

Read the relevant independent patent claims and trace original inventors/employers. Reconstruct early funding separately from later growth capital; label any cost estimate as an estimate with its method. Verify the exact initial product and authorization, relevant later changes, production arrangements and payment pathway. Obtain the lecture stakeholder-map format before claiming format compliance. Keep a source-based fallback if interviews do not happen.
