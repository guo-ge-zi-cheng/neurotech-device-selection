# Writing guide for this workspace

This guide separates recommended craft from mandatory assignment rules. Sources are the unchanged PDFs in the separately retained local course archive.

## Reader and structure

For selection briefs, write for engineering teammates who understand the module but may not know the clinical condition or device mechanism. Begin with the decision-relevant point: what makes this topic attractive, what will be hard, and whether local first-hand evidence is realistic. Explain unfamiliar device-specific ideas briefly, then connect mechanism to clinical evidence, regulatory route and payment. A full report should lead with its central case-study conclusion and then develop the five required topics.

The Nature collection (*The write stuff*, PDF pp1-2) emphasizes a single thread, a clear central message, reader orientation and claims grounded in evidence. Savage and Yeh's account of Cormac McCarthy's advice (2019, especially PDF pp2-5) emphasizes removing unnecessary material, one message per paragraph, direct sentences and concrete examples. The Medium article encourages understandable expression rather than ornate vocabulary. These are writing recommendations, not independent clinical evidence.

Humphrey and Holmes, *Style and Ethics of Communication in Science and Engineering* (152 PDF pages): contents and selected passages on outlining, critical editing, paragraph continuity, citation and technical reports were reviewed; the complete text was extracted for targeted retrieval, not read cover to cover. Preserve this coverage distinction.

## Paragraph and evidence checks

- Each paragraph should make one claim and give the evidence or reasoning needed to assess it.
- State the finding before secondary background; avoid a long generic account of neurotechnology.
- Keep dates, jurisdictions, populations, denominators, device versions and comparison groups attached to numbers.
- Separate a manufacturer's interpretation from a trial result or regulator's indication.
- Use figures/tables only when they clarify a decision. Captions must explain what is compared and what the figure cannot establish.
- Make source identity retrievable without relying only on hyperlinks; the team-facing HTML can link sources for convenience, but a future assessed document needs a printable reference list.

The Mindy Barker report article (2019, PDF pp2-3) links visuals to a real decision, a defined measure, accountability and a resulting action. Apply that principle to the selection table instead of adding decorative scores.
