# Sources used in the two course examples

This audit records what the examples used, what those sources help explain and where the inference stops. Use the [research source guide](research-source-guide.md) for the practical method. Checked 20 October 2026. Physical PDF page numbers include the opening anonymisation page and differ from printed page numbers.

## Authority and review scope

- **Explicit course requirements:** Personal communications are allowed if actually obtained. When earliest funding is unavailable, estimate required capital using bottom-up/top-down approaches and state the method. Relevant source suggestions include filings, registries, Imperial Library/PitchBook, patents and market-specific payment resources. (Project spec 2026.pdf, physical PDF pages 1-3).
- **Observed source use:** The examples combine direct product records with disease/workflow studies, investors, grant programmes, transaction databases, supplier material, provider prices, comparisons and reported interviews. Appearance in an example does not establish approval of every attached claim. (The ten report/topic entries below).
- **Visible positive feedback:** Good coverage is annotated beside Quadsense fundraising and the stakeholder map; its scope should not be extended to every reference or conclusion. (Quadsense_MDE.pdf, physical PDF pages 7 and 13).
- **Visible corrective feedback:** Quadsense has comments requesting citations for paraphrases, less basic information and better paragraph structure. Optomed has a warning about lost embedded references and mark deductions. (Quadsense_MDE.pdf, physical PDF pages 8-9 and 11-12; Optomed_Aurora_MDE_Report.pdf, physical PDF page 1).
- **Our methodological recommendation:** Assess how well each question can be answered using fact, background, inference and explicit estimates; retain unknowns. Record source fit and access effort before changing a device access label. (Synthesis of the specification, example audit and selected source checks; not a lecturer quotation).

All three specification pages were reviewed. Both examples were rendered and their relevant sections and references inspected using extraction/OCR plus visual checks. Important feedback and selected citation details were checked visually. External checking was selective, as recorded below. Database valuations, interview accounts, all patent legal statuses and all payer conclusions were not independently re-established.

## Unmet medical need

### Optomed Aurora

Location: PDF 2-4; interviews listed on PDF 18. References: [1-18]; interviews A and B.

**Sources used:** WHO; AAO/NEI and disease organisations; epidemiology and screening/cost papers; ophthalmologist and device-company interviews.

**What they help explain:** Describe who lacks screening, the burden and geographic distribution of disease, the old care pathway, and practical barriers to access.

**Extra route for our research:** Use disease-level data and studies of the existing care pathway when exact-brand outcome or cost data are limited. Interview a clinician who knows that pathway.

**Limits:** Disease burden is not device benefit. The example itself says savings could not be reliably quantified. A clinician cannot establish product performance outside their experience.

**Lecturer evidence:** Source use observed; no source-specific endorsement identified.

### Quadsense

Location: PDF 3-4; bibliography PDF 15. References: [2-16], especially [4-5], [8], [10-14].

**Sources used:** Journal studies; WHO/NHS; company projections; commercial market research; studies of dissatisfaction and painful/revision knee replacement.

**What they help explain:** Connect osteoarthritis and knee surgery volumes to the specific unresolved problem: poor outcomes, dissatisfaction and the cost of investigation or revision.

**Extra route for our research:** Use procedure registries, epidemiology and studies of current treatment failures, rather than waiting for a device-specific market report.

**Limits:** Overall knee-surgery market size is not the device-eligible population. Company forecasts and commercial market shares need their methods and denominators checked.

**Lecturer evidence:** Source use observed; no source-specific endorsement identified.

## Intellectual property

### Optomed Aurora

Location: PDF 10. References: [51-55], [57].

**Sources used:** EPO and USPTO records; patent classification search; company IP list; trademark records.

**What they help explain:** Find candidate patent families, dates, jurisdictions and likely inventors; distinguish technical protection from branding.

**Extra route for our research:** Search the original employer/assignee, inventor names, technical features and predecessor devices when the marketed device name returns little.

**Limits:** The authors explicitly assume pre-launch patents may relate to Aurora. Date overlap and common inventors do not prove claim coverage. Independent-claim analysis is still required by the specification.

**Lecturer evidence:** Source use observed; not permission to replace claim analysis with a portfolio count.

### Quadsense

Location: PDF 5-7; bibliography PDF 15-16. References: [19-23]; personal communication [17] for development context.

**Sources used:** Espacenet patent family and publications; UK IPO trademark register; reported developer/inventor history.

**What they help explain:** Follow the trial-patella kit family and paraphrase its first independent claim, then connect the invention to the device design and development timeline.

**Extra route for our research:** A patent may be filed under a generic technical title, an inventor or an earlier legal entity rather than the product brand. Follow the family and priority chain.

**Limits:** Read the claims of the exact publication/jurisdiction used. Patent status and claims can differ across family members. A trademark supports a branding claim, not technical exclusivity.

**Lecturer evidence:** Source use observed; current patent status and all example legal interpretations were not independently re-audited.

## Fundraising

### Optomed Aurora

Location: PDF 7-9; interviews PDF 18. References: [43], [46-50]; interviews C and D.

**Sources used:** IPO prospectus; PitchBook; Tracxn; Tesi investor portfolio; Halma investor annual report; company annual report; former-board interviews.

**What they help explain:** Reconstruct the company funding timeline from several sides of each transaction and explain the remaining gaps in early funding and valuations.

**Extra route for our research:** Search investors, grant funders, university commercialisation offices and accelerators as well as the company. If early financing remains unknown, build a labelled estimate under the specification.

**Limits:** Company finance is not spending on Aurora. The example could not calculate valuations. The investor source records EUR 2 million in 2010; the example chart labels a $2 million entry, so retain original currencies and explain conversions.

**Lecturer evidence:** Source use observed. Opening lecturer note criticises lost embedded references, not the absence of confidential valuations.

### Quadsense

Location: PDF 7-8; bibliography PDF 16. References: [17], [24-30].

**Sources used:** PitchBook; FinSMEs announcements; company statement; Mercia investor material; Innovate UK Business Connect; CEO personal communication.

**What they help explain:** Combine round size, investors, stated use of proceeds and interview-only development/operating costs to explain the path to market.

**Extra route for our research:** Use an investor announcement to check press coverage; funder case studies can identify grants, incubators, partners and non-cash support. Genuine interviews can fill private details.

**Limits:** Database valuations were not independently verified here. Recent grants and funding may also support Hipsense, a different product. Do not assign all company funding to Quadsense.

**Lecturer evidence:** A visible "Good coverage" annotation sits beside the fundraising heading on PDF 7. It is positive section feedback, not approval of each number.

## Regulatory

### Optomed Aurora

Location: PDF 11-14. References: [43], [59], [61-69], [92-93]; interview B.

**Sources used:** Exact FDA 510(k); GUDID and EUDAMED; government guidance; commercial regulatory explainers; prospectus; manufacturer website/certificates; device-company interview.

**What they help explain:** Connect the device to its intended use, market pathway, classification, tested standards and manufacturing arrangements.

**Extra route for our research:** Use the regulator decision/summary and instructions for use if the company website is vague; use a prospectus or counterparty record for outsourcing details.

**Limits:** A generic rule does not prove the device complied with it. A supplier capability page does not prove a customer relationship; the prospectus provides the relationship evidence in this example.

**Lecturer evidence:** Source use observed; no blanket approval of the example regulatory wording or commercial explainers.

### Quadsense

Location: PDF 8-11; bibliography PDF 16-17. References: [31-44].

**Sources used:** Company launch announcement; FDA classification and exact 510(k) summaries; predicate brochure; MHRA registry; GOV.UK approved-body list; Medsafe guidance; IFU; research papers; supplier sites.

**What they help explain:** Explain the original clearance and later version, compare relevant devices, and identify standards and manufacturing tasks.

**Extra route for our research:** The predicate decision and device IFU can explain the evidence route even when detailed development files are unavailable; official registers identify actors and dates.

**Limits:** A predicate brochure is supporting context, not the clearance decision. Registration is not approval. Supplier websites establish capability unless a separate source links them to the exact product.

**Lecturer evidence:** Visible comments include "Include citation for paraphrasing" and "Basic Info not needed". These favour precise case evidence over a generic regulation tutorial.

## Reimbursement

### Optomed Aurora

Location: PDF 15-17. References: [94-99]; interviews B and D.

**Sources used:** Company/distributor price pages; a manufacturer blog listing CPT amounts; newer product pages; NCQA HEDIS; reported interviews; a stakeholder map.

**What they help explain:** Separate hospital purchase or subscription from the payment for screening, and discuss workflow, access and possible economic incentives.

**Extra route for our research:** Use a clearly labelled successor/comparator price to make a range; study the service payment and procurement route rather than searching only for a brand-specific code.

**Limits:** Aurora IQ pricing is a proxy, not verified original-Aurora pricing. Reference [97] does contain tariff amounts, but is a manufacturer blog dated 2021, not a current payer rate table. A newer AI product cannot automatically establish the original device workflow or savings.

**Lecturer evidence:** Source use observed; no visible positive annotation specifically validating these proxy or tariff claims.

### Quadsense

Location: PDF 11-13; bibliography PDF 17. References: [17], [45-51].

**Sources used:** CMS; NHS OPCS; New Zealand coding/device purchasing resources; private-provider surgery prices; NHS recovery information; company FAQ; CEO personal communication.

**What they help explain:** Map who buys the sensor, how it fits within surgery, what costs might change and where benefits and money flow.

**Extra route for our research:** Use procedure-level payment, hospital procurement and a transparent scenario model when a distinct device payment code or long-term economic study is absent.

**Limits:** The example says true economic impact remains predictive. No separate device code does not establish lack of payment. A private surgery charge is not an NHS cost or tariff; the CMS homepage alone cannot establish a coverage conclusion.

**Lecturer evidence:** "Good coverage" appears beside the stakeholder map on PDF 13; "Poor paragraph structure" appears on PDF 12. Do not extend the map praise to every reimbursement claim.

## Reference handling

Optomed's opening note reports that embedded reference links were lost in submission, causing a mark deduction. The supplied copy nevertheless contains recoverable link annotations. These observations concern different copies and can both be true. Quadsense has a printed bibliography; its image-based body was read with OCR and visual checks. Use full retrievable references in the eventual report as the specification requires.

The original course/example PDFs and interview material have not been republished. Interviews described in the examples were conducted by those reports' authors, not this team. No complete patent-status, database-valuation or payer audit is claimed. This example audit does not change the six devices' access labels.
