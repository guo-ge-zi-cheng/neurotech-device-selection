# Research sources for the five report sections

Use this guide when an important question remains unanswered by the device company's own documents. The aim is to explain the device's route to market with evidence whose role and limits are clear. Start with the exact original product, period and market.

This guide draws on Project spec 2026 and a source audit of the Optomed Aurora and Quadsense examples. [The audit](example-source-audit.md) records their PDF pages, references, feedback and selected external checks. The specification is the authority for requirements. Sources used in an example demonstrate search routes; they do not establish blanket lecturer approval.

Before accepting evidence, apply the [reliability protocol](reliability-protocol.md). These routes have now been tested for all seven devices: [see the findings overview](additional-research-summary.md) and Layer 2 in each device report. New findings supplement the preserved original research; unresolved questions stay visible.

## 1. Unmet medical need

**Questions to answer:** Who had the problem when the device was developed? How many patients, in which places? What care did they receive, what failed and what did it cost? What benefit has this particular device demonstrated?

| Source route | How it helps | Limit to preserve |
|---|---|---|
| Disease studies, registries, WHO/NHS and relevant professional bodies | Population, severity, geography and unmet need | The disease population is larger than the device-eligible population in many cases. State the denominator. |
| Studies of the old care pathway, treatment failures, waiting times and costs | Explain the problem the inventors were trying to solve | A weak existing treatment does not prove this device solves the problem. |
| Exact-device trials and evaluations | Measured benefits, risks, comparators and follow-up | Keep the study design, product version and population visible. |
| Clinicians running the relevant service; patient organisations | Practical barriers, patient selection and priorities for improvement | Attribute experience. It is context unless it concerns actual device use or measured outcomes. |

The examples combine disease burden and existing-care studies with product and clinical explanations. Where post-device savings are unavailable, explain the proposed mechanism and the missing outcome evidence; do not turn a plausible benefit into a quantified saving.

## 2. Intellectual property

**Questions to answer:** Where was the first invention made? Who invented it and employed the inventors? What protection or licence enabled development? What do the relevant independent patent claims cover?

| Source route | How it helps | Limit to preserve |
|---|---|---|
| Patent registers searched by technical feature, inventor and original assignee | Find the invention when the commercial brand returns little; follow priority and family history | Shared dates, inventors or a company name identify candidates, not a completed product-to-claim map. |
| University technology-transfer records, original-employer announcements and licences | Trace the origin and transfer to a company | A research collaboration does not automatically establish ownership or a licence. |
| Product technical documents compared with the exact claims | Explain which features the claimed invention concerns | Specify the publication, jurisdiction and relevant date; application claims and granted claims can differ. |
| Trademark/design registers; documented know-how or agreements | Explain other forms of protection and commercial identity | A trademark is branding evidence, not technical patent coverage. Do not invent confidential know-how. |

Quadsense uses a technical patent title and discusses an independent claim. Optomed searches a wider portfolio and explicitly assumes some pre-launch families relate to Aurora. That is a useful search lead, with weaker attribution. The specification's independent-claim requirement still applies.

## 3. Fundraising

**Questions to answer:** What funded early development, who supplied it, when, how much and for which milestone? What valuations are disclosed? What support came from grants, incubators or infrastructure?

| Source route | How it helps | Limit to preserve |
|---|---|---|
| Company filings, IPO prospectus and registry filings | Historical rounds, ownership, risks and use of proceeds | Company-level funds are not automatically a device-specific budget. |
| Investor annual reports, portfolio records and announcements | Confirm the other side of a transaction and identify early backers | A fund's size is not its investment in this company; an announcement may omit valuations. |
| Public grant/funder records, university and accelerator case studies | Identify grants, milestones, partners and non-cash support | Check recipient, dates and product. Later grants may support a successor device. |
| Imperial Library/PitchBook, other transaction databases and funding news | Find leads, dates and possible valuations to cross-check | Record subscription/access needs and original currency. Do not treat duplicated news as independent confirmation. |
| Actual founder, early staff, investor or funder interviews | Explain private milestones and costs within the person's knowledge | Attribute the statement and date; recollection is not an audited account. |

**If original funding is still unknown:** the specification explicitly permits an estimate with its method stated. For a bottom-up range, source costs for staff, prototypes, preclinical work, trials, quality/regulatory work, IP, manufacturing setup and overhead by milestone. For a top-down range, compare similar devices at the same development stage, adjusting for trial scale, risk, place and year. Reconcile the ranges. Call this **estimated capital required** and retain unknown historical fundraising or valuations as unknown.

The Optomed example uses an investor's annual report; Quadsense combines a database, funding news, investor/funder material and a reported CEO interview. These routes can improve a section without revealing every private number.

## 4. Regulatory and manufacturing

**Questions to answer:** What was the device classified as in each relevant market? What evidence enabled clearance/approval? What standards, manufacturing processes and partners were involved?

| Source route | How it helps | Limit to preserve |
|---|---|---|
| Exact regulator decision/summary, official database and instructions for use | Intended use, class, evidence, predicate and limitations | Historical authorisation is not a complete current-label audit. Keep versions separate. |
| Official rules, guidance and standards referenced in the actual record | Explain why a particular route or test was used | A generic requirement does not prove that the device met it. Registration is not approval. |
| Predicate and predecessor records, supporting studies | Explain the comparison and development changes | A competitor brochure cannot establish the chosen device's clearance. |
| Prospectus, supplier agreement, explicit counterparty statement or informed interview | Identify actual outsourcing and manufacturing responsibilities | A factory's website or certificate alone establishes capability, not a customer relationship. |

The examples use exact FDA records alongside instructions, registers and supporting material. Optomed's prospectus supplies the manufacturing relationship evidence; supplier pages then help explain capability. Prefer a short account of what the developers had to do over a general tutorial about regulation.

## 5. Reimbursement and health economics

**Questions to answer:** Who buys the device, who pays for the care, from which budget or payment route, and under what conditions? What economic case can be supported? How do products, information and money flow in the required stakeholder map?

| Source route | How it helps | Limit to preserve |
|---|---|---|
| Exact payer/commissioning policy, procedure codes, fee schedules and payment manuals | Explain service payment, eligibility, setting and amounts | A code is not a coverage decision. Record payer, place, year and setting. |
| Procurement/tender records, service budgets and purchasing staff | Explain equipment, consumables or subscriptions within a hospital/service budget | No separate brand-specific code does not prove that the device receives no funding. |
| Manufacturer/distributor price, provider charges and comparator/successor prices | Supply price evidence or a stated range for a model | A charge, NHS cost, tariff and purchase price are different quantities. Label proxy prices and explain differences. |
| Economic studies, current-care costs, measured workflow changes and real interviews | Estimate where extra spending and possible savings arise | A model or company claim is not a proven saving or payer decision. Show assumptions and scenarios. |

Optomed uses successor pricing and screening payment; Quadsense uses surgery-level payment, provider prices and a reported interview. Both show how to develop an economic explanation when exact product economics are incomplete. A possible payment route should remain a hypothesis until its applicability is checked. Build the stakeholder map using the actual lecture instructions when available.

## Record what a source establishes

For each useful source, keep a short entry:

> **Question / claim:** What does this answer? **Evidence:** document title, URL or real personal communication, page/section, date and what was reviewed. **Connection:** exact device, predecessor, company, target population, comparator or general system. **Status:** fact, background, inference, estimate or unknown. **Use and limit:** how it helps this section and what it cannot establish. **Access:** open web, archive, university subscription or a conversation still to be obtained.

A source's role depends on the claim. An investor's filing is direct evidence of its investment, while that same record may provide only background for a device budget. Keep the evidence already found separate from a promising search route or an interview request.

## First-hand enquiries in and near London

Recommend a named person only when a documented role or activity explains why they could answer a specific question. Separate:

- **Exact-device experience:** clinical use, investigation, invention or early development.
- **Care-pathway experience:** treating the actual target population or running the relevant service; useful for unmet need, workflow and adoption, without proving brand use.
- **Purchasing/funding experience:** procurement, commissioning, hospital finance, an investor or a grant programme, within the person's remit.

Broad field expertise alone does not justify singling out a contact. Do not infer willingness to meet from a public email, or an interview from a published profile. Cite personal communications only after a real conversation, with date and attribution agreed. No outreach is currently authorised.

## Use this context in the seven-device comparison

Keep the existing five topics and concise README. For each missing answer, identify a useful alternative route, investigate it, then explain what remains unknown. Do not increase an access label merely because alternatives exist. A useful comparison states which questions can be answered, which need inference or an estimate, and which remain unsupported. Keep information access effort separate from evidence strength.
