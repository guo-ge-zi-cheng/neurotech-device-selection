# Comparing the six full report drafts

These are assessments of the **drafts and the evidence available to write them**, not rankings of clinical effectiveness or investment opportunities. Read the [six reports](README.md) alongside this review. No marks from a lecturer, independent examiner or predicted grade are claimed.

## Main finding

**RNS currently supports the most connected public case study. Exablate offers the strongest documented London investigation route and UK service/payment story. Ceribell is particularly useful for explaining academic technology transfer and early commercial finance.** SAINT has a coherent translation narrative but less complete present service economics. ARC-EX offers useful nearby exact-device access, while original IP/finance attribution and payment remain difficult. Precision has the greatest scope mismatch between an ambitious platform story and the evidence for the selected marketed component.

These differences emerged while writing the five sections, rather than from counting sources. The original quick access labels remain unchanged historical notes. This review assesses the new full drafts, including the additional research and deeper patent checks.

## How quality was judged

Project spec gives each content topic 17% and writing 15%. That makes a missing finance or reimbursement answer consequential even when the mechanism and trial sections are strong. I used the following questions, not an invented numerical score:

| Area | What a good draft should do |
|---|---|
| Unmet need | Define the original patient problem; quantify a scoped population and geography; compare prior care, outcomes and costs without equating burden with device benefit. |
| IP | Trace the first invention and inventors/employers; explain the selected independent claims; distinguish technical candidates, actual licensed rights and exact product coverage. |
| Fundraising | Give dated early sources/amounts and valuations where available; distinguish company capital, device expenditure, grants, debt/conversion and later growth. If estimating required capital, provide justified inputs and a method. |
| Regulatory/manufacturing | Explain the original market route and supporting evidence, relevant standards and actual manufacturing responsibilities; retain version and country boundaries. |
| Reimbursement | Identify the payer/buyer and payment conditions, distinguish codes/coverage/amounts from models, and analyse the whole pathway with a stakeholder map. |
| Writing | Lead with a case-specific conclusion, develop a coherent argument, use clear paragraphs and informative captions, keep citations retrievable and remain within the word budget. |

The source guide and the Optomed/Quadsense audit informed the use of broader sources. Those examples do not prove that every source or inference would receive lecturer approval. The separate [writing-method note](../docs/full-report-writing-method.md) records the actual module materials used and the limits of review.

## Assessment of each draft and actual writing difficulties

### RNS: the most complete account for public desk research

The draft can connect an original patient group, controlled evidence, longer follow-up, a foundational architecture, pre-approval capital, manufacturing and a payer policy. This makes its central argument easier to sustain across the whole report. The economic model and payer policy also let the reimbursement section explain value and eligibility separately.

The difficult part was preserving time and scope. The 75% nine-year reduction is open-label follow-up, whereas the earlier 37.9%/17.3% figures belong to the blinded trial. The 2011 filing reports securities sold, not a completed all-cash round. MRI status differs by model. Treating any of these as a single universal product fact would have produced a smoother but less reliable story.

The draft still lacks the first funding/employment/assignment chain, actual current fees and a verified NHS pathway. It is the strongest starting draft, not a completed submission. A useful next investigation would target the earliest company history rather than add another recent clinical paper.

### Ceribell: strongest academic-to-company detail, harder outcome economics

The combination of an actual university licence, grant attribution, early net proceeds and original hardware decisions gives this draft unusually specific development material. The report can explain how a clinical access problem became a licensed, manufactured product without relying only on company slogans.

The hardest writing task was separating at least three things: the original rapid-EEG hardware/sonification, later automated software and newer payment decisions. DECIDE measures access and physician decisions; it is not an AI accuracy trial or mortality trial. A 38-patient pilot with one positive case cannot support an impressive-looking perfect-sensitivity claim. The economic paper predicts savings, and the expired seizure-monitor add-on cannot be replaced by citing a separate delirium award.

The present draft is strong on origin, finance and version history. Its weaker part is demonstrating realised patient/cost benefit and sustainable current purchasing/payment terms. A hospital workflow and finance discussion would add more than another founder biography.

### Exablate: strongest opportunity to improve the draft through London investigation

This draft has the clearest connection among actual local equipment, a specialist team, an NHS commissioning source and an England economic model. It can discuss how infrastructure and patient selection affect an established service, and the model's published critique/reply supports real critical analysis.

The difficult part was preventing apparent contradictions from being silently resolved. The 2012 agreement and closing differ; one document also contains conflicting valuation figures. The economic abstract has a comparator-cost inconsistency, so the body tables were used. A critique questioned cost assumptions, but the authors' reply needed to be read before claiming an omission. Essential-tremor and Parkinsonian-tremor NHS positions also differ.

The draft is convincing on the service/value question, but exact first-version IP, early brain-specific capital and manufacture remain underdeveloped. The London team is a useful route for patient selection and capacity, not automatically for confidential company finance. If firsthand investigation is a priority, this is the most promising report to take forward.

### SAINT: coherent translation story, incomplete ongoing business model

The draft has a clear central question: whether a personalised five-day treatment can be delivered reproducibly and paid for sustainably. The academic/licensor, launch-finance, clearance and supplier records support that question well.

Writing was difficult because dramatic remission percentages can dominate the story. The early trial is small and stopped at an interim analysis; the original regulatory tables also have an inconsistent active/sham header. The draft follows the consistent narrative/rows and retains the denominator. A four-to-six-week UK comparator is useful service context but not SAINT evidence. Fifty sessions and 500 active stimulation minutes also show why a compressed calendar does not establish low resource use.

The weakest section is present service economics after the inpatient NTAP ends. Durability, retreatment, MRI/staff inputs and actual current coverage require better evidence. A London TMS clinic should only be used for a clearly scoped comparator/workflow question unless licensed SAINT delivery is verified.

### ARC-EX: strong implementation potential, significant historical attribution gaps

The clinical/regulatory narrative is substantial, and the nearby provider/funder relationships give a realistic way to ask practical questions. This draft is useful for understanding a device whose performance depends on rehabilitation delivery as much as equipment.

The largest difficulty was following the external technology through academic work, NRT, a merger and a company with distinct implanted programmes. The €36m G-Therapeutics package is explicitly implant-related and cannot be assigned to ARC-EX. The candidate patent's independent claim has a function-scope complication for hand-function attribution. The 72% trial result uses 60 completers, not all 65 enrolled, and the sequential design does not remove training/order effects.

The report reads coherently but remains materially incomplete in original IP, earliest product finance and payment. Charity loans and a provider listing do not settle sustainable reimbursement. Provider/funder conversations could improve implementation and payment evidence; a licensor/company history source is needed for the original rights and capital questions.

### Precision: hardest to complete without choosing a much narrower question

The draft's main strength is a disciplined boundary around the marketed temporary Layer 7-T. Early rounds, a fabrication acquisition, technical evidence and a relevant application claim set provide a useful staged-development case.

The hardest writing problem is that the most compelling platform narrative concerns a future assistive BCI, while the selected clearance concerns a temporary electrode. Paralysis burden, secondary-device control claims and future home-use economics cannot automatically justify the current component's market. Acute feasibility also does not establish a comparative clinical benefit or a procurement case.

The draft therefore has the largest unresolved connection between need, benefit and payment. A product-specific clinical/purchasing source and eligible procedure count would change its quality more than broader neurotechnology coverage. It is a valid entrepreneurship case, but currently the hardest one to finish convincingly across all five weighted topics.

## What remains incomplete in all six drafts

Each draft contains all five narrative sections, a description, claim analysis, financing discussion, an analytical stakeholder diagram, conclusion and retrievable references. Each is within the 4,000-word budget under the recorded counting method. This is structural completion, not a declaration that every specification question is answered.

No original interviews were conducted. Contemporaneous inventor employment and exact product-to-claim maps remain incomplete. No fully justified bottom-up/top-down numerical estimate of missing early capital was produced because attributable staff, study and manufacturing costs were not verified; the drafts explain the method and missing inputs instead of presenting invented history. That is a real funding weakness against the specification, not something a longer paragraph solves.

Current local prices, full current multi-market authorisation and independently verified manufacturing chains are uneven. The stakeholder diagrams are analytical drafts because the lecture-specific required format was not supplied. The documents do not include invented group responsibilities or a false AI-policy declaration, and should not be treated as assessed submissions.

## Practical choice for the team

For a public-source project with the fewest disconnected sections, **start with RNS**. For a London investigation that could materially improve service, cost and adoption analysis, **start with Exablate**. For a strong academic licensing and early-finance story, **consider Ceribell**. ARC-EX remains attractive if the team values its mechanism and can pursue the specific provider/funder/licensor gaps. SAINT is coherent but needs a firmer service-economics account; Precision requires the clearest scope decision and the most original procurement/eligible-market work.

These recommendations concern research feasibility and draft completeness. They are not clinical recommendations or evidence that one technology is medically better than another.
