# Choosing a device through evidence

**Start with the [device-by-device research overview](research/README.md).** Each device below has a plain-language guide explaining what its sources let us write and exactly what is still missing.

The guides address unmet medical need, intellectual property, fundraising, regulation and reimbursement. Each uses the same 31 questions from the supplied report guide. Answers are written as bullet lists: the requirement, the source content, its contribution and limits, the unanswered question and the next investigation. There are no evidence grades or comparison tables in this reading path.

## Precision Neuroscience — Layer 7-T

Precision’s Layer 7-T is a flexible electrode placed on the brain surface for temporary recording, monitoring or stimulation during neurosurgical care. The evidence reviewed concerns this temporary component, not a permanent home-use brain–computer interface. Its FDA record and small acute study explain the product and early feasibility; they leave its effect on patient outcomes and hospital costs unresolved. [Read the evidence guide](research/precision.md).

## NeuroPace — RNS System

NeuroPace’s RNS System uses implanted electrodes and a cranial stimulator to record brain activity and respond to detected seizure patterns with stimulation. It is studied here for selected adults with drug-resistant focal epilepsy. Trials, a prospectus, the original FDA approval and an economic model provide a connected account, while original funding allocations, current purchase prices and UK service arrangements remain incomplete. [Read the evidence guide](research/rns.md).

## Ceribell — Rapid Response EEG

Ceribell combines a rapidly applied scalp EEG system with software that helps clinicians assess brain activity in acute care. The hardware, sonification technology and later seizure-monitoring software need to be kept separate. The reviewed material explains an EEG-access problem, licensing and early finance, but it does not establish that faster EEG produces the claimed financial or patient benefits in London. [Read the evidence guide](research/ceribell.md).

## Magnus Medical — SAINT

Magnus Medical’s SAINT combines MRI-informed targeting with an accelerated schedule of transcranial magnetic stimulation for depression. The selected case is the integrated system covered by the original US clearance, rather than accelerated TMS generally. There is a small controlled trial and useful patent and fundraising material; durability, the full delivery cost and actual local payment still need investigation. [Read the evidence guide](research/saint.md).

## Insightec — Exablate Neuro

Insightec’s Exablate Neuro uses MRI-guided focused ultrasound to create a targeted thermal lesion. This case focuses on the original unilateral treatment for medication-refractory essential tremor. A controlled trial, UK commissioning evidence, an England economic model and a documented London service support the investigation. Early brain-device funding, original patent ownership and actual equipment and service costs remain less complete. [Read the evidence guide](research/exablate.md).

## ONWARD Medical — ARC-EX

ONWARD Medical’s ARC-EX applies external spinal stimulation alongside rehabilitation to improve hand function in selected people with chronic incomplete cervical spinal cord injury. It is separate from ONWARD’s implanted and brain-interface programmes. Regulatory documents, a rehabilitation study and nearby service connections help explain the case, while the original patent match, attributable early funding and payment arrangements remain unresolved. [Read the evidence guide](research/arcex.md).

## Siemens Healthineers — MAGNETOM Terra

Siemens Healthineers’ MAGNETOM Terra is a 7-tesla MRI platform, studied here within the original head and extremity imaging scope. The original system must be separated from Terra.X and later upgrades. Regulatory and subsystem records, a neurovascular comparison and exact equipment at King’s support investigation; original scanner IP, attributable development capital and the economics of clinical use are still incomplete. [Read the evidence guide](research/terra.md).


The [common requirements](docs/report-evidence-requirements.md) come from the supplied guide's evidence tables, with page provenance. One concrete improvement is full access to the RNS economic paper; its cost inputs remain a historical model, rather than a current device quote. Proposed searches are labelled and do not count as evidence found.
