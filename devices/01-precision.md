## Layer 1: Original research

Original snapshot: 19 October 2026, with earlier targeted additions on 20 October (ARC-EX was added on 20 October). The text and access labels below are preserved. Read Layer 2 for new evidence and dated updates.

### What does it do?

Layer 7 is a thin, flexible sheet of electrodes placed on the surface of the brain. The electrodes measure small voltage changes produced by nearby neural activity. Its surface position differs from an array of needles that penetrates the cortex. The sheet must work with recording electronics and other equipment; it is not a complete computer-control system by itself.

The immediate task to understand is cortical recording and monitoring around neurosurgical care. A high-density array can collect signals from many closely spaced locations, which may help researchers or clinicians study activity over the exposed cortex. Separately, the company aims to use cortical signals in an assistive brain-computer interface. That larger task also requires reliable decoding, a practical implant system and evidence that a person can use it over time.

For this coursework, choose the exact product before collecting a market size or business story. The temporary Layer 7-T electrode and a future long-term assistive BCI have different users, clinical evidence, costs and buyers. Treating them as one finished product would make all five sections difficult to support.

### How far has it developed?

A temporary electrode component has a 2025 FDA 510(k) record for use of less than 30 days. The company’s broader assistive BCI remains a separate investigational development. The clearest report would study the marketed component and explain the larger platform as its development context.

Sources for this description and stage: [FDA • PRECISION K242618](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cdrh_docs/pdf24/K242618.pdf); [Precision Neuroscience product and company site](https://www.precisionneuro.io/).

#### What does the clinical evidence actually show?

The reviewed FDA record, K242618 (2025), covers Layer 7-T cortical electrodes used with recording, monitoring and stimulation equipment for temporary use of less than 30 days. It does not establish approval of a permanent implant that restores computer control. The company separately describes the broader BCI system as investigational. This boundary changes the need, evidence and reimbursement sections.

**Practical takeaway:** Interesting BCI technology, but first decide whether the report concerns the temporary electrode or the broader platform. Payment and exact-product IP are the main information gaps.

## Layer 2: Additional research (20 October 2026)

The extra search adds an earlier financing round, a closer array/insertion patent candidate and a real manufacturing acquisition. Acute clinical evidence is stronger than a product description; payment for the cleared temporary component is still unresolved.

**How to read this layer:** each finding states what the evidence supports, why it is usable, how it helps the report and where it stops. [Reliability protocol](../docs/reliability-protocol.md). “Context” is useful background, not a result for this product. No new interview has taken place.

### 1. Unmet medical need

**Search route:** Original technical study rather than only broad paralysis statistics.

#### What the acute human study can establish

**Study result.** Hettick et al. describe acute cortical-array work in five neurosurgical patients: two under general anaesthesia and three during awake language mapping. This supports feasibility in a surgical setting. It is not a trial of durable computer control for people with paralysis.

**Why this evidence is usable:** An original study describes the actual platform and participants. Precision supported the work and many authors report company relationships; it is not independent clinical validation.

**Use in the report:** Separate the immediate temporary-recording use case from the longer-term assistive BCI ambition. Define the unmet problem for the product scope chosen before estimating eligible patients.

**Limit:** Small acute pilot; neither long-term benefit nor patient numbers, hospital savings or stimulation efficacy for the marketed Layer 7-T are established.

Sources: [Hettick et al.: cortical-array technology and acute human pilot](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41551-025-01501-w).

**Where checked:** Human clinical pilot; methods; funding and competing interests. **Evidence route:** New source route. Checked 2026-10-20; source dates and review coverage are in the [source index](../docs/sources.md).

**Remaining gap:** A scope-matched eligible population, prior-care costs and clinical outcomes for the selected use case are still needed.

### 2. Intellectual property

**Search route:** Search the named inventors and array/insertion mechanism, then read independent claims.

#### A closer technical patent candidate

**Documented fact.** US20230210427A1 names Rapoport, Papageorgiou, Hettick, Poole and Ho; it claims priority from 31 December 2021 and lists a Precision assignment. Claim 1 covers a flexible, nonpenetrating electrode assembly with a pocket for an insertion paddle. Claim 5 covers the insertion method, including advancing to a subdural target and withdrawing the paddle.

**Why this evidence is usable:** The actual application text is more informative than a company statement that it has patents. The identified mechanics connect to the platform described in the study.

**Use in the report:** Explain what the proposed protection concerns: array construction and delivery. Start a claim-to-component table for the selected Layer 7-T configuration.

**Limit:** This is an application publication. Exact marketed-product mapping, later prosecution/grant status, all other independent claims and inventors’ employment at invention remain unverified.

Sources: [US20230210427A1: neural interfaces and insertion system](https://patents.google.com/patent/US20230210427A1/en).

**Where checked:** Bibliographic/assignment metadata; independent claims 1 and 5. **Evidence route:** New source route. Checked 2026-10-20; source dates and review coverage are in the [source index](../docs/sources.md).

**Remaining gap:** Obtain official prosecution/assignment records and map the final product to every relevant independent claim before asserting patent protection.

### 5. Reimbursement

**Search route:** Look for exact-product payment; use official procedure-payment context only where direct evidence is absent.

#### Payment remains a scoped question

**Context.** CMS explains that some integral hospital outpatient items are included in the payment for the main service rather than paid separately. This provides a possible route to investigate, not a payment determination for Layer 7-T.

**Why this evidence is usable:** CMS is the payment-system authority. The passage was read, but it does not name this product.

**Use in the report:** Ask which procedure and setting use the temporary array, which provider buys it and whether its cost sits within a service payment or another arrangement. Put hospital purchasing and the clinical service on the stakeholder map.

**Limit:** No Layer 7-T-specific coverage policy, fee, procurement contract or verified price was found. Do not assign an outpatient mechanism to inpatient use, or infer that no separate code means no payment.

Sources: [CMS: Medicare payment systems and packaged outpatient costs](https://www.cms.gov/outreach-and-education/medicare-learning-network-mln/mlnproducts/html/medicare-payment-systems.html).

**Where checked:** Hospital Outpatient Prospective Payment System: Packaged Costs. **Evidence route:** New source route. Checked 2026-10-20; source dates and review coverage are in the [source index](../docs/sources.md).

**Remaining gap:** A real use-setting/code map and buyer or payer evidence are needed; future chronic BCI reimbursement is a separate question.
