Choose Exablate for a combined clinical, regulatory and UK-adoption case. Optune offers a sharper NHS-versus-private adoption contrast; RNS fits neural recording and closed-loop interests more closely.

## Product scope and mechanism

An ultrasound array focuses energy through the skull while MRI guides targeting and monitors heating. Thermal ablation creates a small lesion in a tremor-related brain target. This can avoid an implanted stimulation system, but the lesion is irreversible: it is not the reversible ultrasound neuromodulation being explored in other technologies.

## Clinical evidence and its limits

The original US PMA (2016) concerns unilateral thalamotomy for medication-refractory essential tremor in adults meeting its label criteria. Later Parkinson’s and bilateral indications are separate regulatory events. A 2025 UCL/NHNN methods paper explicitly reports ExAblate Neuro equipment in London, providing stronger local evidence than a generic focused-ultrasound service description.

## Detailed assessment of the five coursework topics

The labels below describe source readiness, not whether this topic has been fully researched. “Strong” can coexist with a small study or a narrow indication: the evidence is available to analyse, but its quality and applicability still need to be judged.

### 1. Clinical need and evidence — Strong starting evidence

Tremor-related disability, unsuccessful medication and selection between DBS, lesion procedures and no surgery provide a focused clinical problem. Candidate eligibility limits the market relative to all people with tremor.

**Why this grade:** Original regulatory evidence plus a London clinical methods paper support a well-bounded essential-tremor case.

[FDA-EXABLATE-P150038][FDA-EXABLATE-P150038] [EXABLATE-LONDON][EXABLATE-LONDON]

### 2. IP and invention origin — Partial / follow-up needed

Array control, skull correction, MR guidance and thermometry suggest a complex patent landscape. Identify first-version inventors, employers and core independent claims; these were not fully mapped here.

**Why this grade:** Technical records provide leads, but the early patent families, inventors and independent claims were not mapped.

### 3. Early funding — Material gap

Primary financing announcements exist, including $150m in 2024, but this is growth funding. Earlier device-development finance and valuations require additional historical work.

**Why this grade:** A later growth round is public; early device-development finance remains a selection-critical gap.

### 4. Regulatory path and manufacturing — Partial / follow-up needed

The PMA gives clinical and technical evidence. Keep the initial indication separate from subsequent expansions; verify production and supply-chain facts for the selected version.

**Why this grade:** PMA evidence is strong, but original versus expanded indications and supply-chain facts require further work.

[FDA-EXABLATE-P150038][FDA-EXABLATE-P150038]

### 5. Reimbursement and health economics — Strong starting evidence

NHS England’s November 2020 policy commissions MR-guided focused-ultrasound thalamotomy for a defined adult essential-tremor population unsuitable for DBS. It does not mean all uses are routinely funded.

**Why this grade:** A specific NHS England commissioning policy gives a concrete UK pathway and eligibility boundary.

UCLH’s service page supplies the Queen Square Imaging Centre route: treatment@queensquare.com, 020 3280 2472. These are public enquiry leads, not introductions or confirmations of availability. No contact has been made. An educational staff discussion may be more realistic than clinical observation; a local domain specialist is not automatically an exact-device user.

### Questions for a useful interview

1. Why do some patients qualify for focused ultrasound rather than DBS?
2. How do scanner time, skull eligibility, staffing and follow-up affect cost and throughput?
3. Which commissioning criteria determine access, and what evidence changed adoption?
