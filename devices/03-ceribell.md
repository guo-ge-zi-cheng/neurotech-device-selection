## Layer 1: Original research

Original snapshot: 19 October 2026, with earlier targeted additions on 20 October (ARC-EX was added on 20 October). The text and access labels below are preserved. Read Layer 2 for new evidence and dated updates.

### What does it do?

Ceribell is a bedside EEG system designed to make a brain recording available quickly in emergency and intensive care settings. The clinical problem is that a patient may have seizures without obvious convulsions, while a conventional EEG setup and specialist review take time. A rapidly applied scalp headband and portable recorder provide an earlier view of electrical activity.

There are several parts to keep separate. The headband and recorder acquire EEG. The Brain Stethoscope feature converts aspects of the signal into sound. Clarity-family software analyses seizure-related patterns and can assist assessment of seizure burden. These features have different development and clearance histories; evidence for one version does not automatically establish the performance of another.

The useful entrepreneurship story is the whole hospital workflow: who can put the headband on, how quickly a usable recording becomes available, how a clinician interprets it, and when conventional EEG is still needed. Reduced coverage and software assistance do not make it a complete substitute for every conventional EEG examination. Faster recording also does not by itself prove better survival or lower costs; those require their own evidence.

### How far has it developed?

Ceribell is a commercial EEG platform. The selected FDA document, K223504, concerns a later Status Epilepticus Monitor configuration. A report should trace the first hardware and sound-based system separately from subsequent AI/software versions.

Sources for this description and stage: [FDA • CERIBELL K223504](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cdrh_docs/pdf22/K223504.pdf); [Vespa et al.: DECIDE prospective observational study](https://ceribell.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/Vespa-online-CCM-DECIDE-Study-2020-1-4.pdf); [Wright et al.: rapid EEG emergency-care pilot](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34039642/); [Stanford: development of the brain stethoscope and Ceribell](https://neuroscience.stanford.edu/news/stanford-researchers-listen-silent-seizures-brain-stethoscope-turns-brain-waves-sound).

#### What does the clinical evidence actually show?

The DECIDE prospective observational study enrolled 181 critically ill patients and assessed workflow and physician decisions. It supports a rapid-access story, not a randomized mortality benefit. A separate 38-patient emergency-care pilot contained only one NCSE case: its nominal 100% sensitivity has a very wide confidence interval. K223504 reviews the Ceribell Status Epilepticus Monitor, so it should not be silently treated as the original headband clearance or every later Clarity version.

**Practical takeaway:** Useful public clinical and economic sources exist. The task is to choose the hardware/software version and reconstruct its early licensing and funding history.

## Layer 2: Additional research (20 October 2026)

The extra routes uncover real Stanford licence terms, academic seed support, early company proceeds and original hardware records. The major time-sensitive finding is that the seizure-monitor NTAP has ended for FY2027; a separate delirium award must not be confused with it.

**How to read this layer:** each finding states what the evidence supports, why it is usable, how it helps the report and where it stops. [Reliability protocol](../docs/reliability-protocol.md). “Context” is useful background, not a result for this product. No new interview has taken place.

### 1. Unmet medical need

**Search route:** Professional-society care-pathway evidence, not only the company’s rapid-EEG trials.

#### Why EEG access matters in critical care

**Context.** The 2015 ACNS consensus describes selected critically ill patients for whom continuous EEG is indicated and practical limits from specialist staffing and resources. It concerns adults and non-neonatal children, with indications and monitoring duration depending on the clinical situation.

**Why this evidence is usable:** This is the society’s own consensus, grounded in the evidence available then. It is not a randomised Ceribell evaluation.

**Use in the report:** Explain why non-obvious seizures and delays in obtaining conventional EEG create a problem. Use care-pathway evidence alongside the original rapid-EEG studies, which answer the device-specific question.

**Limit:** Cohort-specific seizure rates are not the prevalence in every ICU. The guidance does not establish that a short reduced-montage recording replaces full continuous EEG or improves outcomes.

Sources: [ACNS consensus: continuous EEG in critically ill adults and children, indications](https://www.acns.org/UserFiles/file/Consensus_Statement_on_Continuous_EEG_in.1.pdf).

**Where checked:** Introduction, methods, selected indications and Table 1. **Evidence route:** New source route. Checked 2026-10-20; source dates and review coverage are in the [source index](../docs/sources.md).

**Remaining gap:** Need a setting-specific patient denominator, contemporary access delays, full comparative clinical outcomes and observed cost data.

### 2. Intellectual property

**Search route:** Retrieve the university licence filed as a securities exhibit and its later extension.

#### A licence relationship with disclosed terms

**Documented fact.** A Stanford/Ceribell amendment effective 1 April 2017 traces the original agreement to 15 June 2015 and identifies Stanford dockets S11-220, S13-470 and 14-459 from the Parvizi/Chafe labs. It discloses a $36,000 first-commercial-sale milestone; royalty percentages are redacted. A June 2025 disclosure reports a $250,000 payment to extend exclusivity through the last licensed patent’s expiry.

**Why this evidence is usable:** These are dated contractual/company records filed publicly, not just a founder biography. The later filing establishes the extension event within its scope.

**Use in the report:** Explain academic technology transfer, licensed rights and commercial obligations. Link the dockets to patent families before discussing the independent claims already identified.

**Limit:** Not proof that all headband or later Clarity features fall under these patents. A licence payment is not fundraising, royalty rates remain unavailable, and no complete claim-to-product map was completed.

Sources: [Ceribell/Stanford licence amendment: disclosed technology and milestones](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1861107/000095017024100574/cbll-ex10_08.htm); [Ceribell: Stanford exclusivity extension](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1861107/000095017025088309/cbll-20250612.htm).

**Where checked:** Amendment technology/milestone schedule; June 2025 licence disclosure. **Evidence route:** New source route. Checked 2026-10-20; source dates and review coverage are in the [source index](../docs/sources.md).

**Remaining gap:** Map the docket families and independent claims to the original product, distinguish later AI IP and establish original inventor employment.

### 3. Fundraising

**Search route:** Follow university seed-grant records and early-financing notes inside the prospectus archive.

#### Academic support before the commercial rounds

**Documented fact.** Stanford Bio-X attributes Ceribell’s creation to a Round 6 (2012) IIP seed grant for the Parvizi/Chafe brain-wave sonification project.

**Why this evidence is usable:** The university’s own programme identifies the grant and investigators. It does not disclose the grant value.

**Use in the report:** Add the academic funding stage before company equity; it helps explain how the founding technology was developed.

**Limit:** No amount or proof that the grant alone financed the eventual commercial product. Academic seed support is not a company equity round.

Sources: [Stanford Bio-X: academic seed grant behind Ceribell](https://biox.stanford.edu/2019-seed-grants-news).

**Where checked:** 2019 seed-grant news: Ceribell project paragraph. **Evidence route:** New source route. Checked 2026-10-20; source dates and review coverage are in the [source index](../docs/sources.md).

#### Early company proceeds, with conversion kept separate

**Documented fact.** The August 2024 S-1 reports $1.0 million net Series Seed proceeds in August 2015/April 2016, $2.0 million of convertible-note cash in 2016, and $7.0 million net Series A proceeds in Q2 2017. The notes converted in May 2017.

### 4. Regulatory

**Search route:** Trace separate hardware decisions, then inspect manufacturing disclosures.

#### The original recorder is not the later AI product

**Documented fact.** FDA K170363 describes an eight-channel pocket EEG with visual/audio output; that version does not make automatic diagnostic conclusions or adverse-event alerts. K171459 covers the Class II EEG Headband, cleared in August 2017, for patients aged six and above in that label.

**Why this evidence is usable:** Original regulator records distinguish the components and intended uses. This is more precise than treating all Ceribell clearances as one indication.

**Use in the report:** Create a sequence from electrodes/recorder to later Clarity software. Explain which performance and regulatory evidence belongs to which component.

**Limit:** Do not apply the historical age limit or original non-AI scope to every current version. The full current clearance history still needs review.

Sources: [FDA K170363: original Ceribell pocket EEG device](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cdrh_docs/pdf17/K170363.pdf); [FDA K171459: Ceribell EEG Headband](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cdrh_docs/pdf17/K171459.pdf).

**Where checked:** Device descriptions and indications in the two 2017 records. **Evidence route:** New source route. Checked 2026-10-20; source dates and review coverage are in the [source index](../docs/sources.md).

#### The filing identifies assembly and sourcing locations

**Documented fact.** The 2024 filing describes headband manufacturing by third parties in China and recorder final assembly in Sunnyvale, California; it also identifies reliance on contract manufacturers.

**Why this evidence is usable:** The issuer describes its actual sourcing arrangement, rather than a supplier’s general capabilities.

**Use in the report:** Add a dated manufacturing/outsourcing account and explain supply-chain dependencies.

**Limit:** Historical 2024 disclosure, not a current site inspection or complete quality-system audit.

Sources: [Ceribell: August 2024 S-1 with exhibits, public issuer archive](https://ceribell.gcs-web.com/static-files/2d3fe925-344d-40d7-b32c-cfeb9261c271).

**Where checked:** Printed p31 and Manufacturing and Supply, pp119-120. **Evidence route:** Deeper archive extraction of a previously used source route. Checked 2026-10-20; source dates and review coverage are in the [source index](../docs/sources.md).

**Remaining gap:** Update manufacturing/QMS evidence and map all selected current software versions to the relevant decisions and standards.

### 5. Reimbursement

**Search route:** Check the current official rule rather than carry forward a temporary payment announcement.

#### Important update: seizure-monitor NTAP has expired

**Documented fact.** The FY2027 IPPS final rule discontinues the new technology add-on payment (NTAP) for the Ceribell Status Epilepticus Monitor. It separately approves a Ceribell Delirium Monitor System for FY2027; that is not a renewal of the seizure-monitor award.

**Why this evidence is usable:** This is the final government payment rule, read in the official HTML. It takes priority over older promotional descriptions of a time-limited add-on.

**Use in the report:** Explain temporary support versus ongoing hospital economics. Keep seizure and delirium indications separate in the payer and stakeholder discussion.

**Limit:** NTAP expiry does not eliminate all underlying hospital payment. No current Ceribell purchase price, hospital contract or universal insurer coverage was established. Treat older NTAP references in Layer 1 as historical.

Sources: [CMS FY2027 IPPS final rule: NTAP expiry and new technology decisions](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2026-08-04/html/2026-15833.htm).

**Where checked:** II.E.4, FR pp49694-49695; II.E.6, pp49742-49743. **Evidence route:** New source route. Checked 2026-10-20; source dates and review coverage are in the [source index](../docs/sources.md).

**Remaining gap:** Verify the chosen product’s current coding/base payment and real purchase or subscription terms; the economic model is not a contract.
