## Product scope and mechanism

A rapidly applied scalp electrode headband connects to a bedside recorder; clinicians can inspect EEG and use software assistance. Clarity-family algorithms analyse seizure-related patterns. The commercial value is not just classification accuracy: making EEG available promptly can change treatment while a conventional EEG team is unavailable.

## Clinical evidence and its limits

The DECIDE prospective observational study enrolled 181 critically ill patients and assessed workflow and physician decisions. It supports a rapid-access story, not a randomized mortality benefit. A separate 38-patient emergency-care pilot contained only one NCSE case: its nominal 100% sensitivity has a very wide confidence interval. K223504 reviews the Ceribell Status Epilepticus Monitor, so it should not be silently treated as the original headband clearance or every later Clarity version.

## Detailed assessment of the five coursework topics

The labels below describe source readiness, not whether this topic has been fully researched. “Strong” can coexist with a small study or a narrow indication: the evidence is available to analyse, but its quality and applicability still need to be judged.

### 1. Clinical need and evidence — Strong starting evidence

Quantify suspected non-convulsive seizures, delays to conventional EEG and the consequences of unnecessary or delayed anti-seizure treatment. Hospital workflow is the relevant unit of value.

**Why this grade:** Several original studies examine deployment and decisions; availability is strong even though designs have important limits.

### Questions for a useful interview

1. What delays occur in a London ICU before a conventional EEG is available?
2. Which clinical decisions can reduced-channel EEG support, and what still needs full EEG?
3. Who purchases the system and consumables, and where does any operational saving accrue?
