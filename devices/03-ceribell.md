# Rapid Response EEG / Clarity

**Ceribell** · [Back to the comparison](../README.md)

## What does it do?

Ceribell is a bedside EEG system designed to make a brain recording available quickly in emergency and intensive care settings. The clinical problem is that a patient may have seizures without obvious convulsions, while a conventional EEG setup and specialist review take time. A rapidly applied scalp headband and portable recorder provide an earlier view of electrical activity.

There are several parts to keep separate. The headband and recorder acquire EEG. The Brain Stethoscope feature converts aspects of the signal into sound. Clarity-family software analyses seizure-related patterns and can assist assessment of seizure burden. These features have different development and clearance histories; evidence for one version does not automatically establish the performance of another.

The useful entrepreneurship story is the whole hospital workflow: who can put the headband on, how quickly a usable recording becomes available, how a clinician interprets it, and when conventional EEG is still needed. Reduced coverage and software assistance do not make it a complete substitute for every conventional EEG examination. Faster recording also does not by itself prove better survival or lower costs; those require their own evidence.

## How far has it developed?

Ceribell is a commercial EEG platform. The selected FDA document, K223504, concerns a later Status Epilepticus Monitor configuration. A report should trace the first hardware and sound-based system separately from subsequent AI/software versions.

Sources for this description and stage: [FDA • CERIBELL K223504](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cdrh_docs/pdf22/K223504.pdf); [Vespa et al.: DECIDE prospective observational study](https://ceribell.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/Vespa-online-CCM-DECIDE-Study-2020-1-4.pdf); [Wright et al.: rapid EEG emergency-care pilot](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34039642/); [Stanford: development of the brain stethoscope and Ceribell](https://neuroscience.stanford.edu/news/stanford-researchers-listen-silent-seizures-brain-stethoscope-turns-brain-waves-sound).

### What does the clinical evidence actually show?

The DECIDE prospective observational study enrolled 181 critically ill patients and assessed workflow and physician decisions. It supports a rapid-access story, not a randomized mortality benefit. A separate 38-patient emergency-care pilot contained only one NCSE case: its nominal 100% sensitivity has a very wide confidence interval. K223504 reviews the Ceribell Status Epilepticus Monitor, so it should not be silently treated as the original headband clearance or every later Clarity version.

**Practical takeaway:** Useful public clinical and economic sources exist. The task is to choose the hardware/software version and reconstruct its early licensing and funding history.
