## Layer 1: Original research

Original snapshot: 20 October 2026. Terra was added after the six-device investigation; its initial access labels are retained below.

### What does it do?

MAGNETOM Terra is a 7 tesla magnetic resonance imaging scanner. It combines a powerful static magnet, gradient coils, radiofrequency hardware, dedicated imaging coils and software to produce detailed images. It is a diagnostic imaging platform, rather than a brain stimulation or neural recording interface. The useful product boundary includes installation, safety screening, image interpretation and service support, because a magnet alone cannot deliver a clinical decision.

The original US-cleared configuration supports head and extremity imaging in patients above 30 kg, with the appropriate coils; the submission includes a head coil and a knee coil. This makes neuroimaging and selected musculoskeletal imaging relevant, but does not establish all-body clinical use or every possible joint examination. The scanner has separate clinical and research modes. An experimental sequence or coil should not be treated as authorised clinical functionality merely because it runs on a Terra.

A typical pathway begins with a clinical or research question, eligibility and implant/safety checks, selection of an appropriate coil and protocol, acquisition, and specialist interpretation. The potential value is additional information that changes care after existing imaging. That needs to be demonstrated for a defined patient group. Terra.X and later upgrade features belong to later configurations, so a London facility must confirm what it actually operates before the team compares performance, workflow or payment.

### How far has it developed?

The original system received US 510(k) clearance K170840 on 12 October 2017, Class II under 21 CFR 892.1000. King’s lists an exact-model London installation. This review does not independently establish the original EU certificate, present UK documentation or the current local upgrade. Terra.X was announced as a later cleared generation in 2024.

#### What does the clinical evidence actually show?

An actual Terra-versus-Prisma study assessed diagnostic clarification in 53 selected patients with equivocal neurovascular findings. It supports a scoped question, with retrospective design, no independent invasive reference standard and no blinding to field strength. A separate focal-epilepsy source is a prospective protocol, not completed results. No Terra-specific musculoskeletal outcome/economic study was accepted in this search. FDA safety-limit testing must not be called a patient-benefit trial.

**Practical takeaway:** Strong regulatory/manufacturing records and a direct London equipment route make Terra worth considering. Original product IP, early development expenditure, local eligible volume and incremental value/payment remain harder.

### 1. Unmet medical need

**Search route:** Search original clinical studies for the exact scanner and comparator; distinguish completed findings from protocols.

#### A product-specific diagnostic question

**Study result.** A selected neurovascular cohort was scanned on Terra and Prisma; the design supports investigation of diagnostic uncertainty, with important reference-standard and blinding limits.

**Why this evidence is usable:** Original publisher full text identifies the scanner and method.

**Use in the report:** Define a narrow prior-care problem and critically assess incremental information.

**Limit:** No general accuracy, patient outcome, economic saving or London eligible volume established.

Sources: [Radojewski et al. Diagnosis of Small Unruptured Intracranial Aneurysms: Comparison of 7T versus 3T MRI](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00062-023-01282-2).

**Where checked:** Methods, Data Collection/Image Analysis; Discussion limitations. **Evidence route:** New primary-source route checked for Terra. Checked 2026-10-20; source dates and review coverage are in the [source index](../docs/sources.md).

#### Epilepsy as a planned comparison, not a result

**Context.** The published prospective protocol defines a nonlesional presurgical pathway with Terra/3T comparison.

**Why this evidence is usable:** Original protocol clearly separates plan from completed outcomes.

**Use in the report:** Show how an appropriate comparator and referral group could be investigated.

**Limit:** No diagnostic yield or treatment benefit can be taken from a protocol.

Sources: [Study protocol: value of 7-T MRI with prospective motion correction and postprocessing for patients with nonlesional epilepsy](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10309-023-00618-9).
