# ARC-EX System

**ONWARD Medical** · [Back to the comparison](../README.md)

## What does it do?

ARC-EX is an external spinal cord stimulation system used alongside rehabilitation to improve hand sensation and strength after selected cervical spinal cord injuries. Electrodes sit on the skin and connect to a programmable stimulator. It acts on spinal pathways rather than directly on the brain. It does not require a surgically implanted electrode and should be kept separate from ONWARD’s ARC-IM implant and ARC-BCI development programmes.

The treatment aims to make surviving neural pathways more responsive while a person practises useful movements. A rehabilitation professional selects the programme and combines stimulation with functional tasks such as reaching, grasping and manipulating objects. The product includes the stimulator, electrodes, control software and training/support workflow. The later home-use version lets a patient and, where needed, a helper use settings defined by the professional. It is not a stand-alone cure or a system that reads movement intentions from the brain.

For an engineering and business report, the useful unit of analysis is the whole rehabilitation pathway. Ask which patients can benefit, how many sessions and staff hours are needed, what equipment and consumables cost, and whether improvements remain useful outside sessions. These questions connect the technology to unmet need and payment. A nearby provider can explain that practical workflow, while company and university records are needed for the invention and financing history.

## How far has it developed?

A marketed device with original US De Novo authorization on 19 December 2024 (DEN240014) and a home-use extension cleared on 14 November 2025 (K251821). ONWARD announced CE marking in September 2025; its 2025 annual report says UK registration was completed. Exact-product UK provider listings are available. The original certificate/MHRA record and routine NHS commissioning were not independently verified here.

### What does the clinical evidence actually show?

The 2024 Up-LIFT paper describes a prospective, single-arm, sequential study: two months of rehabilitation alone followed by two months with ARC-EX added. Of 65 enrolled participants, 60 completed; 43/60 (71.7%, rounded to 72%) met the predefined combined strength-and-function responder endpoint. The safety population was 64, with no device-related serious adverse events reported. The trial was not randomized or sham-controlled, so cumulative training, treatment order and expectation remain relevant limits. The smaller UK Pathfinder2 pilot provides community-workflow evidence; its abstract reports no significant group-level change in ISNCSCI motor/sensory scores despite improvements in selected functional measures. It should not be presented as universal neurological recovery.

**Practical takeaway:** A useful addition for both public research and first-hand investigation: FDA records and a pivotal paper are accessible, and exact-device providers are near London. Product-specific patent mapping, earliest development spending and verified payment terms remain gaps.

## Finding information for the five report sections

### 1. Unmet medical need

**Access to useful information: Clear sources.**

**What we found:** The Up-LIFT paper defines the hand/arm rehabilitation problem and measures changes in the selected chronic-SCI group. The NSCISC 2026 sheet supplies public US population and severity-specific cost estimates.

**How close is it to this device?** Direct device trial plus broader US traumatic-SCI statistics; population totals are not the number eligible for ARC-EX.

**How it helps write this section:** Explain why hand function matters, define the eligible subgroup, quantify the broader burden and compare measured outcomes with rehabilitation alone.

**Still needed:** A justified estimate for eligible chronic incomplete C2-C8 patients and UK-specific care costs. The cost sheet’s historical model does not demonstrate savings caused by ARC-EX.

Useful sources: [Moritz et al.: Up-LIFT pivotal trial, Nature Medicine](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41591-024-02940-9); [NSCISC: 2026 traumatic spinal cord injury facts and figures](https://bpb-us-w2.wpmucdn.com/sites.uab.edu/dist/f/392/files/2026/03/Facts-and-Figures-English-03.25.2026-Accessible.pdf); [FDA: ARC-EX original De Novo classification and evidence review](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cdrh_docs/reviews/DEN240014.pdf).

**Still needed:** Original EU certificate, verified MHRA entry, applicable UK conditions and the present supplier/manufacturing arrangements. A clearance or registration does not establish NHS reimbursement.

### Jane Symonds / Neurokinex ARC-EX and Pathfinder2 team — Hemel Hempstead and Gatwick; outside London

**Why this person/team is included:** Symonds is a named Pathfinder2 author with a Neurokinex Hemel Hempstead affiliation. Neurokinex identifies its ARC-EX study, and ONWARD lists its Hemel Hempstead and Gatwick centres for the exact product.

**What they could help us investigate:** Ask about patient selection, session structure, therapist training, equipment/consumable costs, outcome measurement and how community rehabilitation is funded. **What their connection does not establish:** The 2025 paper gives a historical affiliation, not a verified current appointment.
