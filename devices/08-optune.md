## Product scope and mechanism

Scalp arrays deliver alternating electric fields intended to disrupt dividing tumour cells. The portable system is used over prolonged daily periods, making adherence, skin effects, support and everyday acceptability part of its value proposition. These Tumor Treating Fields are anti-tumour treatment, not EEG recording or stimulation intended to alter neural signalling.

## Clinical evidence and its limits

The product lineage matters: the original NovoTTF-100A recurrent-glioblastoma pathway, the 2015 PMA supplement for newly diagnosed glioblastoma with temozolomide after standard initial treatment, and the later Optune Gio branding should be traced separately. This brief concerns brain-tumour use; do not borrow indications for Novocure devices used in other cancers. FDA evidence and the company’s public prospectus provide an unusually substantial starting set.

## Detailed assessment of the five coursework topics

The labels below describe source readiness, not whether this topic has been fully researched. “Strong” can coexist with a small study or a narrow indication: the evidence is available to analyse, but its quality and applicability still need to be judged.

### 1. Clinical need and evidence — Strong starting evidence

Glioblastoma has a clear unmet need, but benefits and burdens should be expressed for the selected treatment setting and comparator. Compare survival, daily use and quality of life together.

**Why this grade:** PMA evidence and a substantial public business history support a clearly bounded glioblastoma case.

### 2. IP and invention origin — Partial / follow-up needed

The 2015 S-1 describes Yoram Palti, the early company and a Technion-related settlement. This is a useful origin/ownership trail, not a finding of infringement. Map actual independent claims and assignments.

**Why this grade:** The S-1 provides an early inventor/ownership trail, but patent claims and assignments need a complete map.

### 3. Early funding — Partial / follow-up needed

IPO and later filings make historical financing more inspectable than at many private firms. Earliest grants, investors and device-specific development costs still need reconstruction.

**Why this grade:** Filings expose financing history; original grants and device-specific costs still need reconstruction.

### 4. Regulatory path and manufacturing — Partial / follow-up needed

PMA records show a versioned evidence path. Trace the generator/array supply chain and approval history rather than treating the present brand as the first device.

**Why this grade:** Versioned PMA records exist; distinguish original recurrent disease, later indications and current branding.

### 5. Reimbursement and health economics — Strong starting evidence

A particularly valuable UK contrast: NICE NG99 recommendation 1.2.26 advises against offering TTF for newly diagnosed GBM, while Imperial documents private-care experience. Private use and trials do not establish routine NHS funding.

**Why this grade:** NICE recommendations and documented private-care experience provide a useful UK adoption/payment contrast.

### Questions for a useful interview

1. What prevented broader NHS adoption despite clinical evidence?
2. How do patients manage daily use, skin care and adherence?
3. Which early IP and financing decisions turned the original concept into a viable treatment service?
