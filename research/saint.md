# Magnus Medical — SAINT: what we can write, and what is missing

Magnus Medical’s SAINT combines MRI-informed targeting with an accelerated schedule of transcranial magnetic stimulation for depression. The selected case is the integrated system covered by the original US clearance, rather than accelerated TMS generally. There is a small controlled trial and useful patent and fundraising material; durability, the full delivery cost and actual local payment still need investigation.

This guide works through the five report sections for a teammate deciding what investigation is still needed. Each question explains the requirement, the actual evidence, the conclusion it supports, the unanswered part and a way to investigate it. Evidence limitations are explained in sentences rather than assigned a grade.

[Device-by-device overview](README.md) · [The shared requirements](../docs/report-evidence-requirements.md) · [What Imperial access actually recovered](../docs/imperial-gap-searches.md).

The evidence comes from the existing 19–21 October research. This revision clarifies that evidence; it adds no new retrievals. The next investigations below are proposals unless the access log explicitly records otherwise.

## Unmet medical need

### Who could actually receive this device? (N1)

- **What the report needs to explain:** Explain the condition and the specific patients or procedures the device is intended for. Then give the number who meet those criteria in a named country and year. Explain why a count of everyone with the disease would include people who cannot receive this device.
- **What the sources actually tell us:** The original clearance concerns adults with major depressive disorder that did not improve adequately with medication. The early trial included 29 participants. Source: [FDA • SAINT K220177](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cdrh_docs/pdf22/K220177.pdf); [WHO: Depressive disorder (depression)](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/depression). Relevant passage: Indications; study population; WHO context.
- **What this lets us write:** This defines a more specific group than everyone with depression. The trial sample is not a count of people eligible in a London service.
- **What we cannot yet answer:** We need country- and year-specific eligible numbers and the exclusions applied by the relevant treatment service.
- **Next investigation:** Find a population study or service record that applies the device's actual eligibility criteria, and record the country, year and exclusions. Start with [Clinical literature](../docs/imperial-gap-searches.md#lit); the linked access log states what was actually tried and any access limits.

### Where would the device enter existing care? (N2)

- **What the report needs to explain:** Describe what happens to a patient before, during and after the relevant treatment or investigation. Identify the decision the device changes, the usual alternative and the staff or equipment needed at that point.
- **What the sources actually tell us:** SAINT combines MRI-informed targeting and navigation with treatment over five days. A UK comparator study used 20 sessions spread across four to six weeks. Source: [FDA • SAINT K220177](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cdrh_docs/pdf22/K220177.pdf); [Morriss et al.: BRIGhTMIND RCT, NIHR EME 12(2)](https://njl-admin.nihr.ac.uk/document/download/2047157). Relevant passage: Protocol; BRIGhTMIND abstract.
- **What this lets us write:** This makes a concrete difference in delivery schedules visible. The UK regimen is a comparator, not evidence that SAINT is already used or funded in that pathway.
- **What we cannot yet answer:** We need the actual previous-care pathway, access barriers and retreatment arrangements for the selected patient group.
- **Next investigation:** Find a guideline or actual service description that traces the relevant patient pathway and the decision where this device would enter. Start with [Clinical literature](../docs/imperial-gap-searches.md#lit); the linked access log states what was actually tried and any access limits.

### What specifically goes wrong with existing care? (N3)

- **What the report needs to explain:** Show a measured problem with the care available before the device: for example, continuing symptoms, missed diagnoses, waiting time or patients unable to access treatment. Explain how often it happens. A later trial of the new device does not by itself measure the original care shortfall.
- **What the sources actually tell us:** The early trial reports remission in 11 of 14 active patients and 2 of 15 sham patients within a month. Source: [FDA • SAINT K220177](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cdrh_docs/pdf22/K220177.pdf). Relevant passage: Clinical study narrative/rows.
- **What this lets us write:** This measures a later short-term treatment effect. It does not quantify how frequently the existing pathway failed or how many local patients were waiting for effective treatment.
- **What we cannot yet answer:** We need baseline treatment-failure and delay data and evidence of the continuing unmet need in the target service.
- **Next investigation:** Find a study of the existing pathway that measures the failure or access problem, preferably from the period when development began. Start with [Clinical literature](../docs/imperial-gap-searches.md#lit); the linked access log states what was actually tried and any access limits.

### What are the consequences of that problem? (N4)

- **What the report needs to explain:** Explain how the care shortfall affects patients and services. Connect it to outcomes, staff time, hospital use or costs, and identify which groups carry that burden. A large disease-cost figure needs a reasoned connection to the patients and problem this device addresses.
- **What the sources actually tell us:** The protocol includes fifty ten-minute stimulation sessions, giving 500 minutes of active stimulation. Source: [FDA • SAINT K220177](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cdrh_docs/pdf22/K220177.pdf). Relevant passage: Treatment schedule.
- **What this lets us write:** This quantifies one resource component. It excludes MRI, targeting, intervals between sessions, other staff work and the patient's total attendance burden.
- **What we cannot yet answer:** We need total patient and staff time, imaging and follow-up resources, and measured costs or consequences before and after treatment.
- **Next investigation:** Find measured patient consequences and resource use for the relevant subgroup, then identify the price year and setting of any cost estimate. Start with [Costs and health-service evidence](../docs/imperial-gap-searches.md#cost); the linked access log states what was actually tried and any access limits.

### What benefit has this particular device demonstrated? (N5)

- **What the report needs to explain:** Explain how the device could change the identified problem and what has actually been measured. Distinguish a technical result, a change in a clinical decision and an improvement patients experience. State the study population, comparison and follow-up so the reader can see how far the finding reaches.
- **What the sources actually tell us:** The small sham-controlled trial reports 11 of 14 active patients in remission versus 2 of 15 controls within a month. Source: [FDA • SAINT K220177](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cdrh_docs/pdf22/K220177.pdf). Relevant passage: System description and clinical evidence.
- **What this lets us write:** It supports a short-term proposition for that protocol. The study had 29 participants and stopped at an interim analysis, so it cannot establish broad implementation performance or durable benefit.
- **What we cannot yet answer:** We need longer follow-up, retreatment rates, outcomes in routine delivery and a fuller harm assessment.
- **Next investigation:** Read the original device study for its comparator, measured endpoints, follow-up, adverse events and limitations; look for the missing patient outcome rather than another mechanism description. Start with [Clinical literature](../docs/imperial-gap-searches.md#lit); the linked access log states what was actually tried and any access limits.

### What other options could meet the same need? (N6)

- **What the report needs to explain:** Compare options available to the same patients at the same point in care. Explain their different benefits, risks and practical demands. A product used as a regulatory comparison is not automatically the most appropriate clinical alternative.
- **What the sources actually tell us:** BRIGhTMIND randomised 255 UK patients between other guided stimulation regimens. Source: [Morriss et al.: BRIGhTMIND RCT, NIHR EME 12(2)](https://njl-admin.nihr.ac.uk/document/download/2047157). Relevant passage: Abstract, PDF pp5–6.
- **What this lets us write:** This can describe relevant UK alternatives and service context. The regimens differ from SAINT, and the study is not a head-to-head SAINT comparison.
- **What we cannot yet answer:** We need a comparison with the alternatives available to the same local patients, including conventional TMS or ECT where appropriate to their treatment decision.
- **Next investigation:** Find alternatives for the same patient group and compare their eligibility, outcomes, risks and service requirements. Start with [Clinical literature](../docs/imperial-gap-searches.md#lit); the linked access log states what was actually tried and any access limits.

## Intellectual property

### Who made the original invention, and where? (I1)

- **What the report needs to explain:** Identify the inventors of the technology that became the selected device, their employers when the invention arose and the earliest relevant publication or patent priority. A current university biography is not evidence of where an inventor worked at that earlier date.
- **What the sources actually tell us:** Stanford's technology-transfer account identifies invention in the Williams laboratory. The selected patent names Williams and Sudheimer and has January 2018 priority. Source: [Stanford OTL FY2022 annual report: SAINT commercialisation](https://otl.stanford.edu/sites/g/files/sbiybj16766/files/media/file/stanford-office-of-technology-licensing_fy-2022-annual-report_final.pdf); [US10595735B2: personalized accelerated theta-burst stimulation](https://patents.google.com/patent/US10595735B2/en). Relevant passage: OTL PDF p11; metadata.
- **What this lets us write:** This gives an academic origin and specific inventors to investigate. The institutional story does not by itself verify each inventor's employment at the relevant date.
- **What we cannot yet answer:** We need contemporaneous employer evidence and confirmation of the invention's relationship to the first commercial version.
- **Next investigation:** Trace the original inventors and predecessor organisations using patent priority records and contemporaneous employment or university records. Start with [Patent and rights investigation](../docs/imperial-gap-searches.md#pat); the linked access log states what was actually tried and any access limits.

### How did the company obtain the right to use it? (I2)

- **What the report needs to explain:** Explain who originally owned the invention and how rights reached the developer through assignment, acquisition or licence. Identify the records supporting each step. A shared founder, research collaboration or patent applicant does not establish every transfer or licence term.
- **What the sources actually tell us:** Stanford is the patent assignee, and Magnus identifies patents it has licensed. Source: [US10595735B2: personalized accelerated theta-burst stimulation](https://patents.google.com/patent/US10595735B2/en); [Magnus identifies licensed Stanford patents and describes IP enforcement](https://www.magnusmedical.com/press-release/magnus-medical-saint-ip-protection/). Relevant passage: Assignee; licensed-patent statement.
- **What this lets us write:** This supports a specific licensor–developer relationship. It does not disclose the whole contract, its field, exclusivity or economic terms.
- **What we cannot yet answer:** We need the relevant licence and assignment records and the rights granted for the selected system, keeping unavailable financial terms explicit.
- **Next investigation:** Retrieve the actual assignment or licence record and follow the original owner's rights into the developer, leaving unavailable contractual terms explicit. Start with [Patent and rights investigation](../docs/imperial-gap-searches.md#pat); the linked access log states what was actually tried and any access limits.

### Which patent claims correspond to the device's features? (I3)

- **What the report needs to explain:** Explain each relevant independent claim in ordinary language, then compare its required features with documented features of the selected product. An independent claim sets out a combination of features without relying on another claim. Identify any feature that cannot yet be matched.
- **What the sources actually tell us:** Independent claims 1 and 19 cover intermittent stimulation variants; claims 10 and 24 cover continuous variants with specified schedules or intervals. Source: [US10595735B2: personalized accelerated theta-burst stimulation](https://patents.google.com/patent/US10595735B2/en). Relevant passage: Independent claims 1, 10, 19, 24.
- **What this lets us write:** We can explain the distinct technical combinations. The cleared intermittent theta-burst use should not be treated as though every claimed variant is part of the commercial protocol.
- **What we cannot yet answer:** We need a feature-by-feature match to the cleared system and a separate account of any later improvements or different claimed schedules.
- **Next investigation:** Compare the exact publication's independent claims with technical drawings or instructions for the selected product version, one required feature at a time. Start with [Patent and rights investigation](../docs/imperial-gap-searches.md#pat); the linked access log states what was actually tried and any access limits.

### Where and when does that protection apply? (I4)

- **What the report needs to explain:** Identify the relevant patent family, priority date and countries, and check the dated status of the particular application or granted patent. An application, an expired patent and a live grant answer different questions. Later amendments may change the claims.
- **What the sources actually tell us:** The selected US grant has a 2018 priority date. Source: [US10595735B2: personalized accelerated theta-burst stimulation](https://patents.google.com/patent/US10595735B2/en). Relevant passage: Bibliographic metadata.
- **What this lets us write:** This identifies one patent text and its place in the chronology. It does not establish the current status of all relevant continuations or national family members.
- **What we cannot yet answer:** We need official dated status and the relevant family and continuation records in each market discussed.
- **Next investigation:** Check the correct family and national patent registers for dated status, granted text and amendments rather than relying only on an aggregator's summary. Start with [Patent and rights investigation](../docs/imperial-gap-searches.md#pat); the linked access log states what was actually tried and any access limits.

### What else can the company protect or control? (I5)

- **What the report needs to explain:** Look for documented software rights, designs, trademarks, confidential know-how or data rights that matter to the device. Explain what the company controls and why it could matter commercially. Owning a factory does not by itself establish that a particular process is legally protected.
- **What the sources actually tell us:** The commercial pathway combines targeting software, navigation and stimulation hardware. Source: [FDA • SAINT K220177](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cdrh_docs/pdf22/K220177.pdf). Relevant passage: System description.
- **What this lets us write:** Integration creates questions about software, data and know-how control. The presence of those components is not evidence of who owns every non-patent right.
- **What we cannot yet answer:** We need documented ownership or licence arrangements for the relevant software, data, branding and confidential know-how.
- **Next investigation:** Look for an explicit company or contractual description of the relevant software, know-how, design or data rights and who controls them. Start with [Company and financing history](../docs/imperial-gap-searches.md#fin); the linked access log states what was actually tried and any access limits.

### What could limit that control or allow competition? (I6)

- **What the report needs to explain:** Identify relevant third-party rights, licences on which the developer depends and possible competing designs. Explain what the available records let us say about competition. Our search does not establish a legal right to commercialise every feature or prove freedom from infringement.
- **What the sources actually tell us:** Magnus has made company statements about IP enforcement. Source: [Magnus identifies licensed Stanford patents and describes IP enforcement](https://www.magnusmedical.com/press-release/magnus-medical-saint-ip-protection/). Relevant passage: IP enforcement statement.
- **What this lets us write:** We can report the interested party's account as such. It is not a verified court judgment or proof that the system has no third-party-rights exposure.
- **What we cannot yet answer:** We need the actual proceedings and relevant competitor, supplier and licence records before drawing conclusions about enforceable competitive control.
- **Next investigation:** Investigate specific overlapping claims and licence dependencies; use technical alternatives to frame the unresolved question rather than declaring legal clearance. Start with [Patent and rights investigation](../docs/imperial-gap-searches.md#pat); the linked access log states what was actually tried and any access limits.

## Fundraising

### Was early research supported by grants? (F1)

- **What the report needs to explain:** For each relevant grant, establish who received it, the award identifier, dates, amount and purpose. Explain whether it supported a precursor laboratory, the company or this product. An acknowledgement in a paper can identify a lead without revealing the money attributable to the device.
- **What the sources actually tell us:** Stanford names a BBRF Young Investigator Award and Schwab and Chao philanthropic support in the precursor research history. Source: [Stanford: 2021 SNT study and academic funders](https://med.stanford.edu/news/all-news/2021/10/depression-treatment). Relevant passage: Funding acknowledgements.
- **What this lets us write:** These identify real research-funding leads. They do not establish the amount that supported this invention or money received by Magnus.
- **What we cannot yet answer:** We need award identifiers, recipients, dates, amounts and a defensible allocation to the relevant early work.
- **Next investigation:** Trace the acknowledgement to an authoritative award record and check its recipient, period, amount, purpose and relationship to this device. Start with [Awards and original research support](../docs/imperial-gap-searches.md#grant); the linked access log states what was actually tried and any access limits.

### What money did investors actually provide? (F2)

- **What the report needs to explain:** Reconstruct the relevant early investment: who invested, when it closed, the currency, amount, type of security and any disclosed valuation. Separate money offered from money sold or received, and distinguish a company fundraising total from money allocated to this product.
- **What the sources actually tell us:** The October 2021 launch announcement reports a $25 million Series A co-led by JAZZ and Red Tree. Source: [Magnus Medical: launch and $25m Series A](https://www.magnusmedical.com/press-release/magnus-medical-launch/). Relevant passage: Financing paragraph.
- **What this lets us write:** This establishes a disclosed company financing event. The source's header and body date differ, and neither establishes all closing terms or an original device budget.
- **What we cannot yet answer:** We need the exact closing, instruments, disclosed valuation and any earlier company financing.
- **Next investigation:** Recover the original transaction or filing and check the closing, investors, instrument, proceeds and valuation basis; keep undisclosed fields separate. Start with [Company and financing history](../docs/imperial-gap-searches.md#fin); the linked access log states what was actually tried and any access limits.

### What other resources helped development? (F3)

- **What the report needs to explain:** Investigate founder contributions, loans, convertible instruments, partner support, incubator facilities and in-kind resources. Explain what was actually provided and under what arrangement. Do not count an acquisition the company paid for as investment received by the company.
- **What the sources actually tell us:** The academic philanthropic support is distinct from the company's equity round. Source: [Stanford: 2021 SNT study and academic funders](https://med.stanford.edu/news/all-news/2021/10/depression-treatment). Relevant passage: Support acknowledgements.
- **What this lets us write:** The report can explain support to precursor research without counting it as Magnus cash. Other company resources have not been reconstructed.
- **What we cannot yet answer:** We need evidence of founder contributions, loans, in-kind resources or supplier support and the terms under which they were provided.
- **Next investigation:** Look for the relevant founder, debt, partner or acquisition record and distinguish cash received, cash spent and non-cash support. Start with [Company and financing history](../docs/imperial-gap-searches.md#fin); the linked access log states what was actually tried and any access limits.

### What development work was the money intended to fund? (F4)

- **What the report needs to explain:** Connect the funding to the next prototype, test, trial, manufacturing step or market-entry milestone. State whether the source reports an intention, an allocated budget or actual expenditure. A round announcement alone does not show how the company divided its money.
- **What the sources actually tell us:** The launch release connects the capital to commercial development and Stanford licensing. Source: [Magnus Medical: launch and $25m Series A](https://www.magnusmedical.com/press-release/magnus-medical-launch/). Relevant passage: Development/licence paragraphs.
- **What this lets us write:** This describes intended use of the money. It does not allocate actual spending to imaging, integration, validation, clinical work or launch.
- **What we cannot yet answer:** We need attributable milestone budgets or expenditure records for those development tasks.
- **Next investigation:** Find the stated use of proceeds or project allocation and connect it to a named development milestone without treating the announcement as an invoice. Start with [Company and financing history](../docs/imperial-gap-searches.md#fin); the linked access log states what was actually tried and any access limits.

### What was delivered after the funding? (F5)

- **What the report needs to explain:** Place funding events and documented development outputs on the same timeline. Identify evidence of a prototype, study, regulatory decision or first sale. Timing can show that funding preceded an outcome, but additional evidence is needed to claim the funding paid for that outcome.
- **What the sources actually tell us:** The company launched in 2021 and received the original clearance in September 2022. Source: [Magnus Medical: launch and $25m Series A](https://www.magnusmedical.com/press-release/magnus-medical-launch/); [FDA • SAINT K220177](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cdrh_docs/pdf22/K220177.pdf). Relevant passage: Launch month; decision date.
- **What this lets us write:** This supports a development timeline. It does not show that the announced round alone paid for, or caused, every step required for clearance.
- **What we cannot yet answer:** We need evidence connecting funded work to specific integration, validation or clinical outputs.
- **Next investigation:** Find a dated output and a source linking the relevant resources to that work; distinguish the documented sequence from a causal funding claim. Start with [Company and financing history](../docs/imperial-gap-searches.md#fin); the linked access log states what was actually tried and any access limits.

### What would early development have cost if the budget is private? (F6)

- **What the report needs to explain:** Build a reasoned estimate using both approaches allowed by the specification. Add up sourced staffing, engineering, testing, trial and manufacturing costs from the bottom up, and compare with similar programmes from the top down. State assumptions and ranges. This estimates capital needed; it does not reconstruct undisclosed fundraising.
- **What the sources actually tell us:** No defensible numerical estimate of early required capital has been completed.
- **What this lets us write:** The report cannot replace that estimate with the company round or combine academic and corporate costs without a basis.
- **What we cannot yet answer:** We need MRI and software-development inputs, integration staff, supplier and trial costs, and comparable programmes for both estimation approaches.
- **Next investigation:** Source staff time, prototype, testing, trial and production inputs, then cross-check the range against genuinely comparable development programmes. Start with [Capital estimate inputs](../docs/imperial-gap-searches.md#est); the linked access log states what was actually tried and any access limits.

## Regulatory

### Exactly which product and use are being regulated? (R1)

- **What the report needs to explain:** Define the hardware, software and accessories, the intended patients, user and care setting. Identify the original version being studied and separate later changes. This prevents evidence about a component or research system being applied to a different complete product.
- **What the sources actually tell us:** The selected system integrates MRI-informed targeting, navigation, stimulation and software. Source: [FDA • SAINT K220177](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cdrh_docs/pdf22/K220177.pdf). Relevant passage: System/intended use.
- **What this lets us write:** This lets us define the regulated product rather than calling any accelerated TMS clinic a SAINT service.
- **What we cannot yet answer:** We need the complete configuration and the later change history for whichever system version the report describes.
- **Next investigation:** Retrieve the selected version's instructions and decision documents, then list the exact configuration, users, setting and intended use. Start with [Official records first](../docs/imperial-gap-searches.md#open); the linked access log states what was actually tried and any access limits.

### Why did it follow this regulatory route? (R2)

- **What the report needs to explain:** For each market discussed, explain the device classification and the applicable rule or product code, then connect the route to the device's function and risks. Evidence of a US route does not establish UK or EU authorisation.
- **What the sources actually tell us:** The original US route is Class II repetitive TMS under 21 CFR 882.5805, using a 510(k) comparison. Source: [FDA • SAINT K220177](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cdrh_docs/pdf22/K220177.pdf). Relevant passage: Classification/predicate.
- **What this lets us write:** We can relate the system's function to its US regulatory route. The record does not establish UK or EU authorisation.
- **What we cannot yet answer:** We need primary records for any non-US market-access claim, including the exact configuration and intended use covered.
- **Next investigation:** Use the exact market's regulator or conformity records to establish the classification and route, explaining their connection to the device's function. Start with [Official records first](../docs/imperial-gap-searches.md#open); the linked access log states what was actually tried and any access limits.

### What actual decision allowed market entry? (R3)

- **What the report needs to explain:** Give the decision identifier, date, type and authorised scope from the original record. Distinguish clearance, approval, registration and a manufacturer's market announcement. Check later changes separately if the report makes claims about the present product.
- **What the sources actually tell us:** K220177 records clearance on 1 September 2022. Source: [FDA • SAINT K220177](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cdrh_docs/pdf22/K220177.pdf). Relevant passage: Decision letter.
- **What this lets us write:** This provides the original US market-entry decision and a traceable starting point for the evidence account.
- **What we cannot yet answer:** We need any later relevant decisions and a complete current label before extending the original decision to later configurations or indications.
- **Next investigation:** Follow the original decision to any relevant supplements or later clearances, keeping each version and indication separate. Start with [Official records first](../docs/imperial-gap-searches.md#open); the linked access log states what was actually tried and any access limits.

### What tests and standards supported that decision? (R4)

- **What the report needs to explain:** Connect the device's specific risks to the tests or standards reported in its regulatory record. Identify the edition where available and what the test showed. A general standard explains an expectation; it does not prove the manufacturer performed or passed that test.
- **What the sources actually tell us:** The summary addresses software and risk management, electrical and electromagnetic performance, usability and biological evaluation. Source: [FDA • SAINT K220177](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cdrh_docs/pdf22/K220177.pdf). Relevant passage: Testing; IEC 62304, ISO 14971/10993.
- **What this lets us write:** This connects tests to the risks of integrated targeting and delivery. It is not the complete submission or every subsequent conformity record.
- **What we cannot yet answer:** We need exact standard editions, reported results and the evidence applicable to the selected present configuration.
- **Next investigation:** Read the device's test summary and the exact historical standard where accessible; record the reported test result and which supporting details remain private. Start with [Standards](../docs/imperial-gap-searches.md#std); the linked access log states what was actually tried and any access limits.

### Who made the device and took responsibility for quality? (R5)

- **What the report needs to explain:** Identify the legal manufacturer, important contract manufacturers or suppliers, production sites and who controlled assembly, testing and release. Distinguish a documented relationship from a supplier that merely has relevant capabilities. State the date of the manufacturing account.
- **What the sources actually tell us:** MagVenture confirms a role supplying MagPro equipment and providing installation or service support. Source: [MagVenture: supplying the magnetic stimulation system for SAINT](https://magventure.com/magventure-announces-collaboration-with-magnus-medical/). Relevant passage: Collaboration statement.
- **What this lets us write:** This establishes a specific counterparty relationship. It does not explain the complete division of legal manufacturing, quality control and system release.
- **What we cannot yet answer:** We need the responsibilities of Magnus, MagVenture and other relevant suppliers, including who releases the complete system.
- **Next investigation:** Trace the actual supplier or manufacturing agreement and responsibility split, then verify which sites and arrangements apply to the selected period. Start with [Company and financing history](../docs/imperial-gap-searches.md#fin); the linked access log states what was actually tried and any access limits.

### What obligations and problems continued after launch? (R6)

- **What the report needs to explain:** Explain the relevant quality and surveillance duties and investigate product-specific recalls or reported incidents. State the database, model, terms and date searched. A general quality rule does not establish company compliance, and a missing search result does not establish that no problem occurred.
- **What the sources actually tell us:** The general QMSR source explains US quality obligations. Source: [FDA. Medical Devices; Quality System Regulation Amendments. 89 FR 7496-7525, document 2024-01709](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2024-02-02/html/2024-01709.htm). Relevant passage: Scope/effective date.
- **What this lets us write:** It can explain the regulatory environment, but cannot verify SAINT's compliance or determine whether its components have relevant incidents.
- **What we cannot yet answer:** We need dated product-specific recall and incident searches and evidence about the actual continuing quality and surveillance duties.
- **Next investigation:** Run and document the exact-model recall and incident searches, and distinguish reported events, proven causes and general quality obligations. Start with [Official records first](../docs/imperial-gap-searches.md#open); the linked access log states what was actually tried and any access limits.

## Reimbursement

### Who buys what, and at what price? (P1)

- **What the report needs to explain:** Describe the actual sale, lease, subscription or consumable arrangement, including the configuration, date and price. Separate the manufacturer's selling price from a hospital charge, a service payment or a modelled treatment cost.
- **What the sources actually tell us:** The treatment involves imaging, targeting, navigation, stimulation and follow-up, but no verified complete system or treatment-package price was obtained. Source: [FDA • SAINT K220177](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cdrh_docs/pdf22/K220177.pdf). Relevant passage: Delivery protocol.
- **What this lets us write:** We can identify cost-bearing activities. We cannot price the whole pathway from a stimulation-session figure or another clinic's different TMS package.
- **What we cannot yet answer:** We need the actual buyer, hardware and software terms and a dated price specifying what the complete package includes.
- **Next investigation:** Find a dated quotation, tender or contract for the selected configuration and clarify what equipment, service and consumables the amount includes. Start with [Actual service and purchasing evidence](../docs/imperial-gap-searches.md#service); the linked access log states what was actually tried and any access limits.

### Who pays for the patient's care? (P2)

- **What the report needs to explain:** Identify whether the relevant care is funded by a commissioner, insurer, hospital, patient, research grant or charity. Connect that source to a real setting. The perspective chosen by an economic model does not prove that the named payer actually funds the device.
- **What the sources actually tell us:** The reviewed US inpatient add-on policy identifies one payment setting. Source: [CMS FY2027 IPPS final rule: NTAP expiry and new technology decisions](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2026-08-04/html/2026-15833.htm). Relevant passage: SAINT final-policy paragraph.
- **What this lets us write:** It provides a specific funding-policy context. It does not establish outpatient, private or NHS payment for a complete treatment episode.
- **What we cannot yet answer:** We need the actual payer and funding arrangement for the service and setting the report chooses.
- **Next investigation:** Find the actual commissioning, insurance or service-funding arrangement and identify the patient episode and budget it covers. ### Under what rules and amounts is care paid for? (P3)

- **What the report needs to explain:** For a named payer, place, setting and year, separate billing codes, clinical coverage conditions and payment amounts. - **What the report needs to explain:** Trace each proposed saving or health gain to evidence about an outcome or event frequency. - **What the sources actually tell us:** The small short-term trial does not measure the long-term relapse and retreatment parameters required by a sustained-savings argument.
