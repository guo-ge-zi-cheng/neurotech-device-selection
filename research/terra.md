# Siemens Healthineers — MAGNETOM Terra: what we can write, and what is missing

Siemens Healthineers’ MAGNETOM Terra is a 7-tesla MRI platform, studied here within the original head and extremity imaging scope. The original system must be separated from Terra.X and later upgrades. Regulatory and subsystem records, a neurovascular comparison and exact equipment at King’s support investigation; original scanner IP, attributable development capital and the economics of clinical use are still incomplete.

This guide works through the five report sections for a teammate deciding what investigation is still needed. Each question explains the requirement, the actual evidence, the conclusion it supports, the unanswered part and a way to investigate it. Evidence limitations are explained in sentences rather than assigned a grade.

[Device-by-device overview](README.md) · [The shared requirements](../docs/report-evidence-requirements.md) · [What Imperial access actually recovered](../docs/imperial-gap-searches.md).

The evidence comes from the existing 19–21 October research. This revision clarifies that evidence; it adds no new retrievals. The next investigations below are proposals unless the access log explicitly records otherwise.

## Unmet medical need

### Who could actually receive this device? (N1)

- **What the report needs to explain:** Explain the condition and the specific patients or procedures the device is intended for. Then give the number who meet those criteria in a named country and year. Explain why a count of everyone with the disease would include people who cannot receive this device.
- **What the sources actually tell us:** The original US record limits Terra to head and extremity imaging in patients above 30 kg. The neurovascular study selected 53 patients with uncertain findings. Source: [FDA. MAGNETOM Terra: K170840 clearance and 510(k) summary](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cdrh_docs/pdf17/K170840.pdf); [Radojewski et al. Diagnosis of Small Unruptured Intracranial Aneurysms: Comparison of 7T versus 3T MRI](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00062-023-01282-2). Relevant passage: Indication; Methods.
- **What this lets us write:** This defines a particular imaging scope and research group. It does not make the entire MRI or neurological-disease population eligible for the same proposed benefit.
- **What we cannot yet answer:** We need annual eligible local referrals and exclusions for a chosen use, with a separate population and rationale for musculoskeletal imaging.
- **Next investigation:** Find a population study or service record that applies the device's actual eligibility criteria, and record the country, year and exclusions. Start with [Clinical literature](../docs/imperial-gap-searches.md#lit); the linked access log states what was actually tried and any access limits.

### Where would the device enter existing care? (N2)

- **What the report needs to explain:** Describe what happens to a patient before, during and after the relevant treatment or investigation. Identify the decision the device changes, the usual alternative and the staff or equipment needed at that point.
- **What the sources actually tell us:** The clinical study compares Terra with 3T Prisma after unclear vascular findings. Source: [Radojewski et al. Diagnosis of Small Unruptured Intracranial Aneurysms: Comparison of 7T versus 3T MRI](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00062-023-01282-2). Relevant passage: Data Collection/Image Analysis.
- **What this lets us write:** This places the additional scan after uncertainty in standard imaging. It is a pathway in one study, not evidence of the same referral and reporting process at a London facility.
- **What we cannot yet answer:** We need local patient selection, referral, image reading and escalation arrangements for the use being investigated.
- **Next investigation:** Find a guideline or actual service description that traces the relevant patient pathway and the decision where this device would enter. Start with [Actual service and purchasing evidence](../docs/imperial-gap-searches.md#service); the linked access log states what was actually tried and any access limits.

### What specifically goes wrong with existing care? (N3)

- **What the report needs to explain:** Show a measured problem with the care available before the device: for example, continuing symptoms, missed diagnoses, waiting time or patients unable to access treatment. Explain how often it happens. A later trial of the new device does not by itself measure the original care shortfall.
- **What the sources actually tell us:** Among 53 selected patients, aneurysm was suspected in 42 at 3T and 23 at 7T. The study did not use DSA as an independent reference, and readers were not blinded to field strength. Source: [Radojewski et al. Diagnosis of Small Unruptured Intracranial Aneurysms: Comparison of 7T versus 3T MRI](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00062-023-01282-2). Relevant passage: Results; reference-standard limitations.
- **What this lets us write:** We can report diagnostic reclassification. We cannot call every changed diagnosis a corrected false positive or calculate established diagnostic accuracy from those counts.
- **What we cannot yet answer:** We need independent accuracy and management evidence and a measure of how often the relevant local pathway produces uncertainty.
- **Next investigation:** Find a study of the existing pathway that measures the failure or access problem, preferably from the period when development began. Start with [Clinical literature](../docs/imperial-gap-searches.md#lit); the linked access log states what was actually tried and any access limits.

### What are the consequences of that problem? (N4)

- **What the report needs to explain:** Explain how the care shortfall affects patients and services. Connect it to outcomes, staff time, hospital use or costs, and identify which groups carry that burden. A large disease-cost figure needs a reasoned connection to the patients and problem this device addresses.
- **What the sources actually tell us:** The study discusses the possibility of avoiding unnecessary investigations but does not report a cost-effectiveness analysis. Source: [Radojewski et al. Diagnosis of Small Unruptured Intracranial Aneurysms: Comparison of 7T versus 3T MRI](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00062-023-01282-2). Relevant passage: Discussion.
- **What this lets us write:** This identifies a hypothesis worth testing. A changed imaging assessment does not show that an investigation was actually cancelled or that a patient avoided harm or cost.
- **What we cannot yet answer:** We need observed management changes, investigations added or avoided, patient consequences and local resource costs.
- **Next investigation:** Find measured patient consequences and resource use for the relevant subgroup, then identify the price year and setting of any cost estimate. Start with [Costs and health-service evidence](../docs/imperial-gap-searches.md#cost); the linked access log states what was actually tried and any access limits.

### What benefit has this particular device demonstrated? (N5)

- **What the report needs to explain:** Explain how the device could change the identified problem and what has actually been measured. Distinguish a technical result, a change in a clinical decision and an improvement patients experience. State the study population, comparison and follow-up so the reader can see how far the finding reaches.
- **What the sources actually tell us:** The reviewed neurovascular paper reports exact-device findings. The separate epilepsy publication is a study protocol, rather than completed outcome evidence. Source: [Radojewski et al. Diagnosis of Small Unruptured Intracranial Aneurysms: Comparison of 7T versus 3T MRI](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00062-023-01282-2); [Study protocol: value of 7-T MRI with prospective motion correction and postprocessing for patients with nonlesional epilepsy](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10309-023-00618-9). Relevant passage: Study results; protocol design.
- **What this lets us write:** This supports a bounded neurovascular investigation. It cannot establish patient benefit in epilepsy or musculoskeletal imaging from the same evidence.
- **What we cannot yet answer:** We need patient outcomes, completed epilepsy results and separate comparative knee or other musculoskeletal evidence for any claims in those fields.
- **Next investigation:** Read the original device study for its comparator, measured endpoints, follow-up, adverse events and limitations; look for the missing patient outcome rather than another mechanism description. Start with [Clinical literature](../docs/imperial-gap-searches.md#lit); the linked access log states what was actually tried and any access limits.

### What other options could meet the same need? (N6)

- **What the report needs to explain:** Compare options available to the same patients at the same point in care. Explain their different benefits, risks and practical demands. A product used as a regulatory comparison is not automatically the most appropriate clinical alternative.
- **What the sources actually tell us:** The direct clinical comparator is 3T MRI, and further specialist imaging is a possible pathway alternative. Source: [Radojewski et al. Diagnosis of Small Unruptured Intracranial Aneurysms: Comparison of 7T versus 3T MRI](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00062-023-01282-2); [FDA. MAGNETOM Terra: K170840 clearance and 510(k) summary](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cdrh_docs/pdf17/K170840.pdf). Relevant passage: Comparative methods; coil/scope.
- **What this lets us write:** We can frame the decision as whether an extra high-field scan adds useful information in selected cases. A higher field strength is not evidence that it is the better option for every examination.
- **What we cannot yet answer:** We need matched alternative-imaging trade-offs, patient exclusions, scan time and resource consequences in the chosen pathway.
- **Next investigation:** Find alternatives for the same patient group and compare their eligibility, outcomes, risks and service requirements. Start with [Clinical literature](../docs/imperial-gap-searches.md#lit); the linked access log states what was actually tried and any access limits.

## Intellectual property

### Who made the original invention, and where? (I1)

- **What the report needs to explain:** Identify the inventors of the technology that became the selected device, their employers when the invention arose and the earliest relevant publication or patent priority. A current university biography is not evidence of where an inventor worked at that earlier date.
- **What the sources actually tell us:** The brochure attributes magnet development and manufacture to Oxford, while the FDA record names system and coil manufacturers. Source: [Siemens Healthineers. MAGNETOM Terra brochure](https://cdn0.scrvt.com/39b415fb07de4d9656c7b516d8e2d907/1800000006633504/e6260b86a79c/2019-MAGNETOM-Terra-brochure_1800000006633504.pdf); [FDA. MAGNETOM Terra: K170840 clearance and 510(k) summary](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cdrh_docs/pdf17/K170840.pdf). Relevant passage: Brochure PDF pp34–35; manufacturing table.
- **What this lets us write:** These identify organisations and engineering roles to investigate. A manufacturing location is not the name or employer of the original inventor.
- **What we cannot yet answer:** We need original magnet, coil and system inventors, their employers and the relevant earliest priority families.
- **Next investigation:** Trace the original inventors and predecessor organisations using patent priority records and contemporaneous employment or university records. Start with [Patent and rights investigation](../docs/imperial-gap-searches.md#pat); the linked access log states what was actually tried and any access limits.

### How did the company obtain the right to use it? (I2)

- **What the report needs to explain:** Explain who originally owned the invention and how rights reached the developer through assignment, acquisition or licence. Identify the records supporting each step. A shared founder, research collaboration or patent applicant does not establish every transfer or licence term.
- **What the sources actually tell us:** The later optimisation application lists Siemens as its original assignee. Source: [GB2606208A. Computer-implemented magnetic resonance image optimisation method](https://patents.google.com/patent/GB2606208A/en). Relevant passage: Assignee metadata.
- **What this lets us write:** This establishes an ownership detail for that later method only. It does not establish who controlled every technology in the original 2017 scanner.
- **What we cannot yet answer:** We need original subsystem assignments and licences and the route by which the developer obtained the rights required for the first system.
- **Next investigation:** Retrieve the actual assignment or licence record and follow the original owner's rights into the developer, leaving unavailable contractual terms explicit. Start with [Patent and rights investigation](../docs/imperial-gap-searches.md#pat); the linked access log states what was actually tried and any access limits.

### Which patent claims correspond to the device's features? (I3)

- **What the report needs to explain:** Explain each relevant independent claim in ordinary language, then compare its required features with documented features of the selected product. An independent claim sets out a combination of features without relying on another claim. Identify any feature that cannot yet be matched.
- **What the sources actually tell us:** GB2606208A has a 2021 priority; independent claims 1 and 12 concern voxel-clustering optimisation and a processing system. Source: [GB2606208A. Computer-implemented magnetic resonance image optimisation method](https://patents.google.com/patent/GB2606208A/en). Relevant passage: Claims 1/12; Terra example.
- **What this lets us write:** We can explain those later claims, but they postdate the original scanner. Using Terra in a later invention does not make that invention the core IP behind its 2017 market entry.
- **What we cannot yet answer:** We need the original relevant magnet, coil and imaging-system families and their independent claims matched to the first product.
- **Next investigation:** Compare the exact publication's independent claims with technical drawings or instructions for the selected product version, one required feature at a time. Start with [Patent and rights investigation](../docs/imperial-gap-searches.md#pat); the linked access log states what was actually tried and any access limits.

### Where and when does that protection apply? (I4)

- **What the report needs to explain:** Identify the relevant patent family, priority date and countries, and check the dated status of the particular application or granted patent. An application, an expired patent and a live grant answer different questions. Later amendments may change the claims.
- **What the sources actually tell us:** The optimisation application records April 2021 priority and November 2022 publication. Source: [GB2606208A. Computer-implemented magnetic resonance image optimisation method](https://patents.google.com/patent/GB2606208A/en). Relevant passage: Bibliographic metadata.
- **What this lets us write:** That chronology cannot supply an original Terra protection map. Its status and jurisdictions would answer a different, later-development question.
- **What we cannot yet answer:** We need the original product families, priorities, countries and official dated status of the rights relevant to them.
- **Next investigation:** Check the correct family and national patent registers for dated status, granted text and amendments rather than relying only on an aggregator's summary. Start with [Patent and rights investigation](../docs/imperial-gap-searches.md#pat); the linked access log states what was actually tried and any access limits.

### What else can the company protect or control? (I5)

- **What the report needs to explain:** Look for documented software rights, designs, trademarks, confidential know-how or data rights that matter to the device. Explain what the company controls and why it could matter commercially. Owning a factory does not by itself establish that a particular process is legally protected.
- **What the sources actually tell us:** The earlier analysis identifies magnet fabrication, calibration and software as possible sources of non-patent advantage, without a verified rights inventory. Source: [Siemens Healthineers. MAGNETOM Terra brochure](https://cdn0.scrvt.com/39b415fb07de4d9656c7b516d8e2d907/1800000006633504/e6260b86a79c/2019-MAGNETOM-Terra-brochure_1800000006633504.pdf). Relevant passage: Development/manufacture; analytical interpretation.
- **What this lets us write:** These are technical questions to pursue, not established protected assets. Complexity and company involvement do not disclose which processes or rights the developer controls.
- **What we cannot yet answer:** We need specific documented software, data, design or confidential know-how rights and their ownership or licensing arrangements.
- **Next investigation:** Look for an explicit company or contractual description of the relevant software, know-how, design or data rights and who controls them. Start with [Company and financing history](../docs/imperial-gap-searches.md#fin); the linked access log states what was actually tried and any access limits.

### What could limit that control or allow competition? (I6)

- **What the report needs to explain:** Identify relevant third-party rights, licences on which the developer depends and possible competing designs. Explain what the available records let us say about competition. Our search does not establish a legal right to commercialise every feature or prove freedom from infringement.
- **What the sources actually tell us:** The system combines magnet, coil, imaging and software technologies from multiple documented organisations. Source: [FDA. MAGNETOM Terra: K170840 clearance and 510(k) summary](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cdrh_docs/pdf17/K170840.pdf); [GB2606208A. Computer-implemented magnetic resonance image optimisation method](https://patents.google.com/patent/GB2606208A/en). Relevant passage: Component identities; claim boundary.
- **What this lets us write:** This makes supplier rights and licences relevant. One later optimisation patent cannot resolve the legal exposure of the complete original system.
- **What we cannot yet answer:** We need original supplier-rights arrangements, competing claims, technical alternatives and any licence dependencies.
- **Next investigation:** Investigate specific overlapping claims and licence dependencies; use technical alternatives to frame the unresolved question rather than declaring legal clearance. Start with [Patent and rights investigation](../docs/imperial-gap-searches.md#pat); the linked access log states what was actually tried and any access limits.

## Fundraising

### Was early research supported by grants? (F1)

- **What the report needs to explain:** For each relevant grant, establish who received it, the award identifier, dates, amount and purpose. Explain whether it supported a precursor laboratory, the company or this product. An acknowledgement in a paper can identify a lead without revealing the money attributable to the device.
- **What the sources actually tell us:** King's and Wellcome supported the 2018 London project, but the reviewed account gives no amount. Source: [King’s College London. London’s most powerful MRI scanner to transform research](https://www.kcl.ac.uk/news/londons-most-powerful-mri-scanner-to-transform-research-copy). Relevant passage: Funding paragraph.
- **What this lets us write:** This is customer infrastructure support after original clearance. It is not evidence of a grant that financed original Terra development.
- **What we cannot yet answer:** We need any original development award's recipient, identifier, period, amount and purpose and its relationship to the scanner programme.
- **Next investigation:** Trace the acknowledgement to an authoritative award record and check its recipient, period, amount, purpose and relationship to this device. Start with [Awards and original research support](../docs/imperial-gap-searches.md#grant); the linked access log states what was actually tried and any access limits.

### What money did investors actually provide? (F2)

- **What the report needs to explain:** Reconstruct the relevant early investment: who invested, when it closed, the currency, amount, type of security and any disclosed valuation. Separate money offered from money sold or received, and distinguish a company fundraising total from money allocated to this product.
- **What the sources actually tell us:** The 2018 IPO generated about €4.2 billion gross proceeds for parent Siemens. Source: [Siemens Healthineers. Shares start trading: IPO placement and proceeds](https://www.siemens-healthineers.com/press/releases/pr-2018030202coen.html). Relevant passage: Placement/proceeds paragraph.
- **What this lets us write:** This is a corporate financing event with a different recipient and purpose. It cannot be reassigned as capital received by Terra's original development programme.
- **What we cannot yet answer:** We need attributable original corporate investment and any genuinely relevant financing terms for work before the scanner's market entry.
- **Next investigation:** Recover the original transaction or filing and check the closing, investors, instrument, proceeds and valuation basis; keep undisclosed fields separate. Start with [Company and financing history](../docs/imperial-gap-searches.md#fin); the linked access log states what was actually tried and any access limits.

- **What we cannot yet answer:** We need records connecting specific resources to magnet development, integration, verification and clinical validation work.

### What would early development have cost if the budget is private? (F6)

- **What the report needs to explain:** Build a reasoned estimate using both approaches allowed by the specification. Add up sourced staffing, engineering, testing, trial and manufacturing costs from the bottom up, and compare with similar programmes from the top down. State assumptions and ranges. This estimates capital needed; it does not reconstruct undisclosed fundraising.
- **What the sources actually tell us:** No justified numerical estimate of original-system development capital has been completed.
- **What this lets us write:** Shared MRI platform work and supplier-developed components need an explicit allocation method. Customer installation funding is not a substitute.
- **What we cannot yet answer:** We need magnet, coil and software staffing, prototypes, testing and tooling inputs and comparable programmes for the top-down estimate.
- **Next investigation:** Source staff time, prototype, testing, trial and production inputs, then cross-check the range against genuinely comparable development programmes. Start with [Capital estimate inputs](../docs/imperial-gap-searches.md#est); the linked access log states what was actually tried and any access limits.

## Regulatory

### Exactly which product and use are being regulated? (R1)

- **What the report needs to explain:** Define the hardware, software and accessories, the intended patients, user and care setting. Identify the original version being studied and separate later changes. This prevents evidence about a component or research system being applied to a different complete product.
- **What the sources actually tell us:** The original scope is head and extremity imaging above 30 kg with specified head and knee coils; clinical and research modes differ. Source: [FDA. MAGNETOM Terra: K170840 clearance and 510(k) summary](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cdrh_docs/pdf17/K170840.pdf); [Siemens Healthineers. MAGNETOM Terra brochure](https://cdn0.scrvt.com/39b415fb07de4d9656c7b516d8e2d907/1800000006633504/e6260b86a79c/2019-MAGNETOM-Terra-brochure_1800000006633504.pdf); [Siemens Healthineers. MAGNETOM Terra.X receives FDA clearance](https://www.siemens-healthineers.com/en-us/press-room/press-releases/magnetom-terra-x-fda-clearance). Relevant passage: Indication; dual-mode; successor scope.
- **What this lets us write:** This keeps original Terra separate from Terra.X and research options. The exact installed London configuration may have changed and is not established by the original record alone.
- **What we cannot yet answer:** We need the local model, software, coils, upgrades and enabled clinical options before describing that installation's clinical scope.
- **Next investigation:** Retrieve the selected version's instructions and decision documents, then list the exact configuration, users, setting and intended use. Start with [Actual service and purchasing evidence](../docs/imperial-gap-searches.md#service); the linked access log states what was actually tried and any access limits.

### Why did it follow this regulatory route? (R2)

- **What the report needs to explain:** For each market discussed, explain the device classification and the applicable rule or product code, then connect the route to the device's function and risks. Evidence of a US route does not establish UK or EU authorisation.
- **What the sources actually tell us:** The original US route is Class II under 21 CFR 892.1000 with product codes LNH, LNI and MOS, using a 510(k). Source: [FDA. MAGNETOM Terra: K170840 clearance and 510(k) summary](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cdrh_docs/pdf17/K170840.pdf). Relevant passage: Classification/predicate.
- **What this lets us write:** We can explain the original US MRI-system route. Those codes do not establish the installed system's UK or EU permissions.
- **What we cannot yet answer:** We need the applicable primary conformity and registration documents for the selected local configuration.
- **Next investigation:** Use the exact market's regulator or conformity records to establish the classification and route, explaining their connection to the device's function. Start with [Official records first](../docs/imperial-gap-searches.md#open); the linked access log states what was actually tried and any access limits.

### What actual decision allowed market entry? (R3)

- **What the report needs to explain:** Give the decision identifier, date, type and authorised scope from the original record. Distinguish clearance, approval, registration and a manufacturer's market announcement. Check later changes separately if the report makes claims about the present product.
- **What the sources actually tell us:** K170840 records clearance on 12 October 2017. Terra.X has a separate later history. Source: [FDA. MAGNETOM Terra: K170840 clearance and 510(k) summary](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cdrh_docs/pdf17/K170840.pdf); [Siemens Healthineers. MAGNETOM Terra.X receives FDA clearance](https://www.siemens-healthineers.com/en-us/press-room/press-releases/magnetom-terra-x-fda-clearance). Relevant passage: Decision; successor announcement.
- **What this lets us write:** This gives a traceable original decision. Later upgrade functions must not be folded into the 2017 record without their own documentation.
- **What we cannot yet answer:** We need the relevant later authorisations and the actual installed upgrade documentation if the report discusses those features.
- **Next investigation:** Follow the original decision to any relevant supplements or later clearances, keeping each version and indication separate. Start with [Official records first](../docs/imperial-gap-searches.md#open); the linked access log states what was actually tried and any access limits.

### What tests and standards supported that decision? (R4)

- **What the report needs to explain:** Connect the device's specific risks to the tests or standards reported in its regulatory record. Identify the edition where available and what the test showed. A general standard explains an expectation; it does not prove the manufacturer performed or passed that test.
- **What the sources actually tell us:** The summary includes MRI safety, image-quality, software and risk-management evidence. A 35-person peripheral-nerve-stimulation study informed safety limits. Source: [FDA. MAGNETOM Terra: K170840 clearance and 510(k) summary](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cdrh_docs/pdf17/K170840.pdf). Relevant passage: Testing; IEC 60601-2-33/62464-1/62304.
- **What this lets us write:** We can explain why the scanner needed safety and performance evidence. The nerve-stimulation work evaluates unwanted stimulation limits, not diagnostic benefit to patients.
- **What we cannot yet answer:** We need complete reported results and historical editions and the conformity evidence applicable to the configuration under discussion.
- **Next investigation:** Read the device's test summary and the exact historical standard where accessible; record the reported test result and which supporting details remain private. Start with [Standards](../docs/imperial-gap-searches.md#std); the linked access log states what was actually tried and any access limits.

- **What the report needs to explain:** Identify the legal manufacturer, important contract manufacturers or suppliers, production sites and who controlled assembly, testing and release. MAGNETOM Terra brochure](https://cdn0.scrvt.com/39b415fb07de4d9656c7b516d8e2d907/1800000006633504/e6260b86a79c/2019-MAGNETOM-Terra-brochure_1800000006633504.pdf).
