# Seven devices: what the evidence lets us write

This page is for teammates choosing a report case. Each device starts with a paragraph explaining what it does and where its investigation stands. The five bullets then explain what the available material contributes to each report section and what we still need to find. Open the detailed guide for the requirement-by-requirement reasoning.

All seven use the same [31 questions from the supplied guide](../docs/report-evidence-requirements.md). This is a rewrite of evidence reviewed on 19–21 October, not a new source audit. [The Imperial access log](../docs/imperial-gap-searches.md) separates completed retrievals from proposed searches.

## Precision Neuroscience — Layer 7-T

Precision’s Layer 7-T is a flexible electrode placed on the brain surface for temporary recording, monitoring or stimulation during neurosurgical care. The evidence reviewed concerns this temporary component, not a permanent home-use brain–computer interface. Its FDA record and small acute study explain the product and early feasibility; they leave its effect on patient outcomes and hospital costs unresolved.

- **Unmet medical need:** The FDA indication tells us to investigate temporary cortical procedures. The five-patient study supports acute recording feasibility. We still need to measure the failure of existing mapping, count eligible procedures and establish whether the new information improves patient outcomes. [Sources and unanswered questions](precision.md#unmet-medical-need).

- **Intellectual property:** The selected application identifies inventors and four independent claims, covering the array, insertion, fabrication and a wider decoding use. We can explain those features, but have not established every inventor’s original employer, the full rights chain or a complete match to the marketed component. [Sources and unanswered questions](precision.md#intellectual-property).

- **Fundraising:** Announcements report $12 million and $41 million company rounds, followed by a foundry acquisition. These give a financing timeline. They do not disclose the valuation, money allocated to Layer 7-T or a defensible estimate of the capital required to develop it. [Sources and unanswered questions](precision.md#fundraising).

- **Regulatory:** K242618 gives a specific US clearance and describes the temporary electrode and supporting tests. We still need the complete manufacturing responsibility split, later label changes and a dated product-specific surveillance search. The US record cannot establish a UK or EU authorisation. [Sources and unanswered questions](precision.md#regulatory).

- **Reimbursement:** No verified purchase price, actual payer arrangement or exact-component economic evaluation was found. General service-payment rules explain possible mechanisms but do not establish this electrode’s payment. A real hospital contract and evidence linking device use to resource or outcome changes are needed. [Sources and unanswered questions](precision.md#reimbursement).

## NeuroPace — RNS System

NeuroPace’s RNS System uses implanted electrodes and a cranial stimulator to record brain activity and respond to detected seizure patterns with stimulation. It is studied here for selected adults with drug-resistant focal epilepsy. Trials, a prospectus, the original FDA approval and an economic model provide a connected account, while original funding allocations, current purchase prices and UK service arrangements remain incomplete.

- **Unmet medical need:** The controlled trial and nine-year follow-up let us describe measured seizure effects and distinguish short-term comparison from open-label durability. They do not count all eligible patients or quantify the original failure of care. Local pathway costs, patient selection and comparable alternatives remain necessary. [Sources and unanswered questions](rns.md#unmet-medical-need).

- **Intellectual property:** The foundational patent and prospectus provide invention and protection history. The two independent claims have been read, but the remote sensor or actuator in claim 46 is not fully mapped to the product. Original assignments, inventor employment and the later live portfolio remain unresolved. [Sources and unanswered questions](rns.md#intellectual-property).

- **Fundraising:** A 2011 filing reports $49,126,004 sold in an offering before approval. That is useful transaction evidence, but not a complete account of cash received or original development finance. Earlier rounds, valuations, grants and the link between proceeds and particular development work still need records. [Sources and unanswered questions](rns.md#fundraising).

- **Regulatory:** P100026 establishes original US approval in 2013. The FDA material, manufacturing disclosure and model-specific MRI instructions support a development account. Later models and indications, current suppliers, complete test details and a dated postmarket search remain separate work. [Sources and unanswered questions](rns.md#regulatory).

- **Reimbursement:** An insurer policy supplies coverage conditions, and the full economic paper exposes a historical nine-year model. The model’s $41,173 implantation input is not a device quote. Current prices, actual payment amounts, UK costs and the hospital’s staffing and budget position remain unverified. [Sources and unanswered questions](rns.md#reimbursement).

## Ceribell — Rapid Response EEG

Ceribell combines a rapidly applied scalp EEG system with software that helps clinicians assess brain activity in acute care. The hardware, sonification technology and later seizure-monitoring software need to be kept separate. The reviewed material explains an EEG-access problem, licensing and early finance, but it does not establish that faster EEG produces the claimed financial or patient benefits in London.

- **Unmet medical need:** DECIDE measured a median five-minute rapid EEG acquisition against a 239-minute conventional EEG delay in that study. This supports an access problem and workflow proposition. It does not establish better survival, a causal reduction in admissions or realised NHS savings. [Sources and unanswered questions](ceribell.md#unmet-medical-need).

- **Intellectual property:** The prospectus describes a Stanford licence and amendments, while the selected patent explains sonification. These can support a concrete technology-transfer story. We still need the complete historical rights record and a feature match for later AI software rather than treating the sonification patent as protection for everything. [Sources and unanswered questions](ceribell.md#intellectual-property).

- **Fundraising:** The prospectus provides early seed, note and Series A information, plus a university support lead. This allows more than a list of recent rounds. The original award amount, valuations and spending allocated to particular technical or clinical milestones remain missing. [Sources and unanswered questions](ceribell.md#fundraising).

- **Regulatory:** FDA and company records distinguish system versions and document parts of the production chain. The report still needs a complete version-by-version decision and testing account, present manufacturing responsibilities and a dated recall or incident search. [Sources and unanswered questions](ceribell.md#regulatory).

- **Reimbursement:** A publication estimates savings, while temporary US add-on payment concerns a particular software use and period. Neither establishes a current UK price or realised saving. We need actual contract terms, applicable base payment and evidence that workflow changes alter costly patient events. [Sources and unanswered questions](ceribell.md#reimbursement).

[Read the complete evidence guide](ceribell.md).

## Magnus Medical — SAINT

Magnus Medical’s SAINT combines MRI-informed targeting with an accelerated schedule of transcranial magnetic stimulation for depression. The selected case is the integrated system covered by the original US clearance, rather than accelerated TMS generally. There is a small controlled trial and useful patent and fundraising material; durability, the full delivery cost and actual local payment still need investigation.

- **Unmet medical need:** The early trial reports short-term remission in 11 of 14 active patients versus 2 of 15 sham patients. It helps assess the treatment proposition, but its small size, early stopping and short follow-up limit what we can conclude. Eligible local numbers and long-term resource consequences remain missing. [Sources and unanswered questions](saint.md#unmet-medical-need).

- **Intellectual property:** The selected Stanford patent gives four independent claims and distinguishes different stimulation schedules. A disclosed licence relationship supplies an ownership lead. The report still needs original employment, the actual contractual rights and a complete match between the cleared system and the relevant claim features. [Sources and unanswered questions](saint.md#intellectual-property).

- **Fundraising:** The $25 million 2021 round and named backers establish an early company funding event. They do not disclose the valuation, all precursor grants or the budget spent on the integrated system. A capital estimate would require sourced MRI, stimulation, trial and engineering inputs. [Sources and unanswered questions](saint.md#fundraising).

- **Regulatory:** K220177 establishes the original 2022 US clearance and reports supporting evidence. It helps explain the integrated system’s route. Present configurations, other-market authorisations, the full manufacturer–supplier responsibility split and postmarket findings remain unverified. [Sources and unanswered questions](saint.md#regulatory).

- **Reimbursement:** The five-day treatment schedule indicates resource demands, and dated US add-on support gives a payment-history lead. We still need total MRI and staff costs, retreatment frequency, actual package price and applicable payer terms. Another London TMS service is not proof of SAINT use or payment. [Sources and unanswered questions](saint.md#reimbursement).

[Read the complete evidence guide](saint.md).

## Insightec — Exablate Neuro

Insightec’s Exablate Neuro uses MRI-guided focused ultrasound to create a targeted thermal lesion. This case focuses on the original unilateral treatment for medication-refractory essential tremor. A controlled trial, UK commissioning evidence, an England economic model and a documented London service support the investigation. Early brain-device funding, original patent ownership and actual equipment and service costs remain less complete.

- **Unmet medical need:** The original trial measures tremor benefit against sham in a selected refractory group. UK service evidence helps place treatment in a real pathway. We still need local eligible volumes, the original care shortfall, longer-term consequences and current service resource use. [Sources and unanswered questions](exablate.md#unmet-medical-need).

- **Intellectual property:** The selected focused-ultrasound patent allows a technical independent-claim explanation. Its exact relationship to the original brain product and rights chain remains incomplete. We must trace the original device and subsystem families rather than assume any Insightec patent covers the whole system. [Sources and unanswered questions](exablate.md#intellectual-property).

- **Fundraising:** Investor-side records describe 2012 financing, including mixed cash and loan elements. Inconsistent valuation figures remain unresolved, and company money has not been allocated to the brain programme. Earliest resources and a justified required-capital estimate still need reconstruction. [Sources and unanswered questions](exablate.md#fundraising).

- **Regulatory:** P150038 establishes the original US essential-tremor approval. The report can explain that indication and supporting evidence. Later indications, current production and UK or EU device records need separate checking; Parkinsonian-tremor guidance cannot substitute for the essential-tremor pathway. [Sources and unanswered questions](exablate.md#regulatory).

- **Reimbursement:** England commissioning and an economic model support a concrete reimbursement discussion. The model estimates pathway costs, rather than reporting the current purchase price. London service enquiry could help with throughput and budget questions, but no interview, current quotation or contract has been obtained. [Sources and unanswered questions](exablate.md#reimbursement).

## ONWARD Medical — ARC-EX

ONWARD Medical’s ARC-EX applies external spinal stimulation alongside rehabilitation to improve hand function in selected people with chronic incomplete cervical spinal cord injury. It is separate from ONWARD’s implanted and brain-interface programmes. Regulatory documents, a rehabilitation study and nearby service connections help explain the case, while the original patent match, attributable early funding and payment arrangements remain unresolved.

- **Unmet medical need:** Up-LIFT measured hand strength and function after stimulation was added to rehabilitation; 43 of 60 completers met its combined endpoint. Its sequential, non-randomised design leaves causal attribution unresolved. Broad spinal-injury numbers do not establish eligibility or reduced care needs for this product. [Sources and unanswered questions](arcex.md#unmet-medical-need).

- **Intellectual property:** University licensing and NeuroRecovery Technologies history identify places to investigate. The selected claim has not been securely matched to ARC-EX’s external product. The original family, licence schedule, inventor employment and complete product-to-claim relationship remain important gaps. [Sources and unanswered questions](arcex.md#intellectual-property).

- **Fundraising:** Public filings give corporate financing and predecessor leads. Grants acknowledged by academic work are not automatically ARC-EX funding, and an implanted-programme round must stay separate. We still need attributable early resources, transaction terms and a sourced capital estimate for the external system. [Sources and unanswered questions](arcex.md#fundraising).

- **Regulatory:** The US De Novo record and later home-use clearance define two specific milestones. Component and supplier details support manufacture discussion. Primary EU or UK records, the complete production responsibility chain and a dated surveillance search remain incomplete. [Sources and unanswered questions](arcex.md#regulatory).

- **Reimbursement:** Near-London rehabilitation and charity arrangements provide real service questions, not proof of routine NHS coverage. We still need device and session prices, independent payer conditions, incremental treatment costs and controlled evidence for reduced care needs or other proposed savings. [Sources and unanswered questions](arcex.md#reimbursement).

## Siemens Healthineers — MAGNETOM Terra

Siemens Healthineers’ MAGNETOM Terra is a 7-tesla MRI platform, studied here within the original head and extremity imaging scope. The original system must be separated from Terra.X and later upgrades. Regulatory and subsystem records, a neurovascular comparison and exact equipment at King’s support investigation; original scanner IP, attributable development capital and the economics of clinical use are still incomplete.

- **Unmet medical need:** The exact-device neurovascular study shows diagnostic reclassification in selected uncertain cases, not established accuracy or better patient outcomes. A separate epilepsy publication is a protocol. We need a defined clinical use, eligible referrals, downstream consequences and separate evidence for musculoskeletal benefits. [Sources and unanswered questions](terra.md#unmet-medical-need).

- **Intellectual property:** The previously selected optimisation application has a 2021 priority, after the original scanner’s market entry. It cannot establish original 2017 IP. The report needs the original magnet, coil and system families, their inventors and employers, and the agreements giving the developer control. [Sources and unanswered questions](terra.md#intellectual-property).

- **Fundraising:** Siemens parent IPO proceeds and a customer’s research-infrastructure grant answer different questions from Terra development finance. Neither can serve as the scanner’s budget. Original internal R&D resources, supplier-development arrangements and a justified estimate of required capital remain missing. [Sources and unanswered questions](terra.md#fundraising).

- **Regulatory:** K170840 defines original US head and extremity scope and identifies important components. This supports a specific engineering and regulatory account. It does not settle the installed London configuration, current UK permissions, all supplier responsibilities or later changes. [Sources and unanswered questions](terra.md#regulatory).

- **Reimbursement:** King’s documents exact Terra equipment and research support, which makes a scoped local investigation possible. It does not establish routine clinical commissioning or per-scan payment. Actual acquisition and operating costs, funded clinical use and comparative pathway economics remain unresolved. [Sources and unanswered questions](terra.md#reimbursement).
