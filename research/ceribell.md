# Ceribell — Rapid Response EEG: what we can write, and what is missing

Ceribell combines a rapidly applied scalp EEG system with software that helps clinicians assess brain activity in acute care. The hardware, sonification technology and later seizure-monitoring software need to be kept separate. The reviewed material explains an EEG-access problem, licensing and early finance, but it does not establish that faster EEG produces the claimed financial or patient benefits in London.

This guide works through the five report sections for a teammate deciding what investigation is still needed. Each question explains the requirement, the actual evidence, the conclusion it supports, the unanswered part and a way to investigate it. Evidence limitations are explained in sentences rather than assigned a grade.

[Device-by-device overview](README.md) · [The shared requirements](../docs/report-evidence-requirements.md) · [What Imperial access actually recovered](../docs/imperial-gap-searches.md).

The evidence comes from the existing 19–21 October research. This revision clarifies that evidence; it adds no new retrievals. The next investigations below are proposals unless the access log explicitly records otherwise.

## Unmet medical need

### Who could actually receive this device? (N1)

- **What the report needs to explain:** Explain the condition and the specific patients or procedures the device is intended for. Then give the number who meet those criteria in a named country and year. Explain why a count of everyone with the disease would include people who cannot receive this device.
- **What the sources actually tell us:** DECIDE examined urgent EEG in critically ill patients, with 181 encounters and complete clinical and EEG information for 164. Source: [Vespa et al.: DECIDE prospective observational study](https://ceribell.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/Vespa-online-CCM-DECIDE-Study-2020-1-4.pdf); [ACNS consensus: continuous EEG in critically ill adults and children, indications](https://www.acns.org/UserFiles/file/Consensus_Statement_on_Continuous_EEG_in.1.pdf). Relevant passage: Study population; critical-care indications.
- **What this lets us write:** This identifies a suspected-seizure care pathway, including patients whose recording may not show seizures. It is not a count of all patients who could need the system.
- **What we cannot yet answer:** We need the annual number of eligible acute-care episodes in a defined geography, including suspected cases that ultimately prove negative.
- **Next investigation:** Find a population study or service record that applies the device's actual eligibility criteria, and record the country, year and exclusions. Start with [Clinical literature](../docs/imperial-gap-searches.md#lit); the linked access log states what was actually tried and any access limits.

### Where would the device enter existing care? (N2)

- **What the report needs to explain:** Describe what happens to a patient before, during and after the relevant treatment or investigation. Identify the decision the device changes, the usual alternative and the staff or equipment needed at that point.
- **What the sources actually tell us:** The ACNS source explains indications and resource constraints for continuous EEG. Ceribell provides an earlier rapid recording while conventional monitoring is being arranged. Source: [ACNS consensus: continuous EEG in critically ill adults and children, indications](https://www.acns.org/UserFiles/file/Consensus_Statement_on_Continuous_EEG_in.1.pdf); [Vespa et al.: DECIDE prospective observational study](https://ceribell.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/Vespa-online-CCM-DECIDE-Study-2020-1-4.pdf). Relevant passage: Indications; workflow methods.
- **What this lets us write:** We can explain the decision the rapid result could inform before conventional EEG arrives. That does not show how every hospital escalates or continues monitoring.
- **What we cannot yet answer:** We need the local sequence of rapid assessment, expert interpretation, treatment and conventional EEG, including the staff available at each step.
- **Next investigation:** Find a guideline or actual service description that traces the relevant patient pathway and the decision where this device would enter. Start with [Clinical literature](../docs/imperial-gap-searches.md#lit); the linked access log states what was actually tried and any access limits.

### What specifically goes wrong with existing care? (N3)

- **What the report needs to explain:** Show a measured problem with the care available before the device: for example, continuing symptoms, missed diagnoses, waiting time or patients unable to access treatment. Explain how often it happens. A later trial of the new device does not by itself measure the original care shortfall.
- **What the sources actually tell us:** DECIDE reports a median five-minute rapid EEG acquisition and a 239-minute delay to conventional EEG. Source: [Vespa et al.: DECIDE prospective observational study](https://ceribell.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/Vespa-online-CCM-DECIDE-Study-2020-1-4.pdf). Relevant passage: Acquisition/delay results; denominators vary.
- **What this lets us write:** This measures an access difference in that observational, company-funded study. It does not establish the same delay in London or isolate a causal effect on patient treatment.
- **What we cannot yet answer:** We need comparable local delay data and evidence of what actually changes in treatment when earlier information is provided.
- **Next investigation:** Find a study of the existing pathway that measures the failure or access problem, preferably from the period when development began. Start with [Clinical literature](../docs/imperial-gap-searches.md#lit); the linked access log states what was actually tried and any access limits.

### What are the consequences of that problem? (N4)

- **What the report needs to explain:** Explain how the care shortfall affects patients and services. Connect it to outcomes, staff time, hospital use or costs, and identify which groups carry that burden. A large disease-cost figure needs a reasoned connection to the patients and problem this device addresses.
- **What the sources actually tell us:** The reviewed economic model predicts approximately $5,633 saved per use in 2019 US dollars. Source: [Ney, Gururangan and Parvizi: economic model of Ceribell Rapid Response EEG](https://ceribell.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/ROI-Paper-Ney-241-p318-327-J-Med-Econ-2021-4.pdf). Relevant passage: Model results and perspective.
- **What this lets us write:** This supplies a conditional economic consequence to examine. A manufacturer-funded model is not a measurement of cash saved by a hospital or of the harm caused by delayed EEG.
- **What we cannot yet answer:** We need observed patient consequences, local resource use and costs, and evidence of savings actually realised rather than predicted.
- **Next investigation:** Find measured patient consequences and resource use for the relevant subgroup, then identify the price year and setting of any cost estimate. Start with [Costs and health-service evidence](../docs/imperial-gap-searches.md#cost); the linked access log states what was actually tried and any access limits.

### What benefit has this particular device demonstrated? (N5)

- **What the report needs to explain:** Explain how the device could change the identified problem and what has actually been measured. Distinguish a technical result, a change in a clinical decision and an improvement patients experience. State the study population, comparison and follow-up so the reader can see how far the finding reaches.
- **What the sources actually tell us:** DECIDE examines clinician interpretation and decisions. The separate 38-patient emergency-department pilot included one positive seizure case. Source: [Vespa et al.: DECIDE prospective observational study](https://ceribell.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/Vespa-online-CCM-DECIDE-Study-2020-1-4.pdf); [Wright et al.: rapid EEG emergency-care pilot](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34039642/). Relevant passage: Endpoints; pilot abstract.
- **What this lets us write:** These studies can support specific workflow observations. One positive case cannot establish universal detection performance, and neither design proves a mortality benefit.
- **What we cannot yet answer:** We need broader diagnostic validation, evidence of correct treatment changes and comparative patient outcomes.
- **Next investigation:** Read the original device study for its comparator, measured endpoints, follow-up, adverse events and limitations; look for the missing patient outcome rather than another mechanism description. Start with [Clinical literature](../docs/imperial-gap-searches.md#lit); the linked access log states what was actually tried and any access limits.

### What other options could meet the same need? (N6)

- **What the report needs to explain:** Compare options available to the same patients at the same point in care. Explain their different benefits, risks and practical demands. A product used as a regulatory comparison is not automatically the most appropriate clinical alternative.
- **What the sources actually tell us:** Conventional continuous EEG is the relevant monitoring comparator, while Ceribell supplies a rapid assessment with fewer electrodes. Source: [ACNS consensus: continuous EEG in critically ill adults and children, indications](https://www.acns.org/UserFiles/file/Consensus_Statement_on_Continuous_EEG_in.1.pdf); [Vespa et al.: DECIDE prospective observational study](https://ceribell.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/Vespa-online-CCM-DECIDE-Study-2020-1-4.pdf). Relevant passage: Monitoring recommendations; study workflow.
- **What this lets us write:** We can explain that the two methods may serve different points in the pathway. Faster reduced-montage recording does not establish that prolonged conventional monitoring is unnecessary.
- **What we cannot yet answer:** We need a comparison of alternative rapid-assessment and monitoring strategies, including missed signals and the rules for escalation.
- **Next investigation:** Find alternatives for the same patient group and compare their eligibility, outcomes, risks and service requirements. Start with [Clinical literature](../docs/imperial-gap-searches.md#lit); the linked access log states what was actually tried and any access limits.

## Intellectual property

### Who made the original invention, and where? (I1)

- **What the report needs to explain:** Identify the inventors of the technology that became the selected device, their employers when the invention arose and the earliest relevant publication or patent priority. A current university biography is not evidence of where an inventor worked at that earlier date.
- **What the sources actually tell us:** US10136862B2 names Chafe and Parvizi and records priority in May 2012. Stanford links their sonification work to Ceribell. Source: [US10136862B2: sonifying brain electrical activity](https://patents.google.com/patent/US10136862B2/en); [Stanford: development of the brain stethoscope and Ceribell](https://neuroscience.stanford.edu/news/stanford-researchers-listen-silent-seizures-brain-stethoscope-turns-brain-waves-sound). Relevant passage: Patent metadata; development account.
- **What this lets us write:** This gives a specific academic invention trail, rather than merely a broad association with EEG research. It does not establish every inventor's employer or the entire first-product invention set.
- **What we cannot yet answer:** We need contemporaneous employment evidence and a clear account of which original inventions were needed for the first system.
- **Next investigation:** Trace the original inventors and predecessor organisations using patent priority records and contemporaneous employment or university records. Start with [Patent and rights investigation](../docs/imperial-gap-searches.md#pat); the linked access log states what was actually tried and any access limits.

### How did the company obtain the right to use it? (I2)

- **What the report needs to explain:** Explain who originally owned the invention and how rights reached the developer through assignment, acquisition or licence. Identify the records supporting each step. A shared founder, research collaboration or patent applicant does not establish every transfer or licence term.
- **What the sources actually tell us:** The 2017 amendment identifies a 2015 Stanford licence and technology dockets. A 2025 filing reports $250,000 for an exclusivity extension. Source: [Ceribell/Stanford licence amendment: disclosed technology and milestones](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1861107/000095017024100574/cbll-ex10_08.htm); [Ceribell: Stanford exclusivity extension](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1861107/000095017025088309/cbll-20250612.htm). Relevant passage: Technology schedule; exclusivity disclosure.
- **What this lets us write:** These records support an actual contractual route to rights and a disclosed later payment. Redactions and incomplete schedules prevent a full reconstruction of the licensed portfolio and economics.
- **What we cannot yet answer:** We need the complete licensed-family schedule and the unavailable royalty or other economic terms before describing the whole arrangement.
- **Next investigation:** Retrieve the actual assignment or licence record and follow the original owner's rights into the developer, leaving unavailable contractual terms explicit. Start with [Patent and rights investigation](../docs/imperial-gap-searches.md#pat); the linked access log states what was actually tried and any access limits.

### Which patent claims correspond to the device's features? (I3)

- **What the report needs to explain:** Explain each relevant independent claim in ordinary language, then compare its required features with documented features of the selected product. An independent claim sets out a combination of features without relying on another claim. Identify any feature that cannot yet be matched.
- **What the sources actually tell us:** Independent claims 1, 18, 20 and 26 describe different method, system and software forms of converting signals into sound. Source: [US10136862B2: sonifying brain electrical activity](https://patents.google.com/patent/US10136862B2/en). Relevant passage: Independent claims 1, 18, 20, 26.
- **What this lets us write:** We can explain the sonification invention and compare it with that part of the product. It does not automatically cover the later automated seizure-detection algorithms.
- **What we cannot yet answer:** We need a docket and family map and a feature-by-feature match for the original hardware, sonification and later Clarity versions separately.
- **Next investigation:** Compare the exact publication's independent claims with technical drawings or instructions for the selected product version, one required feature at a time. Start with [Patent and rights investigation](../docs/imperial-gap-searches.md#pat); the linked access log states what was actually tried and any access limits.

### Where and when does that protection apply? (I4)

- **What the report needs to explain:** Identify the relevant patent family, priority date and countries, and check the dated status of the particular application or granted patent. An application, an expired patent and a live grant answer different questions. Later amendments may change the claims.
- **What the sources actually tell us:** The selected grant was published in November 2018 and has a 2012 priority. Source: [US10136862B2: sonifying brain electrical activity](https://patents.google.com/patent/US10136862B2/en). Relevant passage: Bibliographic metadata.
- **What this lets us write:** This provides one patent's chronology. It does not establish the status, geographical reach or completeness of the broader portfolio.
- **What we cannot yet answer:** We need official dated status, family members and the later software-related families relevant to the version being discussed.
- **Next investigation:** Check the correct family and national patent registers for dated status, granted text and amendments rather than relying only on an aggregator's summary. Start with [Patent and rights investigation](../docs/imperial-gap-searches.md#pat); the linked access log states what was actually tried and any access limits.

### What else can the company protect or control? (I5)

- **What the report needs to explain:** Look for documented software rights, designs, trademarks, confidential know-how or data rights that matter to the device. Explain what the company controls and why it could matter commercially. Owning a factory does not by itself establish that a particular process is legally protected.
- **What the sources actually tell us:** The reviewed prospectus material has not yielded a verified inventory of specific software, datasets or other non-patent rights for this requirement. Source: [Ceribell: August 2024 S-1 with exhibits, public issuer archive](https://ceribell.gcs-web.com/static-files/2d3fe925-344d-40d7-b32c-cfeb9261c271). Relevant passage: Intellectual Property.
- **What this lets us write:** We cannot yet write a product-specific account of those assets. A general discussion of company IP does not identify which algorithm, data right or other non-patent asset the developer controls.
- **What we cannot yet answer:** We need specific documented software, data, trademark or know-how assets and evidence of the company's control over them.
- **Next investigation:** Look for an explicit company or contractual description of the relevant software, know-how, design or data rights and who controls them. Start with [Company and financing history](../docs/imperial-gap-searches.md#fin); the linked access log states what was actually tried and any access limits.

### What could limit that control or allow competition? (I6)

- **What the report needs to explain:** Identify relevant third-party rights, licences on which the developer depends and possible competing designs. Explain what the available records let us say about competition. Our search does not establish a legal right to commercialise every feature or prove freedom from infringement.
- **What the sources actually tell us:** Some royalty terms are redacted, and the original sonification rights are distinct from later AI technology. Source: [Ceribell/Stanford licence amendment: disclosed technology and milestones](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1861107/000095017024100574/cbll-ex10_08.htm); [Ceribell: August 2024 S-1 with exhibits, public issuer archive](https://ceribell.gcs-web.com/static-files/2d3fe925-344d-40d7-b32c-cfeb9261c271). Relevant passage: Licence economics; IP risks.
- **What this lets us write:** This shows why the licence and software generation matter to commercial control. It does not establish the full obligations or whether other rights constrain the system.
- **What we cannot yet answer:** We need relevant third-party claims, technical alternatives and the complete licence dependencies to make a bounded competition account.
- **Next investigation:** Investigate specific overlapping claims and licence dependencies; use technical alternatives to frame the unresolved question rather than declaring legal clearance. Start with [Patent and rights investigation](../docs/imperial-gap-searches.md#pat); the linked access log states what was actually tried and any access limits.

## Fundraising

### Was early research supported by grants? (F1)

- **What the report needs to explain:** For each relevant grant, establish who received it, the award identifier, dates, amount and purpose. Explain whether it supported a precursor laboratory, the company or this product. An acknowledgement in a paper can identify a lead without revealing the money attributable to the device.
- **What the sources actually tell us:** Stanford identifies a 2012 Bio-X Round 6 seed project linked to the work that became Ceribell. Source: [Stanford Bio-X: academic seed grant behind Ceribell](https://biox.stanford.edu/2019-seed-grants-news). Relevant passage: Ceribell project paragraph.
- **What this lets us write:** This is evidence of precursor academic support, not company equity. The reviewed account does not state a grant amount attributable to the product.
- **What we cannot yet answer:** We need the authoritative recipient, amount, dates and project purpose and a clear explanation of what early work it supported.
- **Next investigation:** Trace the acknowledgement to an authoritative award record and check its recipient, period, amount, purpose and relationship to this device. ### What would early development have cost if the budget is private? (F6)

- **What the report needs to explain:** Build a reasoned estimate using both approaches allowed by the specification. Add up sourced staffing, engineering, testing, trial and manufacturing costs from the bottom up, and compare with similar programmes from the top down. State assumptions and ranges. This estimates capital needed; it does not reconstruct undisclosed fundraising.
- **What the sources actually tell us:** No sourced numerical estimate of first-device development cost has been completed.
- **What this lets us write:** Even the unusually useful early financing record does not tell us the minimum resources required to develop the system.
- **What we cannot yet answer:** We need staffing, prototype, validation and manufacturing inputs, plus an appropriately matched rapid-EEG programme for the top-down comparison.
- **Next investigation:** Source staff time, prototype, testing, trial and production inputs, then cross-check the range against genuinely comparable development programmes. Start with [Capital estimate inputs](../docs/imperial-gap-searches.md#est); the linked access log states what was actually tried and any access limits.

## Regulatory

### Exactly which product and use are being regulated? (R1)

- **What the report needs to explain:** Define the hardware, software and accessories, the intended patients, user and care setting. Identify the original version being studied and separate later changes. This prevents evidence about a component or research system being applied to a different complete product.
- **What the sources actually tell us:** The original recorder and headband have 2017 records, while automated seizure-monitoring software appears in later records. Source: [FDA K170363: original Ceribell pocket EEG device](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cdrh_docs/pdf17/K170363.pdf); [FDA K171459: Ceribell EEG Headband](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cdrh_docs/pdf17/K171459.pdf); [FDA • CERIBELL K223504](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cdrh_docs/pdf22/K223504.pdf). Relevant passage: Descriptions and indications.
- **What this lets us write:** We can define each component and explain how the system evolved. The later software's claims and evidence must not be assigned to the original hardware.
- **What we cannot yet answer:** We need a complete configuration and label account for the specific generation the report chooses, including the limits of each software function.
- **Next investigation:** Retrieve the selected version's instructions and decision documents, then list the exact configuration, users, setting and intended use. Start with [Official records first](../docs/imperial-gap-searches.md#open); the linked access log states what was actually tried and any access limits.

### Why did it follow this regulatory route? (R2)

- **What the report needs to explain:** For each market discussed, explain the device classification and the applicable rule or product code, then connect the route to the device's function and risks. Evidence of a US route does not establish UK or EU authorisation.
- **What the sources actually tell us:** The reviewed US hardware and later EEG software records use Class II pathways. Source: [FDA K171459: Ceribell EEG Headband](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cdrh_docs/pdf17/K171459.pdf); [FDA • CERIBELL K223504](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cdrh_docs/pdf22/K223504.pdf). Relevant passage: Classification and predicate sections.
- **What this lets us write:** The classification must be attached to the individual function and component. It cannot be reduced to one claim that the whole brand has a universal approval.
- **What we cannot yet answer:** We need the appropriate non-US primary records if UK or EU classification or authorisation is discussed.
- **Next investigation:** Use the exact market's regulator or conformity records to establish the classification and route, explaining their connection to the device's function. Start with [Official records first](../docs/imperial-gap-searches.md#open); the linked access log states what was actually tried and any access limits.

- **What we cannot yet answer:** We need a consolidated account of exact dates, scopes and subsequent changes for each marketed component included in the report.
- **Next investigation:** Follow the original decision to any relevant supplements or later clearances, keeping each version and indication separate. Start with [Official records first](../docs/imperial-gap-searches.md#open); the linked access log states what was actually tried and any access limits.

Source: [Ceribell: August 2024 S-1 with exhibits, public issuer archive](https://ceribell.gcs-web.com/static-files/2d3fe925-344d-40d7-b32c-cfeb9261c271). - **What this lets us write:** This establishes a production split at the date of the disclosure. It does not identify every present contractual responsibility or later supplier change.
- **What we cannot yet answer:** We need the legal and contract-manufacturer roles, quality and release responsibilities, and any changes relevant to the selected period.
- **Next investigation:** Trace the actual supplier or manufacturing agreement and responsibility split, then verify which sites and arrangements apply to the selected period. Start with [Company and financing history](../docs/imperial-gap-searches.md#fin); the linked access log states what was actually tried and any access limits.

### What obligations and problems continued after launch? (R6)

- **What the report needs to explain:** Explain the relevant quality and surveillance duties and investigate product-specific recalls or reported incidents. State the database, model, terms and date searched. A general quality rule does not establish company compliance, and a missing search result does not establish that no problem occurred.
- **What the sources actually tell us:** The QMSR record supplies general US quality-system context, rather than a Ceribell incident history. Source: [FDA. Medical Devices; Quality System Regulation Amendments. 89 FR 7496-7525, document 2024-01709](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2024-02-02/html/2024-01709.htm). Relevant passage: Scope/effective date.
- **What this lets us write:** It can explain obligations but cannot establish compliance or prove that any particular hardware or software version had no problems.
- **What we cannot yet answer:** We need version-specific surveillance information and dated recall and incident searches with the models and terms recorded.
- **Next investigation:** Run and document the exact-model recall and incident searches, and distinguish reported events, proven causes and general quality obligations. Start with [Official records first](../docs/imperial-gap-searches.md#open); the linked access log states what was actually tried and any access limits.

## Reimbursement

### Who buys what, and at what price? (P1)

- **What the report needs to explain:** Describe the actual sale, lease, subscription or consumable arrangement, including the configuration, date and price. Separate the manufacturer's selling price from a hospital charge, a service payment or a modelled treatment cost.
- **What the sources actually tell us:** The system has a recorder, consumable headbands and software, but no verified current complete contract or configuration price was recovered. Source: [Ceribell: August 2024 S-1 with exhibits, public issuer archive](https://ceribell.gcs-web.com/static-files/2d3fe925-344d-40d7-b32c-cfeb9261c271). Relevant passage: Business model.
- **What this lets us write:** We can identify the items that may create initial and recurring costs. We cannot determine what a particular hospital actually buys or pays from the component list.
- **What we cannot yet answer:** We need the buyer, price date, hardware and consumable charges, software arrangement and included support for an actual configuration.
- **Next investigation:** Find a dated quotation, tender or contract for the selected configuration and clarify what equipment, service and consumables the amount includes. Start with [Actual service and purchasing evidence](../docs/imperial-gap-searches.md#service); the linked access log states what was actually tried and any access limits.

### Who pays for the patient's care? (P2)

- **What the report needs to explain:** Identify whether the relevant care is funded by a commissioner, insurer, hospital, patient, research grant or charity. Connect that source to a real setting. The perspective chosen by an economic model does not prove that the named payer actually funds the device.
- **What the sources actually tell us:** The reviewed payment material concerns US inpatient care; no actual hospital purchasing contract was obtained. Source: [CMS FY2027 IPPS final rule: NTAP expiry and new technology decisions](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2026-08-04/html/2026-15833.htm); [Ceribell: August 2024 S-1 with exhibits, public issuer archive](https://ceribell.gcs-web.com/static-files/2d3fe925-344d-40d7-b32c-cfeb9261c271). Relevant passage: Seizure-monitor NTAP; business model.
- **What this lets us write:** It helps explain how the care episode may be funded. It does not identify which department or budget bears the ongoing system costs.
- **What we cannot yet answer:** We need an actual hospital and payer arrangement, including who pays the supplier and who receives the service payment.
- **Next investigation:** Find the actual commissioning, insurance or service-funding arrangement and identify the patient episode and budget it covers. Start with [Costs and health-service evidence](../docs/imperial-gap-searches.md#cost); the linked access log states what was actually tried and any access limits.

### Under what rules and amounts is care paid for? (P3)

- **What the report needs to explain:** For a named payer, place, setting and year, separate billing codes, clinical coverage conditions and payment amounts. A code permits classification; it does not on its own promise coverage or a particular payment. Temporary support needs its own dates and conditions.
- **What the sources actually tell us:** The FY2027 rule ends the seizure-monitor's temporary NTAP support; the delirium technology has a separate decision. Source: [CMS FY2027 IPPS final rule: NTAP expiry and new technology decisions](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2026-08-04/html/2026-15833.htm). Relevant passage: II.E.4; II.E.6.
- **What this lets us write:** This prevents the report from treating the separate award as a renewal or the temporary supplement as permanent. Ending the supplement does not itself remove all underlying service payment.
- **What we cannot yet answer:** We need applicable base payment, codes, outpatient or private-payer terms and evidence of their relevance to the selected setting.
- **Next investigation:** Retrieve the applicable payer policy, code rules and fee or contract amount, checking dates, setting and patient conditions separately. Start with [Official records first](../docs/imperial-gap-searches.md#open); the linked access log states what was actually tried and any access limits.

### What extra costs and savings can be supported? (P4)

- **What the report needs to explain:** Compare the device pathway with the relevant alternative. Include equipment, implementation, staff, maintenance and downstream events, and state the perspective and time horizon. Explain which amounts are observed and which are model inputs; do not turn a hypothetical saving into a realised one.
- **What the sources actually tell us:** The economic model predicts $5,633 savings per use and savings in 75% of its simulations. Source: [Ney, Gururangan and Parvizi: economic model of Ceribell Rapid Response EEG](https://ceribell.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/ROI-Paper-Ney-241-p318-327-J-Med-Econ-2021-4.pdf). Relevant passage: Methods/results; 2019 USD.
- **What this lets us write:** We can explain the model's proposed cost difference and uncertainty. Those simulated results are not observed cash released by a hospital and depend on the authors' assumptions.
- **What we cannot yet answer:** We need local input costs, actual commercial terms and measured changes in resource use before applying the estimate to a London service.
- **Next investigation:** Retrieve the full economic methods and current local inputs, keeping purchase price, care cost and payment as separate quantities. Start with [Costs and health-service evidence](../docs/imperial-gap-searches.md#cost); the linked access log states what was actually tried and any access limits.

### What clinical evidence supports the economic assumptions? (P5)

- **What the report needs to explain:** Trace each proposed saving or health gain to evidence about an outcome or event frequency. Explain how a change in a measured endpoint leads to fewer admissions, less care or better quality of life. Faster setup or better signals do not automatically establish those later effects.
- **What the sources actually tell us:** DECIDE measures acquisition and decision-related outcomes rather than a causal mortality or length-of-stay effect. Source: [Vespa et al.: DECIDE prospective observational study](https://ceribell.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/Vespa-online-CCM-DECIDE-Study-2020-1-4.pdf); [Ney, Gururangan and Parvizi: economic model of Ceribell Rapid Response EEG](https://ceribell.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/ROI-Paper-Ney-241-p318-327-J-Med-Econ-2021-4.pdf). Relevant passage: Clinical endpoints; model assumptions.
- **What this lets us write:** This identifies the missing step between faster information and some economic claims. A recorded workflow improvement cannot be substituted for a measured reduction in expensive patient events.
- **What we cannot yet answer:** We need evidence of causal treatment and resource-use changes that can credibly supply the economic model's inputs.
- **Next investigation:** Find measured patient outcomes and resource events for the device, then check whether the economic calculation extrapolates beyond those measurements. Start with [Clinical literature](../docs/imperial-gap-searches.md#lit); the linked access log states what was actually tried and any access limits.

### Who has a practical reason to adopt it? (P6)

- **What the report needs to explain:** Identify who controls the budget, who pays the extra costs and who benefits, over what time period. Include staffing, capacity and throughput. A treatment can look worthwhile to the whole health system while leaving the hospital that implements it with an immediate cost.
- **What the sources actually tell us:** The existing analysis considers whether earlier information could release bed capacity without reducing fixed expenditure. Source: [Ney, Gururangan and Parvizi: economic model of Ceribell Rapid Response EEG](https://ceribell.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/ROI-Paper-Ney-241-p318-327-J-Med-Econ-2021-4.pdf). Relevant passage: Model plus analytical interpretation.
- **What this lets us write:** This is a useful adoption question, not a verified hospital saving. More available capacity and a lower cash budget are different outcomes.
- **What we cannot yet answer:** We need the actual budget holder, variable costs, throughput changes and the distribution of benefits between the hospital, payer and patient.
- **Next investigation:** Investigate the actual service budget, staff time, throughput and allocation of benefits with procurement records or appropriately scoped service enquiries. Start with [Actual service and purchasing evidence](../docs/imperial-gap-searches.md#service); the linked access log states what was actually tried and any access limits.

### How do the stakeholders and flows fit together? (P7)

- **What the report needs to explain:** Map the verified product, care, information, money and authority flows, and explain the participants' goals or tensions. Distinguish documented relationships from proposed ones. Use the lecture's required map format once it is available rather than assuming our existing diagram matches it.
- **What the sources actually tell us:** The draft maps Stanford's licence role, Ceribell, acute-care staff, hospital, patient and a payer subject to applicable rules. Source: [Ceribell/Stanford licence amendment: disclosed technology and milestones](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1861107/000095017024100574/cbll-ex10_08.htm); [Ceribell: August 2024 S-1 with exhibits, public issuer archive](https://ceribell.gcs-web.com/static-files/2d3fe925-344d-40d7-b32c-cfeb9261c271). Relevant passage: Role premises; draft map.
- **What this lets us write:** Some relationships have documents; the full payment and authority flows remain analytical. The map does not verify present purchasing arrangements.
- **What we cannot yet answer:** We need current contractual and decision-making relationships and the lecture's required map format.
- **Next investigation:** Retrieve the actual lecture instructions and verify the case's purchasing and care relationships before treating an analytical diagram as the required final map. Start with [Course map format](../docs/imperial-gap-searches.md#course); the linked access log states what was actually tried and any access limits.
