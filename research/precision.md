# Precision Neuroscience — Layer 7-T: what we can write, and what is missing

Precision’s Layer 7-T is a flexible electrode placed on the brain surface for temporary recording, monitoring or stimulation during neurosurgical care. The evidence reviewed concerns this temporary component, not a permanent home-use brain–computer interface. Its FDA record and small acute study explain the product and early feasibility; they leave its effect on patient outcomes and hospital costs unresolved.

This guide works through the five report sections for a teammate deciding what investigation is still needed. Each question explains the requirement, the actual evidence, the conclusion it supports, the unanswered part and a way to investigate it. Evidence limitations are explained in sentences rather than assigned a grade.

[Device-by-device overview](README.md) · [The shared requirements](../docs/report-evidence-requirements.md) · [What Imperial access actually recovered](../docs/imperial-gap-searches.md).

The evidence comes from the existing 19–21 October research. This revision clarifies that evidence; it adds no new retrievals. The next investigations below are proposals unless the access log explicitly records otherwise.

## Unmet medical need

### Who could actually receive this device? (N1)

- **What the report needs to explain:** Explain the condition and the specific patients or procedures the device is intended for. Then give the number who meet those criteria in a named country and year. Explain why a count of everyone with the disease would include people who cannot receive this device.
- **What the sources actually tell us:** The FDA record describes Layer 7-T as a temporary cortical electrode used for less than 30 days. It does not describe permanent assistive use by everyone with paralysis. Source: [FDA • PRECISION K242618](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cdrh_docs/pdf24/K242618.pdf). Relevant passage: Indications for use.
- **What this lets us write:** This lets us define the relevant population as people undergoing procedures that need temporary cortical recording or stimulation. A paralysis prevalence figure would count many people outside this product's use.
- **What we cannot yet answer:** We do not yet have the annual number of eligible procedures in a specified country, or the proportion of those procedures in which this array could be used.
- **Next investigation:** Find a population study or service record that applies the device's actual eligibility criteria, and record the country, year and exclusions. Start with [Clinical literature](../docs/imperial-gap-searches.md#lit); the linked access log states what was actually tried and any access limits.

### Where would the device enter existing care? (N2)

- **What the report needs to explain:** Describe what happens to a patient before, during and after the relevant treatment or investigation. Identify the decision the device changes, the usual alternative and the staff or equipment needed at that point.
- **What the sources actually tell us:** The electrode array is used with external recording, monitoring or stimulation equipment during neurosurgical care. Source: [FDA • PRECISION K242618](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cdrh_docs/pdf24/K242618.pdf). Relevant passage: Device description and predicate.
- **What this lets us write:** We can explain where the component connects to a surgical workflow. The record does not reconstruct the whole patient pathway or show which clinical decision changes when it is used.
- **What we cannot yet answer:** We need a description of the usual mapping procedure, who performs it, the equipment it replaces or supplements, and the decision at which its extra information matters.
- **Next investigation:** Find a guideline or actual service description that traces the relevant patient pathway and the decision where this device would enter. Start with [Clinical literature](../docs/imperial-gap-searches.md#lit); the linked access log states what was actually tried and any access limits.

### What specifically goes wrong with existing care? (N3)

- **What the report needs to explain:** Show a measured problem with the care available before the device: for example, continuing symptoms, missed diagnoses, waiting time or patients unable to access treatment. Explain how often it happens. A later trial of the new device does not by itself measure the original care shortfall.
- **What the sources actually tell us:** The reviewed five-patient pilot shows acute cortical recording with the array. It does not report the rate of failures in the older mapping pathway. Source: [Hettick et al.: cortical-array technology and acute human pilot](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41551-025-01501-w). Relevant passage: Human clinical pilot.
- **What this lets us write:** The pilot is evidence that the new component can record signals in these cases. It cannot establish how often existing mapping was inadequate or how much of that problem the component could solve.
- **What we cannot yet answer:** We still need measured rates of inadequate mapping, localisation errors or delays, together with the consequences of those failures for the relevant procedures.
- **Next investigation:** Find a study of the existing pathway that measures the failure or access problem, preferably from the period when development began. Start with [Clinical literature](../docs/imperial-gap-searches.md#lit); the linked access log states what was actually tried and any access limits.

### What are the consequences of that problem? (N4)

- **What the report needs to explain:** Explain how the care shortfall affects patients and services. Connect it to outcomes, staff time, hospital use or costs, and identify which groups carry that burden. A large disease-cost figure needs a reasoned connection to the patients and problem this device addresses.
- **What the sources actually tell us:** No verified study in the reviewed evidence measures the baseline patient harm or care costs attributable to the mapping problem addressed by Layer 7-T.
- **What this lets us write:** We cannot quantify the unmet need in money or patient harm from the component's signal quality alone. The section can identify this as an unanswered part of the case.
- **What we cannot yet answer:** We need evidence connecting inadequate mapping to outcomes, operating-theatre time or other resource use, with costs for a named setting and year.
- **Next investigation:** Find measured patient consequences and resource use for the relevant subgroup, then identify the price year and setting of any cost estimate. Start with [Costs and health-service evidence](../docs/imperial-gap-searches.md#cost); the linked access log states what was actually tried and any access limits.

### What benefit has this particular device demonstrated? (N5)

- **What the report needs to explain:** Explain how the device could change the identified problem and what has actually been measured. Distinguish a technical result, a change in a clinical decision and an improvement patients experience. State the study population, comparison and follow-up so the reader can see how far the finding reaches.
- **What the sources actually tell us:** The acute study involved five patients: two under anaesthesia and three undergoing awake mapping. It reports placement and recording feasibility. Source: [Hettick et al.: cortical-array technology and acute human pilot](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41551-025-01501-w). Relevant passage: Human pilot; funding/conflicts.
- **What this lets us write:** We can describe an early human demonstration and its setting. Five acute cases, with company interests involved, do not establish comparative patient benefit, reliable complication rates or long-term outcomes.
- **What we cannot yet answer:** We need a comparison showing whether the array changes clinical decisions and improves patient outcomes, with enough patients and follow-up to assess harms as well as benefit.
- **Next investigation:** Read the original device study for its comparator, measured endpoints, follow-up, adverse events and limitations; look for the missing patient outcome rather than another mechanism description. Start with [Clinical literature](../docs/imperial-gap-searches.md#lit); the linked access log states what was actually tried and any access limits.

### What other options could meet the same need? (N6)

- **What the report needs to explain:** Compare options available to the same patients at the same point in care. Explain their different benefits, risks and practical demands. A product used as a regulatory comparison is not automatically the most appropriate clinical alternative.
- **What the sources actually tell us:** The FDA record identifies a cortical-electrode predicate used in the substantial-equivalence comparison. Source: [FDA • PRECISION K242618](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cdrh_docs/pdf24/K242618.pdf). Relevant passage: Predicate and performance comparison.
- **What this lets us write:** This gives us a specific earlier product to investigate. A regulatory comparison does not establish that it is the best clinical alternative or that the new array improves outcomes over conventional grids and strips.
- **What we cannot yet answer:** We need a clinical and workflow comparison with the relevant conventional electrodes, including mapping performance, practical limitations and patient consequences.
- **Next investigation:** Find alternatives for the same patient group and compare their eligibility, outcomes, risks and service requirements. Start with [Clinical literature](../docs/imperial-gap-searches.md#lit); the linked access log states what was actually tried and any access limits.

## Intellectual property

### Who made the original invention, and where? (I1)

- **What the report needs to explain:** Identify the inventors of the technology that became the selected device, their employers when the invention arose and the earliest relevant publication or patent priority. A current university biography is not evidence of where an inventor worked at that earlier date.
- **What the sources actually tell us:** The selected application, US20230210427A1, names Rapoport, Papageorgiou, Hettick, Poole and Ho and records a 31 December 2021 priority date. Source: [US20230210427A1: neural interfaces and insertion system](https://patents.google.com/patent/US20230210427A1/en). Relevant passage: Bibliographic metadata.
- **What this lets us write:** We can identify the people and timing associated with this candidate invention. We cannot yet treat the application as a complete account of the first marketed component's origin.
- **What we cannot yet answer:** We need evidence of each inventor's employer when the invention arose and confirmation that this is the appropriate original-device family.
- **Next investigation:** Trace the original inventors and predecessor organisations using patent priority records and contemporaneous employment or university records. Start with [Patent and rights investigation](../docs/imperial-gap-searches.md#pat); the linked access log states what was actually tried and any access limits.

### How did the company obtain the right to use it? (I2)

- **What the report needs to explain:** Explain who originally owned the invention and how rights reached the developer through assignment, acquisition or licence. Identify the records supporting each step. A shared founder, research collaboration or patent applicant does not establish every transfer or licence term.
- **What the sources actually tell us:** The application's bibliographic and assignment information identifies Precision as an ownership lead. Source: [US20230210427A1: neural interfaces and insertion system](https://patents.google.com/patent/US20230210427A1/en). Relevant passage: Assignment metadata.
- **What this lets us write:** This supports investigating company control of the invention. It does not establish every original assignment or prove that a university licence was required or obtained.
- **What we cannot yet answer:** We need the official transfer history and any relevant employment, invention-assignment or licence records linking the original rights to the developer.
- **Next investigation:** Retrieve the actual assignment or licence record and follow the original owner's rights into the developer, leaving unavailable contractual terms explicit. Start with [Patent and rights investigation](../docs/imperial-gap-searches.md#pat); the linked access log states what was actually tried and any access limits.

### Which patent claims correspond to the device's features? (I3)

- **What the report needs to explain:** Explain each relevant independent claim in ordinary language, then compare its required features with documented features of the selected product. An independent claim sets out a combination of features without relying on another claim. Identify any feature that cannot yet be matched.
- **What the sources actually tell us:** Independent claims 1 and 5 concern the array and insertion; claim 13 concerns fabrication; claim 17 extends to decoding and control of another device. Source: [US20230210427A1: neural interfaces and insertion system](https://patents.google.com/patent/US20230210427A1/en). Relevant passage: Independent claims 1, 5, 13, 17.
- **What this lets us write:** We can explain that these claims address different technical combinations. Claim 17's wider brain-interface use must not be presented as proof that the temporary component is authorised or configured for that complete use.
- **What we cannot yet answer:** We need to match every required claim feature to documented Layer 7-T features and identify which claimed features belong to a wider system rather than the marketed component.
- **Next investigation:** Compare the exact publication's independent claims with technical drawings or instructions for the selected product version, one required feature at a time. Start with [Patent and rights investigation](../docs/imperial-gap-searches.md#pat); the linked access log states what was actually tried and any access limits.

### Where and when does that protection apply? (I4)

- **What the report needs to explain:** Identify the relevant patent family, priority date and countries, and check the dated status of the particular application or granted patent. An application, an expired patent and a live grant answer different questions. Later amendments may change the claims.
- **What the sources actually tell us:** The reviewed document is a published US application with a 2021 priority date. Source: [US20230210427A1: neural interfaces and insertion system](https://patents.google.com/patent/US20230210427A1/en). Relevant passage: Priority/publication metadata.
- **What this lets us write:** This establishes the type and date of the text we read. It does not establish that these exact claims were granted, remain enforceable or apply in other countries.
- **What we cannot yet answer:** We need dated official status, any granted or amended claims, and the national members of the relevant patent family.
- **Next investigation:** Check the correct family and national patent registers for dated status, granted text and amendments rather than relying only on an aggregator's summary. Start with [Patent and rights investigation](../docs/imperial-gap-searches.md#pat); the linked access log states what was actually tried and any access limits.

### What else can the company protect or control? (I5)

- **What the report needs to explain:** Look for documented software rights, designs, trademarks, confidential know-how or data rights that matter to the device. Explain what the company controls and why it could matter commercially. Owning a factory does not by itself establish that a particular process is legally protected.
- **What the sources actually tell us:** The foundry acquisition documents fabrication capability under Precision's control. It does not identify a specific protected manufacturing secret. Source: [Precision: acquisition of a US MEMS manufacturing facility](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2023/10/05/2755458/0/en/precision-neuroscience-acquires-u-s-manufacturing-facility-and-receives-fda-breakthrough-device-designation.html). Relevant passage: Acquisition announcement.
- **What this lets us write:** We can discuss control of an engineering resource. Calling that resource proprietary know-how requires evidence about the process, rights or confidentiality arrangements.
- **What we cannot yet answer:** We need documented software, design, process or know-how protection and an explanation of who controls those rights.
- **Next investigation:** Look for an explicit company or contractual description of the relevant software, know-how, design or data rights and who controls them. Start with [Company and financing history](../docs/imperial-gap-searches.md#fin); the linked access log states what was actually tried and any access limits.

### What could limit that control or allow competition? (I6)

- **What the report needs to explain:** Identify relevant third-party rights, licences on which the developer depends and possible competing designs. Explain what the available records let us say about competition. Our search does not establish a legal right to commercialise every feature or prove freedom from infringement.
- **What the sources actually tell us:** The selected application is technically relevant, but the reviewed material does not assess overlapping third-party rights. Source: [US20230210427A1: neural interfaces and insertion system](https://patents.google.com/patent/US20230210427A1/en). Relevant passage: Claims and product-mapping boundary.
- **What this lets us write:** We can explain the company's candidate protection without claiming it is free to use every feature. The existence of its own application does not answer the separate infringement question.
- **What we cannot yet answer:** We need relevant competitor claims, possible alternative designs and any licences on which commercialisation depends.
- **Next investigation:** Investigate specific overlapping claims and licence dependencies; use technical alternatives to frame the unresolved question rather than declaring legal clearance. Start with [Patent and rights investigation](../docs/imperial-gap-searches.md#pat); the linked access log states what was actually tried and any access limits.

## Regulatory

### Exactly which product and use are being regulated? (R1)

- **What the report needs to explain:** Define the hardware, software and accessories, the intended patients, user and care setting. Identify the original version being studied and separate later changes. This prevents evidence about a component or research system being applied to a different complete product.
- **What the sources actually tell us:** The original FDA record concerns a temporary cortical electrode used with appropriate external equipment. Source: [FDA • PRECISION K242618](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cdrh_docs/pdf24/K242618.pdf). Relevant passage: Intended use and system description.
- **What this lets us write:** This lets the report define the regulated component. It prevents the wider permanent brain-interface ambition from being substituted for the cleared product.
- **What we cannot yet answer:** We need a complete account of the relevant accessories and users, and separate records for any later changes to the product or intended use.
- **Next investigation:** Retrieve the selected version's instructions and decision documents, then list the exact configuration, users, setting and intended use. ## Reimbursement

### Who buys what, and at what price? (P1)
