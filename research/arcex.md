# ONWARD Medical — ARC-EX: what we can write, and what is missing

ONWARD Medical’s ARC-EX applies external spinal stimulation alongside rehabilitation to improve hand function in selected people with chronic incomplete cervical spinal cord injury. It is separate from ONWARD’s implanted and brain-interface programmes. Regulatory documents, a rehabilitation study and nearby service connections help explain the case, while the original patent match, attributable early funding and payment arrangements remain unresolved.

This guide works through the five report sections for a teammate deciding what investigation is still needed. Each question explains the requirement, the actual evidence, the conclusion it supports, the unanswered part and a way to investigate it. Evidence limitations are explained in sentences rather than assigned a grade.

[Device-by-device overview](README.md) · [The shared requirements](../docs/report-evidence-requirements.md) · [What Imperial access actually recovered](../docs/imperial-gap-searches.md).

The evidence comes from the existing 19–21 October research. This revision clarifies that evidence; it adds no new retrievals. The next investigations below are proposals unless the access log explicitly records otherwise.

## Unmet medical need

### Who could actually receive this device? (N1)

- **What the report needs to explain:** Explain the condition and the specific patients or procedures the device is intended for. Then give the number who meet those criteria in a named country and year. Explain why a count of everyone with the disease would include people who cannot receive this device.
- **What the sources actually tell us:** The original label concerns people aged 18–75 with chronic incomplete C2–C8 spinal cord injury. A UK account separately estimates 105,000 people living with spinal cord injury and 4,400 new cases annually. Source: [FDA: ARC-EX original De Novo classification and evidence review](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cdrh_docs/reviews/DEN240014.pdf); [Spinal Injuries Association: revised UK spinal cord injury estimates](https://www.spinal.co.uk/news/new-spinal-cord-injury-data-revealed/). Relevant passage: Indication; SIA scope/data explanation.
- **What this lets us write:** The broad numbers describe disease burden, not the group eligible for this external stimulation and training programme.
- **What we cannot yet answer:** We need the UK number meeting the injury level, completeness, chronicity and training criteria, in a stated year.
- **Next investigation:** Find a population study or service record that applies the device's actual eligibility criteria, and record the country, year and exclusions. Start with [Clinical literature](../docs/imperial-gap-searches.md#lit); the linked access log states what was actually tried and any access limits.

### Where would the device enter existing care? (N2)

- **What the report needs to explain:** Describe what happens to a patient before, during and after the relevant treatment or investigation. Identify the decision the device changes, the usual alternative and the staff or equipment needed at that point.
- **What the sources actually tell us:** Up-LIFT used a rehabilitation-only phase followed by rehabilitation with stimulation. The provider describes intensive functional practice. Source: [Moritz et al.: Up-LIFT pivotal trial, Nature Medicine](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41591-024-02940-9); [Neurokinex: ARC-EX spinal-cord stimulation service](https://neurokinex.org/spinal-cord-stimulation/). Relevant passage: Study design; service description.
- **What this lets us write:** We can explain the actual study sequence and why therapy delivery matters. The research schedule does not establish every local referral and long-term care arrangement.
- **What we cannot yet answer:** We need local selection and exclusion rules, session intensity and the pathway before and after treatment.
- **Next investigation:** Find a guideline or actual service description that traces the relevant patient pathway and the decision where this device would enter. Start with [Actual service and purchasing evidence](../docs/imperial-gap-searches.md#service); the linked access log states what was actually tried and any access limits.

### What specifically goes wrong with existing care? (N3)

- **What the report needs to explain:** Show a measured problem with the care available before the device: for example, continuing symptoms, missed diagnoses, waiting time or patients unable to access treatment. Explain how often it happens. A later trial of the new device does not by itself measure the original care shortfall.
- **What the sources actually tell us:** Up-LIFT enrolled 65 people, analysed 60 completers and reported 43 meeting the combined strength and function endpoint. Source: [Moritz et al.: Up-LIFT pivotal trial, Nature Medicine](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41591-024-02940-9). Relevant passage: Primary endpoint; design.
- **What this lets us write:** This is a later change after stimulation was added. The sequential design does not isolate the stimulation effect or count the patients failed by existing rehabilitation.
- **What we cannot yet answer:** We need the baseline unmet functional goals and a controlled estimate of the added benefit over comparable rehabilitation alone.
- **Next investigation:** Find a study of the existing pathway that measures the failure or access problem, preferably from the period when development began. Start with [Clinical literature](../docs/imperial-gap-searches.md#lit); the linked access log states what was actually tried and any access limits.

### What are the consequences of that problem? (N4)

- **What the report needs to explain:** Explain how the care shortfall affects patients and services. Connect it to outcomes, staff time, hospital use or costs, and identify which groups carry that burden. A large disease-cost figure needs a reasoned connection to the patients and problem this device addresses.
- **What the sources actually tell us:** The reviewed evidence does not verify a reduction in care hours or costs caused by ARC-EX. Source: [Spinal Injuries Association: revised UK spinal cord injury estimates](https://www.spinal.co.uk/news/new-spinal-cord-injury-data-revealed/). Relevant passage: Burden context; no device cost-effect.
- **What this lets us write:** A large overall spinal-injury burden cannot be presented as money recoverable through this device. The economic consequence of better hand function still needs measurement.
- **What we cannot yet answer:** We need changes in independence, assistance and other resource use and the corresponding UK costs.
- **Next investigation:** Find measured patient consequences and resource use for the relevant subgroup, then identify the price year and setting of any cost estimate. Start with [Costs and health-service evidence](../docs/imperial-gap-searches.md#cost); the linked access log states what was actually tried and any access limits.

### What benefit has this particular device demonstrated? (N5)

- **What the report needs to explain:** Explain how the device could change the identified problem and what has actually been measured. Distinguish a technical result, a change in a clinical decision and an improvement patients experience. State the study population, comparison and follow-up so the reader can see how far the finding reaches.
- **What the sources actually tell us:** External stimulation accompanies task practice; 43 of 60 Up-LIFT completers met the combined endpoint. The Pathfinder2 abstract reports no significant group change in its neurological impairment score. Source: [Moritz et al.: Up-LIFT pivotal trial, Nature Medicine](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41591-024-02940-9); [Suggitt et al.: community rehabilitation Pathfinder2 pilot](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39998450/). Relevant passage: Endpoints; Pathfinder2 abstract.
- **What this lets us write:** We can distinguish improvement in particular functional measures from universal neurological recovery. The sequential study and abstract-level findings do not establish a durable causal effect for every patient.
- **What we cannot yet answer:** We need controlled incremental effects, follow-up durability and evidence of broader patient benefits.
- **Next investigation:** Read the original device study for its comparator, measured endpoints, follow-up, adverse events and limitations; look for the missing patient outcome rather than another mechanism description. Start with [Clinical literature](../docs/imperial-gap-searches.md#lit); the linked access log states what was actually tried and any access limits.

### What other options could meet the same need? (N6)

- **What the report needs to explain:** Compare options available to the same patients at the same point in care. Explain their different benefits, risks and practical demands. A product used as a regulatory comparison is not automatically the most appropriate clinical alternative.
- **What the sources actually tell us:** The rehabilitation-only phase supplies one comparison. ONWARD's implanted and brain-interface programmes are different technologies. Source: [Moritz et al.: Up-LIFT pivotal trial, Nature Medicine](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41591-024-02940-9); [ONWARD: prospectus dated 21 March 2024](https://ir.onwd.com/static-files/48d3e9a2-73bf-40be-a385-d37e7f1cc9bd). Relevant passage: Study phases; programmes.
- **What this lets us write:** We can compare external stimulation with the care it supplements. We cannot import an implanted device's benefits, risks or evidence into this product.
- **What we cannot yet answer:** We need matched alternative rehabilitation and stimulation strategies and their practical, clinical and eligibility trade-offs.
- **Next investigation:** Find alternatives for the same patient group and compare their eligibility, outcomes, risks and service requirements. Start with [Clinical literature](../docs/imperial-gap-searches.md#lit); the linked access log states what was actually tried and any access limits.

## Intellectual property

### Who made the original invention, and where? (I1)

- **What the report needs to explain:** Identify the inventors of the technology that became the selected device, their employers when the invention arose and the earliest relevant publication or patent priority. A current university biography is not evidence of where an inventor worked at that earlier date.
- **What the sources actually tell us:** The 2015 precursor paper links Edgerton and Gerasimenko, an NRT-supplied stimulator and licensed University of California IP. Source: [Gerasimenko et al.: transcutaneous spinal-cord stimulation in humans](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5021439/). Relevant passage: Financial/IP disclosures.
- **What this lets us write:** This documents a specific precursor lineage. It does not name every inventor and employer associated with the exact marketed ARC-EX configuration.
- **What we cannot yet answer:** We need contemporaneous employment and the original invention families that actually underpin the external product.
- **Next investigation:** Trace the original inventors and predecessor organisations using patent priority records and contemporaneous employment or university records. Start with [Patent and rights investigation](../docs/imperial-gap-searches.md#pat); the linked access log states what was actually tried and any access limits.

### How did the company obtain the right to use it? (I2)

- **What the report needs to explain:** Explain who originally owned the invention and how rights reached the developer through assignment, acquisition or licence. Identify the records supporting each step. A shared founder, research collaboration or patent applicant does not establish every transfer or licence term.
- **What the sources actually tell us:** The merger release and prospectus connect NeuroRecovery Technologies and university licensing to ONWARD. Source: [GTX and NeuroRecovery Technologies: 2019 merger announcement](https://www.gimv.com/sites/default/files/media/2019-10/20191022_English_Press-release-merger-GTX-medical-NeuroRecovery-Technologies.pdf); [ONWARD: prospectus dated 21 March 2024](https://ir.onwd.com/static-files/48d3e9a2-73bf-40be-a385-d37e7f1cc9bd). Relevant passage: Licensing/merger disclosures.
- **What this lets us write:** This gives a corporate and licensing route to investigate. It does not reveal the complete ARC-EX family schedule or every original assignment.
- **What we cannot yet answer:** We need the actual relevant licence schedules, original transfers and terms connecting those rights to the external system.
- **Next investigation:** Retrieve the actual assignment or licence record and follow the original owner's rights into the developer, leaving unavailable contractual terms explicit. Start with [Patent and rights investigation](../docs/imperial-gap-searches.md#pat); the linked access log states what was actually tried and any access limits.

### Which patent claims correspond to the device's features? (I3)

- **What the report needs to explain:** Explain each relevant independent claim in ordinary language, then compare its required features with documented features of the selected product. An independent claim sets out a combination of features without relying on another claim. Identify any feature that cannot yet be matched.
- **What the sources actually tell us:** Claim 1 of US10806927B2 uses locomotor, postural and bladder-function wording, although other text discusses grasping or reaching. Source: [US10806927B2: transcutaneous spinal cord stimulation](https://patents.google.com/patent/US10806927B2/en). Relevant passage: Independent claim 1.
- **What this lets us write:** A related mechanism and later hand-function language are not enough to establish that this independent claim covers the marketed hand-function product.
- **What we cannot yet answer:** We need the correct original product family and a complete independent-claim-to-feature match; the present candidate cannot settle that requirement.
- **Next investigation:** Compare the exact publication's independent claims with technical drawings or instructions for the selected product version, one required feature at a time. Start with [Patent and rights investigation](../docs/imperial-gap-searches.md#pat); the linked access log states what was actually tried and any access limits.

### Where and when does that protection apply? (I4)

- **What the report needs to explain:** Identify the relevant patent family, priority date and countries, and check the dated status of the particular application or granted patent. An application, an expired patent and a live grant answer different questions. Later amendments may change the claims.
- **What the sources actually tell us:** The candidate family has November 2011 priority and an October 2020 grant publication. Source: [US10806927B2: transcutaneous spinal cord stimulation](https://patents.google.com/patent/US10806927B2/en). Relevant passage: Bibliographic metadata.
- **What this lets us write:** This is a history for the candidate we read, not a verified map of ARC-EX protection. The unresolved product match carries into any geographical claim.
- **What we cannot yet answer:** We need the applicable licensed families, official dated status and jurisdictions for the actual external product.
- **Next investigation:** Check the correct family and national patent registers for dated status, granted text and amendments rather than relying only on an aggregator's summary. Start with [Patent and rights investigation](../docs/imperial-gap-searches.md#pat); the linked access log states what was actually tried and any access limits.

### What else can the company protect or control? (I5)

- **What the report needs to explain:** Look for documented software rights, designs, trademarks, confidential know-how or data rights that matter to the device. Explain what the company controls and why it could matter commercially. Owning a factory does not by itself establish that a particular process is legally protected.
- **What the sources actually tell us:** ARC-EX uses software, programming and electrodes, but no specific non-patent-rights inventory was verified. Source: [ONWARD: ARC-EX professional EU manual, Rev 5](https://www.onwd.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/007351_IFU_Professional_EU_ARC-EX-EN-EXIFU01PROEUEN_v5.pdf). Relevant passage: System/components.
- **What this lets us write:** The presence of these components creates ownership questions; it does not answer them. We cannot infer control of data or know-how from product integration.
- **What we cannot yet answer:** We need documented software, design, trademark, data or know-how rights and who owns or licenses them.
- **Next investigation:** Look for an explicit company or contractual description of the relevant software, know-how, design or data rights and who controls them. Start with [Company and financing history](../docs/imperial-gap-searches.md#fin); the linked access log states what was actually tried and any access limits.

### What could limit that control or allow competition? (I6)

- **What the report needs to explain:** Identify relevant third-party rights, licences on which the developer depends and possible competing designs. Explain what the available records let us say about competition. Our search does not establish a legal right to commercialise every feature or prove freedom from infringement.
- **What the sources actually tell us:** The commercial history involves a merged company and academic licences with incomplete schedules in this review. Source: [ONWARD: prospectus dated 21 March 2024](https://ir.onwd.com/static-files/48d3e9a2-73bf-40be-a385-d37e7f1cc9bd); [GTX and NeuroRecovery Technologies: 2019 merger announcement](https://www.gimv.com/sites/default/files/media/2019-10/20191022_English_Press-release-merger-GTX-medical-NeuroRecovery-Technologies.pdf). Relevant passage: Licence and company history.
- **What this lets us write:** These may be important dependencies, but the records do not establish all field restrictions, sublicences or competing rights.
- **What we cannot yet answer:** We need the relevant third-party claims and licence conditions, including restrictions on the fields in which rights may be used.
- **Next investigation:** Investigate specific overlapping claims and licence dependencies; use technical alternatives to frame the unresolved question rather than declaring legal clearance. Start with [Patent and rights investigation](../docs/imperial-gap-searches.md#pat); the linked access log states what was actually tried and any access limits.

## Fundraising

### Was early research supported by grants? (F1)

- **What the report needs to explain:** For each relevant grant, establish who received it, the award identifier, dates, amount and purpose. Explain whether it supported a precursor laboratory, the company or this product. An acknowledgement in a paper can identify a lead without revealing the money attributable to the device.
- **What the sources actually tell us:** The precursor paper prints NIH acknowledgements including U01EB15521 and R01EB007615 and names Reeve and other support. Source: [Gerasimenko et al.: transcutaneous spinal-cord stimulation in humans](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5021439/). Relevant passage: Acknowledgements; spelling as printed.
- **What this lets us write:** These are leads to authoritative awards, not verified ARC-EX funding amounts. The identifier and product allocation must be checked against the original award record.
- **What we cannot yet answer:** We need verified award identifiers, recipients, periods, amounts and the portion of work genuinely attributable to the external system.
- **Next investigation:** Trace the acknowledgement to an authoritative award record and check its recipient, period, amount, purpose and relationship to this device. Start with [Awards and original research support](../docs/imperial-gap-searches.md#grant); the linked access log states what was actually tried and any access limits.

### What money did investors actually provide? (F2)

- **What the report needs to explain:** Reconstruct the relevant early investment: who invested, when it closed, the currency, amount, type of security and any disclosed valuation. Separate money offered from money sold or received, and distinguish a company fundraising total from money allocated to this product.
- **What the sources actually tell us:** The prospectus records €5 million from former NRT investors in the merger context. Source: [ONWARD: prospectus dated 21 March 2024](https://ir.onwd.com/static-files/48d3e9a2-73bf-40be-a385-d37e7f1cc9bd); [EPFL: G-Therapeutics raises EUR36m in 2016](https://actu.epfl.ch/news/g-therapeutics-raises-eur-36-million/). Relevant passage: Merger funding; EPFL programme description.
- **What this lets us write:** This is more relevant to the external-device lineage than the excluded €36 million implanted-programme package. It is still not a complete account of the earliest NRT financing or a device budget.
- **What we cannot yet answer:** We need the earliest external-programme rounds, investors, closing terms and valuations and any attributable product allocation.
- **Next investigation:** Recover the original transaction or filing and check the closing, investors, instrument, proceeds and valuation basis; keep undisclosed fields separate. Start with [Company and financing history](../docs/imperial-gap-searches.md#fin); the linked access log states what was actually tried and any access limits.

### What other resources helped development? (F3)

- **What the report needs to explain:** Investigate founder contributions, loans, convertible instruments, partner support, incubator facilities and in-kind resources. Explain what was actually provided and under what arrangement. Do not count an acquisition the company paid for as investment received by the company.
- **What the sources actually tell us:** The €10 million 2016 innovation loan belongs to the implant programme. Later UK charity device loans concern access rather than original development cash. Source: [EPFL: G-Therapeutics raises EUR36m in 2016](https://actu.epfl.ch/news/g-therapeutics-raises-eur-36-million/); [Spinal Research: ARC-EX Real World Programme launch](https://spinal-research.org/news/spinal-research-launches-arc-ex-real-world-programme/). Relevant passage: Programme funding descriptions.
- **What this lets us write:** These facts help prevent unrelated money or equipment access from being counted as ARC-EX's original finance.
- **What we cannot yet answer:** We need founder, debt, in-kind and partnership resources specifically supporting external-system development.
- **Next investigation:** Look for the relevant founder, debt, partner or acquisition record and distinguish cash received, cash spent and non-cash support. Start with [Company and financing history](../docs/imperial-gap-searches.md#fin); the linked access log states what was actually tried and any access limits.

### What development work was the money intended to fund? (F4)

- **What the report needs to explain:** Connect the funding to the next prototype, test, trial, manufacturing step or market-entry milestone. State whether the source reports an intention, an allocated budget or actual expenditure. A round announcement alone does not show how the company divided its money.
- **What the sources actually tell us:** The academic, merger and service-access records describe support at different stages. Source: [Gerasimenko et al.: transcutaneous spinal-cord stimulation in humans](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5021439/); [ONWARD: prospectus dated 21 March 2024](https://ir.onwd.com/static-files/48d3e9a2-73bf-40be-a385-d37e7f1cc9bd). Relevant passage: Acknowledgements; merger context.
- **What this lets us write:** They identify possible resources but do not allocate commercial ARC-EX development spending or demonstrate the use of particular proceeds.
- **What we cannot yet answer:** We need a company or project account connecting identified resources to engineering, trials, supplier setup and other development milestones.
- **Next investigation:** Find the stated use of proceeds or project allocation and connect it to a named development milestone without treating the announcement as an invoice. Start with [Company and financing history](../docs/imperial-gap-searches.md#fin); the linked access log states what was actually tried and any access limits.

### What was delivered after the funding? (F5)

- **What the report needs to explain:** Place funding events and documented development outputs on the same timeline. Identify evidence of a prototype, study, regulatory decision or first sale. Timing can show that funding preceded an outcome, but additional evidence is needed to claim the funding paid for that outcome.
- **What the sources actually tell us:** The record runs from precursor work in 2015 through the 2019 merger, 2024 authorisation and 2025 home-use extension. Source: [GTX and NeuroRecovery Technologies: 2019 merger announcement](https://www.gimv.com/sites/default/files/media/2019-10/20191022_English_Press-release-merger-GTX-medical-NeuroRecovery-Technologies.pdf); [FDA: ARC-EX original De Novo classification and evidence review](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cdrh_docs/reviews/DEN240014.pdf); [FDA: ARC-EX 510(k) extending the indication to home use](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cdrh_docs/pdf25/K251821.pdf). Relevant passage: Event/decision dates.
- **What this lets us write:** This provides a development and corporate timeline. Continuity of organisations does not prove which funding paid for each output.
- **What we cannot yet answer:** We need attributable links between resources and the prototype, Up-LIFT trial, manufacturing setup and authorisation work.
- **Next investigation:** Find a dated output and a source linking the relevant resources to that work; distinguish the documented sequence from a causal funding claim. Start with [Company and financing history](../docs/imperial-gap-searches.md#fin); the linked access log states what was actually tried and any access limits.

### What would early development have cost if the budget is private? (F6)

- **What the report needs to explain:** Build a reasoned estimate using both approaches allowed by the specification. Add up sourced staffing, engineering, testing, trial and manufacturing costs from the bottom up, and compare with similar programmes from the top down. State assumptions and ranges. This estimates capital needed; it does not reconstruct undisclosed fundraising.
- **What the sources actually tell us:** No sourced numerical estimate of external-system development capital has been completed.
- **What this lets us write:** An implanted-programme financing figure is not a valid shortcut without explaining major differences in product and trial demands.
- **What we cannot yet answer:** We need external stimulator and software costs, rehabilitation-trial resources, verification and production inputs and suitable comparison programmes.
- **Next investigation:** Source staff time, prototype, testing, trial and production inputs, then cross-check the range against genuinely comparable development programmes. Start with [Capital estimate inputs](../docs/imperial-gap-searches.md#est); the linked access log states what was actually tried and any access limits.

## Regulatory

### Exactly which product and use are being regulated? (R1)

- **What the report needs to explain:** Define the hardware, software and accessories, the intended patients, user and care setting. Identify the original version being studied and separate later changes. This prevents evidence about a component or research system being applied to a different complete product.
- **What the sources actually tell us:** The system combines external stimulation and electrodes with functional practice. Original professional use and a later home-use extension have separate records. Source: [FDA: ARC-EX original De Novo classification and evidence review](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cdrh_docs/reviews/DEN240014.pdf); [FDA: ARC-EX 510(k) extending the indication to home use](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cdrh_docs/pdf25/K251821.pdf). Relevant passage: Indications/configuration.
- **What this lets us write:** This prevents the clinic, home and implanted systems from being treated as one product scope.
- **What we cannot yet answer:** We need the complete components, intended users and applicable label for each market and configuration actually discussed.
- **Next investigation:** Retrieve the selected version's instructions and decision documents, then list the exact configuration, users, setting and intended use. Start with [Official records first](../docs/imperial-gap-searches.md#open); the linked access log states what was actually tried and any access limits.

### Why did it follow this regulatory route? (R2)

- **What the report needs to explain:** For each market discussed, explain the device classification and the applicable rule or product code, then connect the route to the device's function and risks. Evidence of a US route does not establish UK or EU authorisation.
- **What the sources actually tell us:** The original US route was De Novo, with Class II, 21 CFR 890.5851 and product code SDO; home use later followed a 510(k). Source: [FDA: ARC-EX original De Novo classification and evidence review](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cdrh_docs/reviews/DEN240014.pdf); [FDA: ARC-EX 510(k) extending the indication to home use](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cdrh_docs/pdf25/K251821.pdf). Relevant passage: Classification and pathway.
- **What this lets us write:** We can explain the sequence of creating a device category and then a later decision. This does not establish the original EU or UK status.
- **What we cannot yet answer:** We need the primary EU certificate and verified UK record rather than relying only on a company announcement.
- **Next investigation:** Use the exact market's regulator or conformity records to establish the classification and route, explaining their connection to the device's function. Start with [Official records first](../docs/imperial-gap-searches.md#open); the linked access log states what was actually tried and any access limits.

### What actual decision allowed market entry? (R3)

- **What the report needs to explain:** Give the decision identifier, date, type and authorised scope from the original record. Distinguish clearance, approval, registration and a manufacturer's market announcement. Check later changes separately if the report makes claims about the present product.
- **What the sources actually tell us:** DEN240014 is dated 19 December 2024; K251821 is dated 14 November 2025. Source: [FDA: ARC-EX original De Novo classification and evidence review](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cdrh_docs/reviews/DEN240014.pdf); [FDA: ARC-EX 510(k) extending the indication to home use](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cdrh_docs/pdf25/K251821.pdf). Relevant passage: Decision records.
- **What this lets us write:** These establish two distinct US milestones. They should not be collapsed into one date or treated as decisions by other jurisdictions.
- **What we cannot yet answer:** We need independent EU and UK documentation if those market-entry claims are included.
- **Next investigation:** Follow the original decision to any relevant supplements or later clearances, keeping each version and indication separate. Start with [Official records first](../docs/imperial-gap-searches.md#open); the linked access log states what was actually tried and any access limits.

### What tests and standards supported that decision? (R4)

- **What the report needs to explain:** Connect the device's specific risks to the tests or standards reported in its regulatory record. Identify the edition where available and what the test showed. A general standard explains an expectation; it does not prove the manufacturer performed or passed that test.
- **What the sources actually tell us:** The review covers electrical, electromagnetic, software and skin-contact risks; some biological support uses prior electrode and material evidence. Source: [FDA: ARC-EX original De Novo classification and evidence review](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cdrh_docs/reviews/DEN240014.pdf). Relevant passage: Testing; IEC 60601-1-2/-2-10.
- **What this lets us write:** This explains an actual testing strategy rather than assuming every test was newly run. The summary is not a complete present whole-system conformity file.
- **What we cannot yet answer:** We need exact editions, reported results and the evidence applicable to the selected clinic or home configuration.
- **Next investigation:** Read the device's test summary and the exact historical standard where accessible; record the reported test result and which supporting details remain private. Start with [Standards](../docs/imperial-gap-searches.md#std); the linked access log states what was actually tried and any access limits.

### Who made the device and took responsibility for quality? (R5)

- **What the report needs to explain:** Identify the legal manufacturer, important contract manufacturers or suppliers, production sites and who controlled assembly, testing and release. Distinguish a documented relationship from a supplier that merely has relevant capabilities. State the date of the manufacturing account.
- **What the sources actually tell us:** The revision 5 EU manual identifies a Samsung tablet and Axelgaard PALS electrodes. Source: [ONWARD: ARC-EX professional EU manual, Rev 5](https://www.onwd.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/007351_IFU_Professional_EU_ARC-EX-EN-EXIFU01PROEUEN_v5.pdf); [ONWARD: prospectus dated 21 March 2024](https://ir.onwd.com/static-files/48d3e9a2-73bf-40be-a385-d37e7f1cc9bd). Relevant passage: Sections 5.5–5.6; supplier development.
- **What this lets us write:** These are documented component relationships. They do not identify every party making the stimulator or controlling final assembly, quality and release.
- **What we cannot yet answer:** We need the stimulator's actual contract-manufacturing relationship, production site and division of quality and release duties.
- **Next investigation:** Trace the actual supplier or manufacturing agreement and responsibility split, then verify which sites and arrangements apply to the selected period. Start with [Company and financing history](../docs/imperial-gap-searches.md#fin); the linked access log states what was actually tried and any access limits.

### What obligations and problems continued after launch? (R6)

- **What the report needs to explain:** Explain the relevant quality and surveillance duties and investigate product-specific recalls or reported incidents. State the database, model, terms and date searched. A general quality rule does not establish company compliance, and a missing search result does not establish that no problem occurred.
- **What the sources actually tell us:** The annual report describes company status, while QMSR gives general US quality requirements. Source: [ONWARD: Annual Report 2025](https://ir.onwd.com/static-files/7cf553fe-7094-4860-8979-678416de476f); [FDA. Medical Devices; Quality System Regulation Amendments. 89 FR 7496-7525, document 2024-01709](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2024-02-02/html/2024-01709.htm). Relevant passage: Status disclosure; rule scope.
- **What this lets us write:** Neither is an independent product-surveillance audit or a complete history of problems affecting clinic or home use.
- **What we cannot yet answer:** We need dated recall and incident searches and the relevant continuing duties for both settings.
- **Next investigation:** Run and document the exact-model recall and incident searches, and distinguish reported events, proven causes and general quality obligations. Start with [Official records first](../docs/imperial-gap-searches.md#open); the linked access log states what was actually tried and any access limits.

## Reimbursement

### Who buys what, and at what price? (P1)

- **What the report needs to explain:** Describe the actual sale, lease, subscription or consumable arrangement, including the configuration, date and price. Separate the manufacturer's selling price from a hospital charge, a service payment or a modelled treatment cost.
- **What the sources actually tell us:** Clinic equipment, home equipment and rehabilitation sessions are distinct possible purchases; no verified exact price was obtained. Source: [ONWARD: Annual Report 2025](https://ir.onwd.com/static-files/7cf553fe-7094-4860-8979-678416de476f). Relevant passage: Commercial/payment strategy.
- **What this lets us write:** A general rehabilitation-session fee cannot establish the device's acquisition cost or a complete home-use contract.
- **What we cannot yet answer:** We need dated equipment and session terms, consumables, support and replacement arrangements for the selected commercial model.
- **Next investigation:** Find a dated quotation, tender or contract for the selected configuration and clarify what equipment, service and consumables the amount includes. Start with [Actual service and purchasing evidence](../docs/imperial-gap-searches.md#service); the linked access log states what was actually tried and any access limits.

### Who pays for the patient's care? (P2)

- **What the report needs to explain:** Identify whether the relevant care is funded by a commissioner, insurer, hospital, patient, research grant or charity. Connect that source to a real setting. The perspective chosen by an economic model does not prove that the named payer actually funds the device.
- **What the sources actually tell us:** Spinal Research supports UK device loans and Neurokinex provides rehabilitation sessions. Source: [Spinal Research: ARC-EX Real World Programme launch](https://spinal-research.org/news/spinal-research-launches-arc-ex-real-world-programme/); [Neurokinex: ARC-EX spinal-cord stimulation service](https://neurokinex.org/spinal-cord-stimulation/). Relevant passage: Programme and service descriptions.
- **What this lets us write:** This establishes a real access mechanism. It does not establish who sustainably funds every session, replacement or future home-use episode.
- **What we cannot yet answer:** We need the recurring patient, insurer or commissioner terms and the obligations attached to the loan and session arrangements.
- **Next investigation:** Find the actual commissioning, insurance or service-funding arrangement and identify the patient episode and budget it covers. Start with [Actual service and purchasing evidence](../docs/imperial-gap-searches.md#service); the linked access log states what was actually tried and any access limits.

### Under what rules and amounts is care paid for? (P3)

- **What the report needs to explain:** For a named payer, place, setting and year, separate billing codes, clinical coverage conditions and payment amounts. A code permits classification; it does not on its own promise coverage or a particular payment. Temporary support needs its own dates and conditions.
- **What the sources actually tell us:** The annual report discusses use of existing rehabilitation billing and unfinished home-category or coding work. Source: [ONWARD: Annual Report 2025](https://ir.onwd.com/static-files/7cf553fe-7094-4860-8979-678416de476f). Relevant passage: Reimbursement strategy.
- **What this lets us write:** This is the company's payment strategy, not an independent payer decision promising coverage or a particular amount.
- **What we cannot yet answer:** We need the exact payer policy, codes, eligible patients and payment amounts for each setting discussed.
- **Next investigation:** Retrieve the applicable payer policy, code rules and fee or contract amount, checking dates, setting and patient conditions separately. Start with [Official records first](../docs/imperial-gap-searches.md#open); the linked access log states what was actually tried and any access limits.

### What extra costs and savings can be supported? (P4)

- **What the report needs to explain:** Compare the device pathway with the relevant alternative. Include equipment, implementation, staff, maintenance and downstream events, and state the perspective and time horizon. Explain which amounts are observed and which are model inputs; do not turn a hypothetical saving into a realised one.
- **What the sources actually tell us:** No complete device-specific economic model or measured care saving was verified.
- **What this lets us write:** Additional equipment and training have costs, while possible offsets remain unmeasured. Neither can be assigned a supported net value from function scores alone.
- **What we cannot yet answer:** We need incremental staffing and sessions, maintenance, changes in care hours, the perspective and horizon and the relevant prices.
- **Next investigation:** Retrieve the full economic methods and current local inputs, keeping purchase price, care cost and payment as separate quantities. Start with [Costs and health-service evidence](../docs/imperial-gap-searches.md#cost); the linked access log states what was actually tried and any access limits.

### What clinical evidence supports the economic assumptions? (P5)

- **What the report needs to explain:** Trace each proposed saving or health gain to evidence about an outcome or event frequency. Explain how a change in a measured endpoint leads to fewer admissions, less care or better quality of life. Faster setup or better signals do not automatically establish those later effects.
- **What the sources actually tell us:** The sequential functional findings do not measure a causal reduction in assistance hours or other costly events. Source: [Moritz et al.: Up-LIFT pivotal trial, Nature Medicine](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41591-024-02940-9); [Suggitt et al.: community rehabilitation Pathfinder2 pilot](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39998450/). Relevant passage: Design and functional endpoints.
- **What this lets us write:** We can explain a possible route from better hand function to independence, but cannot put an observed economic saving at the end of that route.
- **What we cannot yet answer:** We need durable changes in assistance and resource use and a controlled estimate of the device's incremental contribution.
- **Next investigation:** Find measured patient outcomes and resource events for the device, then check whether the economic calculation extrapolates beyond those measurements. Start with [Clinical literature](../docs/imperial-gap-searches.md#lit); the linked access log states what was actually tried and any access limits.

### Who has a practical reason to adopt it? (P6)

- **What the report needs to explain:** Identify who controls the budget, who pays the extra costs and who benefits, over what time period. Include staffing, capacity and throughput. A treatment can look worthwhile to the whole health system while leaving the hospital that implements it with an immediate cost.
- **What the sources actually tell us:** A charity equipment loan may reduce an upfront barrier while leaving session staffing and future replacement to be funded. Source: [Spinal Research: ARC-EX Real World Programme launch](https://spinal-research.org/news/spinal-research-launches-arc-ex-real-world-programme/). Relevant passage: Loan programme; analytical interpretation.
- **What this lets us write:** This identifies a concrete adoption tension. It is not evidence of the provider's margin, capacity or long-term affordability.
- **What we cannot yet answer:** We need the actual budget holder, recurring costs, capacity, patient charges and replacement funding.
- **Next investigation:** Investigate the actual service budget, staff time, throughput and allocation of benefits with procurement records or appropriately scoped service enquiries. Start with [Actual service and purchasing evidence](../docs/imperial-gap-searches.md#service); the linked access log states what was actually tried and any access limits.

### How do the stakeholders and flows fit together? (P7)

- **What the report needs to explain:** Map the verified product, care, information, money and authority flows, and explain the participants' goals or tensions. Distinguish documented relationships from proposed ones. Use the lecture's required map format once it is available rather than assuming our existing diagram matches it.
- **What the sources actually tell us:** The draft connects ONWARD, therapists and the centre, the charity, patients or helpers and possible payer or home-use routes. Source: [Spinal Research: ARC-EX Real World Programme launch](https://spinal-research.org/news/spinal-research-launches-arc-ex-real-world-programme/); [Neurokinex: ARC-EX spinal-cord stimulation service](https://neurokinex.org/spinal-cord-stimulation/). Relevant passage: Role premises; draft map.
- **What this lets us write:** It helps distinguish documented access relationships from assumed payments. It does not verify every contract, incentive or decision right.
- **What we cannot yet answer:** We need actual contracts and authority flows and the lecture's required map format before finalising the diagram.
- **Next investigation:** Retrieve the actual lecture instructions and verify the case's purchasing and care relationships before treating an analytical diagram as the required final map. Start with [Course map format](../docs/imperial-gap-searches.md#course); the linked access log states what was actually tried and any access limits.
