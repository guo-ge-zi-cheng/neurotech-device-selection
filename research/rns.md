# NeuroPace — RNS System: what we can write, and what is missing

NeuroPace’s RNS System uses implanted electrodes and a cranial stimulator to record brain activity and respond to detected seizure patterns with stimulation. It is studied here for selected adults with drug-resistant focal epilepsy. Trials, a prospectus, the original FDA approval and an economic model provide a connected account, while original funding allocations, current purchase prices and UK service arrangements remain incomplete.

This guide works through the five report sections for a teammate deciding what investigation is still needed. Each question explains the requirement, the actual evidence, the conclusion it supports, the unanswered part and a way to investigate it. Evidence limitations are explained in sentences rather than assigned a grade.

[Device-by-device overview](README.md) · [The shared requirements](../docs/report-evidence-requirements.md) · [What Imperial access actually recovered](../docs/imperial-gap-searches.md).

The evidence comes from the existing 19–21 October research. This revision clarifies that evidence; it adds no new retrievals. The next investigations below are proposals unless the access log explicitly records otherwise.

## Unmet medical need

### Who could actually receive this device? (N1)

- **What the report needs to explain:** Explain the condition and the specific patients or procedures the device is intended for. Then give the number who meet those criteria in a named country and year. Explain why a count of everyone with the disease would include people who cannot receive this device.
- **What the sources actually tell us:** The original FDA population is adults with drug-resistant focal seizures arising from no more than two foci. A separate estimate counts 2.865 million US adults with active epilepsy in 2021. Source: [FDA • RNS P100026](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cdrh_docs/pdf10/P100026B.pdf); [Kobau et al.: active epilepsy in US adults, 2021 NHIS](https://stacks.cdc.gov/view/cdc/154714). Relevant passage: Indications; CDC repository abstract.
- **What this lets us write:** The FDA criteria define the relevant subgroup; the population statistic gives wider context. The wider number cannot be presented as the number eligible for RNS.
- **What we cannot yet answer:** We need a country- and year-specific count of people satisfying all the treatment criteria, including the required seizure pattern and treatment history.
- **Next investigation:** Find a population study or service record that applies the device's actual eligibility criteria, and record the country, year and exclusions. Start with [Clinical literature](../docs/imperial-gap-searches.md#lit); the linked access log states what was actually tried and any access limits.

### Where would the device enter existing care? (N2)

- **What the report needs to explain:** Describe what happens to a patient before, during and after the relevant treatment or investigation. Identify the decision the device changes, the usual alternative and the staff or equipment needed at that point.
- **What the sources actually tell us:** The FDA clinical account places implantation after seizure localisation and includes subsequent programming and monitoring. Source: [FDA • RNS P100026](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cdrh_docs/pdf10/P100026B.pdf). Relevant passage: Device/clinical programme.
- **What this lets us write:** We can explain RNS as an ongoing epilepsy-care pathway rather than a one-off purchase or operation. It does not specify the staffing or referral arrangement of a London service.
- **What we cannot yet answer:** We need local referral, selection, implantation and follow-up arrangements, including the staff and capacity needed at each stage.
- **Next investigation:** Find a guideline or actual service description that traces the relevant patient pathway and the decision where this device would enter. Start with [Clinical literature](../docs/imperial-gap-searches.md#lit); the linked access log states what was actually tried and any access limits.

### What specifically goes wrong with existing care? (N3)

- **What the report needs to explain:** Show a measured problem with the care available before the device: for example, continuing symptoms, missed diagnoses, waiting time or patients unable to access treatment. Explain how often it happens. A later trial of the new device does not by itself measure the original care shortfall.
- **What the sources actually tell us:** The 191-person pivotal trial reports seizure reductions of 37.9% with treatment and 17.3% with the control during the blinded period. Source: [Morrell et al.: responsive cortical stimulation trial](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21917777/). Relevant passage: Abstract; blinded evaluation.
- **What this lets us write:** This is evidence about a later treatment effect. It does not measure how many patients were failed by existing care when RNS development began.
- **What we cannot yet answer:** We need historical failure or access rates for the relevant focal-epilepsy group and evidence explaining why some patients could not use existing surgical options.
- **Next investigation:** Find a study of the existing pathway that measures the failure or access problem, preferably from the period when development began. Start with [Clinical literature](../docs/imperial-gap-searches.md#lit); the linked access log states what was actually tried and any access limits.

### What are the consequences of that problem? (N4)

- **What the report needs to explain:** Explain how the care shortfall affects patients and services. Connect it to outcomes, staff time, hospital use or costs, and identify which groups carry that burden. A large disease-cost figure needs a reasoned connection to the patients and problem this device addresses.
- **What the sources actually tell us:** The full economic paper includes initial implantation and continuing care, with $41,173 as a modelled implantation input. Source: [Youngerman et al. Cost-effectiveness analysis of responsive neurostimulation for drug-resistant focal onset epilepsy. Epilepsia 62:2804-2813. DOI 10.1111/epi.17049](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/epi.17049). Relevant passage: Section 2.6.1; new Imperial full-text review.
- **What this lets us write:** It shows which resource categories matter to the treatment pathway. Historical US fee inputs are not observed UK patient burden or a before-and-after cost study.
- **What we cannot yet answer:** We need measured care use, patient consequences and costs for the eligible UK subgroup, with the source year and service setting stated.
- **Next investigation:** Find measured patient consequences and resource use for the relevant subgroup, then identify the price year and setting of any cost estimate. Start with [Costs and health-service evidence](../docs/imperial-gap-searches.md#cost); the linked access log states what was actually tried and any access limits.

### What benefit has this particular device demonstrated? (N5)

- **What the report needs to explain:** Explain how the device could change the identified problem and what has actually been measured. Distinguish a technical result, a change in a clinical decision and an improvement patients experience. State the study population, comparison and follow-up so the reader can see how far the finding reaches.
- **What the sources actually tell us:** The nine-year follow-up reports a 75% median seizure reduction. The earlier blinded trial supplies a separate controlled comparison. Source: [Nair et al.: nine-year prospective RNS follow-up](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32690786/); [Morrell et al.: responsive cortical stimulation trial](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21917777/). Relevant passage: Abstracts and design.
- **What this lets us write:** We can describe both a controlled short-term effect and longer observed follow-up. The open-label follow-up cannot by itself attribute every later change to stimulation or establish superiority to all alternatives.
- **What we cannot yet answer:** We need relevant comparative quality-of-life and harm evidence and outcomes that match the local patient group and treatment decision.
- **Next investigation:** Read the original device study for its comparator, measured endpoints, follow-up, adverse events and limitations; look for the missing patient outcome rather than another mechanism description. Start with [Clinical literature](../docs/imperial-gap-searches.md#lit); the linked access log states what was actually tried and any access limits.

### What other options could meet the same need? (N6)

- **What the report needs to explain:** Compare options available to the same patients at the same point in care. Explain their different benefits, risks and practical demands. A product used as a regulatory comparison is not automatically the most appropriate clinical alternative.
- **What the sources actually tell us:** The economic analysis compares RNS plus medication with medication alone. Source: [Youngerman et al. Cost-effectiveness analysis of responsive neurostimulation for drug-resistant focal onset epilepsy. Epilepsia 62:2804-2813. DOI 10.1111/epi.17049](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/epi.17049). Relevant passage: Section 2.1.
- **What this lets us write:** This gives the report one clearly defined comparator. It is not a head-to-head comparison with resection, vagus-nerve stimulation or deep-brain stimulation in equally eligible patients.
- **What we cannot yet answer:** We need to compare the alternatives available to the same patients and explain differences in eligibility, benefit, risk and follow-up requirements.
- **Next investigation:** Find alternatives for the same patient group and compare their eligibility, outcomes, risks and service requirements. Start with [Clinical literature](../docs/imperial-gap-searches.md#lit); the linked access log states what was actually tried and any access limits.

## Intellectual property

### Who made the original invention, and where? (I1)

- **What the report needs to explain:** Identify the inventors of the technology that became the selected device, their employers when the invention arose and the earliest relevant publication or patent priority. A current university biography is not evidence of where an inventor worked at that earlier date.
- **What the sources actually tell us:** US6016449A names Robert and David Fischell and Adrian Upton and records a 1997 filing. Source: [US6016449A: implantable seizure warning/treatment system](https://patents.google.com/patent/US6016449A/en); [Johns Hopkins Engineering: Robert Fischell and NeuroPace history](https://engineering.jhu.edu/magazine-archive/2008/09/fischell-awarded-honorary-degree/). Relevant passage: Bibliographic metadata; founder history.
- **What this lets us write:** This is a specific starting point for the invention history. Founder biographies or later affiliations do not establish each person's employer when that invention arose.
- **What we cannot yet answer:** We need contemporaneous employment and original assignment evidence to connect the named inventors, originating organisations and earliest relevant invention.
- **Next investigation:** Trace the original inventors and predecessor organisations using patent priority records and contemporaneous employment or university records. Start with [Patent and rights investigation](../docs/imperial-gap-searches.md#pat); the linked access log states what was actually tried and any access limits.

### How did the company obtain the right to use it? (I2)

- **What the report needs to explain:** Explain who originally owned the invention and how rights reached the developer through assignment, acquisition or licence. Identify the records supporting each step. A shared founder, research collaboration or patent applicant does not establish every transfer or licence term.
- **What the sources actually tell us:** The patent identifies the original inventors, while the 2021 prospectus describes invention-assignment and confidentiality agreements. The reviewed record does not establish the complete original transfer history. Source: [US6016449A: implantable seizure warning/treatment system](https://patents.google.com/patent/US6016449A/en); [NeuroPace: 2021 IPO prospectus amendment](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1528287/000162828021006852/neuropaces-1a1.htm). Relevant passage: Assignment metadata; IP discussion.
- **What this lets us write:** We can distinguish named inventors from the company's disclosed protection arrangements. Neither establishes every original transfer or a university licence; a founder's institutional connection cannot fill that gap.
- **What we cannot yet answer:** We need the official original assignments, any relevant licence documents and the chain by which the developer obtained the rights used for the product.
- **Next investigation:** Retrieve the actual assignment or licence record and follow the original owner's rights into the developer, leaving unavailable contractual terms explicit. Start with [Patent and rights investigation](../docs/imperial-gap-searches.md#pat); the linked access log states what was actually tried and any access limits.

### Which patent claims correspond to the device's features? (I3)

- **What the report needs to explain:** Explain each relevant independent claim in ordinary language, then compare its required features with documented features of the selected product. An independent claim sets out a combination of features without relying on another claim. Identify any feature that cannot yet be matched.
- **What the sources actually tell us:** Independent claim 1 concerns responsive intracranial stimulation and programming. Claim 46 includes an additional remote sensor or actuator. Source: [US6016449A: implantable seizure warning/treatment system](https://patents.google.com/patent/US6016449A/en). Relevant passage: Claims 1 and 46.
- **What this lets us write:** We can explain the two claimed technical combinations. We cannot assume the commercial system contains every extra feature required by claim 46.
- **What we cannot yet answer:** We need an element-by-element comparison with the selected RNS configuration, especially documentation of the remote feature required by claim 46.
- **Next investigation:** Compare the exact publication's independent claims with technical drawings or instructions for the selected product version, one required feature at a time. Start with [Patent and rights investigation](../docs/imperial-gap-searches.md#pat); the linked access log states what was actually tried and any access limits.

### Where and when does that protection apply? (I4)

- **What the report needs to explain:** Identify the relevant patent family, priority date and countries, and check the dated status of the particular application or granted patent. An application, an expired patent and a live grant answer different questions. Later amendments may change the claims.
- **What the sources actually tell us:** The aggregator's metadata indicates that the foundational patent has reached lifetime expiry. Source: [US6016449A: implantable seizure warning/treatment system](https://patents.google.com/patent/US6016449A/en). Relevant passage: Legal-status metadata.
- **What this lets us write:** This makes it useful for historical origin, rather than sufficient evidence of a current exclusion right. Later patents may matter, but have not been fully mapped here.
- **What we cannot yet answer:** We need official dated status and the relevant later families and jurisdictions before describing the present protection around the selected product.
- **Next investigation:** Check the correct family and national patent registers for dated status, granted text and amendments rather than relying only on an aggregator's summary. Start with [Patent and rights investigation](../docs/imperial-gap-searches.md#pat); the linked access log states what was actually tried and any access limits.

### What else can the company protect or control? (I5)

- **What the report needs to explain:** Look for documented software rights, designs, trademarks, confidential know-how or data rights that matter to the device. Explain what the company controls and why it could matter commercially. Owning a factory does not by itself establish that a particular process is legally protected.
- **What the sources actually tell us:** The 2021 prospectus describes trade secrets, confidentiality provisions and invention-assignment agreements. Source: [NeuroPace: 2021 IPO prospectus amendment](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1528287/000162828021006852/neuropaces-1a1.htm). Relevant passage: Intellectual Property.
- **What this lets us write:** This supports a statement that protection is not limited to the old patent. It does not reveal the confidential technical content or establish which specific assets matter to today's version.
- **What we cannot yet answer:** We need the documented connection between particular software, data or know-how assets and the selected product, within what the company publicly discloses.
- **Next investigation:** Look for an explicit company or contractual description of the relevant software, know-how, design or data rights and who controls them. Start with [Company and financing history](../docs/imperial-gap-searches.md#fin); the linked access log states what was actually tried and any access limits.

### What could limit that control or allow competition? (I6)

- **What the report needs to explain:** Identify relevant third-party rights, licences on which the developer depends and possible competing designs. Explain what the available records let us say about competition. Our search does not establish a legal right to commercialise every feature or prove freedom from infringement.
- **What the sources actually tell us:** The historical patent's expiry and the incomplete later claim map leave current competitive control unresolved. Source: [US6016449A: implantable seizure warning/treatment system](https://patents.google.com/patent/US6016449A/en); [NeuroPace: 2021 IPO prospectus amendment](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1528287/000162828021006852/neuropaces-1a1.htm). Relevant passage: Historical patent; IP risks.
- **What this lets us write:** A portfolio count cannot show whether a competitor can implement a similar system or whether NeuroPace depends on another party's rights.
- **What we cannot yet answer:** We need specific competing claims, technical alternatives and relevant licence dependencies before making a bounded statement about competitive protection.
- **Next investigation:** Investigate specific overlapping claims and licence dependencies; use technical alternatives to frame the unresolved question rather than declaring legal clearance. Start with [Patent and rights investigation](../docs/imperial-gap-searches.md#pat); the linked access log states what was actually tried and any access limits.

## Fundraising

### Was early research supported by grants? (F1)

- **What the report needs to explain:** For each relevant grant, establish who received it, the award identifier, dates, amount and purpose. Explain whether it supported a precursor laboratory, the company or this product. An acknowledgement in a paper can identify a lead without revealing the money attributable to the device.
- **What the sources actually tell us:** No authoritative earliest grant record was accepted in the reviewed research.
- **What this lets us write:** The report cannot assign an amount of precompany research support from the existence of academic or founder connections.
- **What we cannot yet answer:** We need the original award recipient, identifier, period, amount and purpose, with evidence connecting the award to the relevant early work.
- **Next investigation:** Trace the acknowledgement to an authoritative award record and check its recipient, period, amount, purpose and relationship to this device. Start with [Awards and original research support](../docs/imperial-gap-searches.md#grant); the linked access log states what was actually tried and any access limits.

### What money did investors actually provide? (F2)

- **What the report needs to explain:** Reconstruct the relevant early investment: who invested, when it closed, the currency, amount, type of security and any disclosed valuation. Separate money offered from money sold or received, and distinguish a company fundraising total from money allocated to this product.
- **What the sources actually tell us:** The 2011 Form D records a first sale on 16 August and $49,126,004 sold from an offering of $61,914,481. - **What we cannot yet answer:** We need earlier rounds, investor-level terms, the form of proceeds and any disclosed valuation to reconstruct original development finance.

### What other resources helped development? (F3)

State whether the source reports an intention, an allocated budget or actual expenditure. A round announcement alone does not show how the company divided its money.
- **What the sources actually tell us:** The offering notice records securities sold without allocating the proceeds to device-development tasks. ### What was delivered after the funding? (F5)

- **What the report needs to explain:** Place funding events and documented development outputs on the same timeline. Identify evidence of a prototype, study, regulatory decision or first sale. Timing can show that funding preceded an outcome, but additional evidence is needed to claim the funding paid for that outcome.
- **What the sources actually tell us:** The public record places incorporation in 1997, the offering in 2011 and original FDA approval on 14 November 2013. Relevant passage: Company history; sale; decision.
- **What this lets us write:** This provides a dated sequence of company formation, financing and market entry. It does not identify which resources paid for each development output.
- **What we cannot yet answer:** We need evidence linking particular funding or resources to prototypes, clinical work and the work required for approval.
- **Next investigation:** Find a dated output and a source linking the relevant resources to that work; distinguish the documented sequence from a causal funding claim. Start with [Company and financing history](../docs/imperial-gap-searches.md#fin); the linked access log states what was actually tried and any access limits.

### What would early development have cost if the budget is private? (F6)

- **What the report needs to explain:** Build a reasoned estimate using both approaches allowed by the specification. Add up sourced staffing, engineering, testing, trial and manufacturing costs from the bottom up, and compare with similar programmes from the top down. State assumptions and ranges. This estimates capital needed; it does not reconstruct undisclosed fundraising.
- **What the sources actually tell us:** No justified numerical range for required early-development capital has been completed.
- **What this lets us write:** The offering amount is not a substitute for estimating the resources needed to build, verify and clinically evaluate an implantable system.
- **What we cannot yet answer:** We need staffing and engineering inputs, verification and production costs, trial and follow-up costs, and comparable programmes to construct both estimation approaches.
- **Next investigation:** Source staff time, prototype, testing, trial and production inputs, then cross-check the range against genuinely comparable development programmes. Start with [Capital estimate inputs](../docs/imperial-gap-searches.md#est); the linked access log states what was actually tried and any access limits.

## Regulatory

### Exactly which product and use are being regulated? (R1)

- **What the report needs to explain:** Define the hardware, software and accessories, the intended patients, user and care setting. Identify the original version being studied and separate later changes. This prevents evidence about a component or research system being applied to a different complete product.
- **What the sources actually tell us:** The original system combines intracranial leads, a cranial neurostimulator and external programming and data equipment for selected adult patients. Source: [FDA • RNS P100026](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cdrh_docs/pdf10/P100026B.pdf). Relevant passage: Device description and indication.
- **What this lets us write:** This defines a complete regulated system and use population. It does not cover every later model, accessory or indication without additional records.
- **What we cannot yet answer:** We need a complete version history for the configurations and indications the report intends to discuss.
- **Next investigation:** Retrieve the selected version's instructions and decision documents, then list the exact configuration, users, setting and intended use. Start with [Official records first](../docs/imperial-gap-searches.md#open); the linked access log states what was actually tried and any access limits.

### Why did it follow this regulatory route? (R2)

- **What the report needs to explain:** For each market discussed, explain the device classification and the applicable rule or product code, then connect the route to the device's function and risks. Evidence of a US route does not establish UK or EU authorisation.
- **What the sources actually tell us:** The implanted responsive stimulation system followed the US Class III premarket-approval route. Source: [FDA • RNS P100026](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cdrh_docs/pdf10/P100026B.pdf). Relevant passage: Classification and regulatory background.
- **What this lets us write:** We can explain the original route in relation to an implanted therapeutic system. US approval does not establish authorisation or availability in a different market.
- **What we cannot yet answer:** We need the relevant primary UK or EU records if those markets form part of the report's claims.
- **Next investigation:** Use the exact market's regulator or conformity records to establish the classification and route, explaining their connection to the device's function. Start with [Official records first](../docs/imperial-gap-searches.md#open); the linked access log states what was actually tried and any access limits.

### What actual decision allowed market entry? (R3)

- **What the report needs to explain:** Give the decision identifier, date, type and authorised scope from the original record. Distinguish clearance, approval, registration and a manufacturer's market announcement. Check later changes separately if the report makes claims about the present product.
- **What the sources actually tell us:** The primary decision is P100026, approved on 14 November 2013. Source: [FDA • RNS P100026](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cdrh_docs/pdf10/P100026B.pdf). Relevant passage: Approval/SSED dates.
- **What this lets us write:** This gives a traceable original US market-entry event. Later supplements may change the configuration or indication and must be read separately.
- **What we cannot yet answer:** We need the relevant later supplements and current label before claiming that the original decision describes the present system completely.
- **Next investigation:** Follow the original decision to any relevant supplements or later clearances, keeping each version and indication separate. ### What tests and standards supported that decision? (R4)

A general standard explains an expectation; it does not prove the manufacturer performed or passed that test.

- **What the sources actually tell us:** No verified current acquisition quote was obtained. The economic paper's $41,173 is an estimated implantation cost based on fees. Source: [Youngerman et al. DOI 10.1111/epi.17049](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/epi.17049). Relevant passage: Section 2.6.1; new Imperial review.
- **What this lets us write:** The amount can be used only as the paper's cost input. It cannot be called the manufacturer's selling price or the hospital's current purchase cost.
- **What we cannot yet answer:** We need a dated product quotation or supplier contract stating the configuration and included hardware, service and follow-up elements.
- **Next investigation:** Find a dated quotation, tender or contract for the selected configuration and clarify what equipment, service and consumables the amount includes. Start with [Actual service and purchasing evidence](../docs/imperial-gap-searches.md#service); the linked access log states what was actually tried and any access limits.

### Who pays for the patient's care? (P2)

- **What the report needs to explain:** Identify whether the relevant care is funded by a commissioner, insurer, hospital, patient, research grant or charity. Connect that source to a real setting. The perspective chosen by an economic model does not prove that the named payer actually funds the device.
- **What the sources actually tell us:** Aetna's policy states coverage criteria for selected use. The economic study separately takes a public-payer perspective. Source: [Aetna CPB 0394: epilepsy surgery and responsive cortical stimulation](https://www.aetna.com/cpb/medical/data/300_399/0394.html); [Youngerman et al. DOI 10.1111/epi.17049](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/epi.17049). Relevant passage: Responsive Cortical Stimulation; section 2.2.
- **What this lets us write:** The policy shows one insurer's stated conditions; the model identifies whose costs the authors analysed. Neither establishes an actual NHS commissioner or a particular patient's payment arrangement.
- **What we cannot yet answer:** We need the applicable plan or commissioning arrangement for the care setting being discussed.
- **Next investigation:** Find the actual commissioning, insurance or service-funding arrangement and identify the patient episode and budget it covers. Start with [Official records first](../docs/imperial-gap-searches.md#open); the linked access log states what was actually tried and any access limits.

### Under what rules and amounts is care paid for? (P3)

- **What the report needs to explain:** For a named payer, place, setting and year, separate billing codes, clinical coverage conditions and payment amounts. A code permits classification; it does not on its own promise coverage or a particular payment. Temporary support needs its own dates and conditions.
- **What the sources actually tell us:** Aetna Clinical Policy Bulletin 0394 includes eligibility conditions for responsive cortical stimulation. Source: [Aetna CPB 0394: epilepsy surgery and responsive cortical stimulation](https://www.aetna.com/cpb/medical/data/300_399/0394.html). Relevant passage: Responsive Cortical Stimulation.
- **What this lets us write:** This lets us explain clinical coverage conditions for that insurer. It does not provide all billing codes, actual contract fees or the rules of other payers.
- **What we cannot yet answer:** We need the relevant coding, current fee schedule and actual payment terms for the selected payer, service and year.
- **Next investigation:** Retrieve the applicable payer policy, code rules and fee or contract amount, checking dates, setting and patient conditions separately. Start with [Official records first](../docs/imperial-gap-searches.md#open); the linked access log states what was actually tried and any access limits.

### What extra costs and savings can be supported? (P4)

- **What the report needs to explain:** Compare the device pathway with the relevant alternative. Include equipment, implementation, staff, maintenance and downstream events, and state the perspective and time horizon. Explain which amounts are observed and which are model inputs; do not turn a hypothetical saving into a realised one.
- **What the sources actually tell us:** The full paper estimates nine-year costs of $246,284 for RNS and $212,279 for medication, reporting an increment of $34,006. Its professional-fee inputs are from 2019 and facility inputs from 2017. Source: [Youngerman et al. Cost-effectiveness analysis of responsive neurostimulation for drug-resistant focal onset epilepsy. Epilepsia 62:2804-2813. DOI 10.1111/epi.17049](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/epi.17049). Relevant passage: Section 2.6; Table 1; new Imperial review.
- **What this lets us write:** We can explain a historical US pathway model and its assumptions. The rounded displayed totals differ by one dollar from the reported increment, so we retain the authors' result and disclose that boundary rather than inventing a corrected model.
- **What we cannot yet answer:** We need current local costs and resource frequencies before using the calculation for a UK service, and we cannot treat the model as an observed saving.
- **Next investigation:** Retrieve the full economic methods and current local inputs, keeping purchase price, care cost and payment as separate quantities. Start with [Costs and health-service evidence](../docs/imperial-gap-searches.md#cost); the linked access log states what was actually tried and any access limits.

### What clinical evidence supports the economic assumptions? (P5)

- **What the report needs to explain:** Trace each proposed saving or health gain to evidence about an outcome or event frequency. Explain how a change in a measured endpoint leads to fewer admissions, less care or better quality of life. Faster setup or better signals do not automatically establish those later effects.
- **What the sources actually tell us:** The paper derives utilities indirectly from seizure frequency and estimates event costs using medication-treated cohorts. Source: [Youngerman et al. Cost-effectiveness analysis of responsive neurostimulation for drug-resistant focal onset epilepsy. Epilepsia 62:2804-2813. DOI 10.1111/epi.17049](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/epi.17049). Relevant passage: Section 4.1; new Imperial review.
- **What this lets us write:** This shows the evidential bridge behind the economic result: measured seizure changes are translated into assumed quality-of-life and resource effects. The translation is not a direct RNS measurement of those effects.
- **What we cannot yet answer:** We need device-specific utility and longitudinal resource-use measurements to test those assumptions.
- **Next investigation:** Find measured patient outcomes and resource events for the device, then check whether the economic calculation extrapolates beyond those measurements. Start with [Clinical literature](../docs/imperial-gap-searches.md#lit); the linked access log states what was actually tried and any access limits.

### Who has a practical reason to adopt it? (P6)

- **What the report needs to explain:** Identify who controls the budget, who pays the extra costs and who benefits, over what time period. Include staffing, capacity and throughput. A treatment can look worthwhile to the whole health system while leaving the hospital that implements it with an immediate cost.
- **What the sources actually tell us:** The model values benefits over nine years, whereas implantation and continuing programming require provider resources. Source: [Youngerman et al. Cost-effectiveness analysis of responsive neurostimulation for drug-resistant focal onset epilepsy. Epilepsia 62:2804-2813. DOI 10.1111/epi.17049](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/epi.17049). Relevant passage: Methods/discussion.
- **What this lets us write:** This identifies a timing and budget question. A favourable long-term payer result does not tell us whether the implanting hospital has the capacity or financial incentive to deliver treatment.
- **What we cannot yet answer:** We need the actual budget holder, hospital revenues and costs, staff capacity and how benefits and spending fall across organisations.
- **Next investigation:** Investigate the actual service budget, staff time, throughput and allocation of benefits with procurement records or appropriately scoped service enquiries. Start with [Actual service and purchasing evidence](../docs/imperial-gap-searches.md#service); the linked access log states what was actually tried and any access limits.

### How do the stakeholders and flows fit together? (P7)

- **What the report needs to explain:** Map the verified product, care, information, money and authority flows, and explain the participants' goals or tensions. Distinguish documented relationships from proposed ones. Use the lecture's required map format once it is available rather than assuming our existing diagram matches it.
- **What the sources actually tell us:** The draft map connects the manufacturer, epilepsy centre, patient, FDA and an insurer subject to its coverage conditions. Source: [FDA • RNS P100026](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cdrh_docs/pdf10/P100026B.pdf); [Aetna CPB 0394: epilepsy surgery and responsive cortical stimulation](https://www.aetna.com/cpb/medical/data/300_399/0394.html). Relevant passage: Role premises; draft stakeholder map.
- **What this lets us write:** It can organise the roles supported by these sources. It does not verify purchasing contracts, exact money flows or the required lecture format.
- **What we cannot yet answer:** We need actual purchasing and authority relationships and the lecture's map instructions before finalising the diagram.
- **Next investigation:** Retrieve the actual lecture instructions and verify the case's purchasing and care relationships before treating an analytical diagram as the required final map.
